# ui/incident/map-cards/web

What the web adds to the cards of a piece: the tab of the browser of a device (`browser-card.tsx`) and the strip of the url of a terminal (`url-strip.tsx`).

- The browser is a card of the device it runs on, one more tab of the card of its piece (`pieceCard` lists it right after the terminal; it is not a piece of the map). Like every card it is a frame (`browserFrame`) and a card of its own (`BrowserCard`), and its draft, the address picked, is kept above the tabs by `PieceCard` (`useBrowserDraft`), so switching tabs loses neither the address, nor the user picked, nor what the browser showed for it (that is read from the session, `browserView`).
- It shows what `@/game` computed (`BrowserCard`, `BrowserView`) and reports the player's pick: a `ChipTray` «Dirección» with the whole url of each address, the primary button «Abrir» (disabled until an address is picked; it sends the message `browser` from the origin of the card), when the incident offers a sign-in a `ChipTray` «Usuario» with a chip for each user and the small `Button` «Iniciar sesión» under it (disabled until an address and a user are picked; it sends the message of that user from the origin, a send like «Abrir», and the player types nothing; V29 = A) and, once that address was opened or signed in to since the world last changed, «Lo que ve el navegador»: the address in `code` and either the lines of the page as plain text of the card or the error in plain words with a ✕ mark that CSS draws (`.browser__error`). The error is never a `Message`: it is what the network said, not a notice of the game. The group that holds it sits in a polite live region that is always mounted, so the screen reader hears the page or the error appear. The card holds no text of its own: the words come from `BROWSER_TEXT` and the lines of the page from the response. Under that, once the term `cookie` is known, «Lo que recuerda»: a group with the line «Cada fila es una cookie.» (the term tagged) and the `Table` of `@/design` with a row for each cookie the browser holds (the site, the name and the value, in `code`) or «Todavía no guarda nada.»; it adds no primitive. `sign-in.test.tsx` holds the tests of the sign-in and of the table. At 360 px the card is 300 px wide and its body 272: what the tab adds (the tray «Usuario», the button, the group «Lo que ve el navegador» and the table) fits without a sideways scroll, but the body of the card of a laptop with four tabs (Terminal, Navegador, Ajustes de red and Tabla de rutas, as `tu-laptop` has from 03-03) scrolls sideways by 86 px because of the tab bar alone, as it already did in 03-03; that is `Tabs` of `@/design`, not this card.
- **The strip of the url (V28 = B, 03-01).** When the address chips of a send give their urls by parts, the terminal draws `UrlStripTray` in place of its tray «Destino»: a group «Dirección» with the same `ChipTray` for each part of the url, in the order of the url (Esquema, Nombre, Puerto, Path, Query string) and the separators `://` (after the scheme) and `:` (after the name) between them. The strip wraps: the card of the map is 300 px wide at every window, and the five parts do not fit in one line (about 480 px of chips alone), so at 300 px they lay out in three rows: scheme and name, port and path, query string; the separator is glued after the part that precedes it, so a row never starts with a lone separator. The strip adds no primitive: each part is a `ChipTray` and `src/styles.css` (`.strip`) only lets a tray be as wide as its chips. A part that offers one value arrives picked. The picks of the parts are the terminal's draft (`TerminalDraft.parts`); the address chip they stand for is read by `stripAddress` of `@/game`. `url-strip.browser.test.tsx` holds the two tests of the strip (`the url chips lay out as the prototype V28 chose`, `at 360 px the strip of the url does not scroll sideways and every chip is within reach`): in a docked card of about 200 px the body shows about 50 px, so the wheel over the body (in steps of the height of a chip) is what brings each part into reach.
- The «Sitios» card of a web server needs no file here: it is the `TableCard` of `table-card.tsx` (a table of kind `sites`), with its two trays and its refusal under the table. Its tests are here with the other tests of the web (`web-cards.test.tsx`: the browser tab, the sites card and the output of a `curl`, each in a `describe`, over one `Hosted` piece card of `webSendIncident`): a site needs both trays to be placed, a refused site is an error `Message` under the table in a `role="status"` region that was already there, and the focus goes back to the title after adding, as in the records of chapter 2. A piece with a single card draws no tab bar.
- The repeated blocks of the output of a `curl` are tested there too, though the card is the terminal's (`terminal/`); `send-rules.browser.test.tsx` holds the test of the rules card of a send incident, which needs the real map.
- Not here: the chip card and the other cards (`src/ui/incident/map-cards/`), the terminal (`src/ui/incident/map-cards/terminal/`), what each card shows (`src/game/view/cards/web/`), the Spanish text (`src/copy/web/`).
