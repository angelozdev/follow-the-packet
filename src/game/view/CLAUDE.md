# game/view

What the map and the panels show, computed from the session: piece ids, piece labels and locks, what the map draws (`drawn.ts`: `isDrawn`, `isShown`, `drawsRouter`), where a router stands for the player (`router-place.ts`), the inspection box (`inspection.ts`, with the fields of a packet in `packet-fields.ts`), and, in `cards/`, a firewall's rules named by chips (`firewall-card.ts`), the terminal of an origin (`cards/terminal/terminal-card.ts`), what a traceroute printed (`cards/terminal/traceroute-lines.ts`, and `revealed-routers.ts`: which routers its lines name), the card of a device in an identify incident (`identify-card.ts`) and the one that classifies other devices (`cards/identify/classify-card.ts`), the «Ajustes de red» and «Servicios» cards of a device (`settings-card.ts`), the tables of a piece (`table-card.ts`), the card of the case a diagnose is on (`case-card.ts`) and the cards a piece opens from its one button, each as a tab (`piece-card.ts`).

- Every piece is an entity of the catalog in the variant of its model (`EntityRef`): a device and a firewall by their `kind`, the edge by the network's `edge`, an actor by its `kind`. Its term comes from `entityTerm` (`ENTITY_TERMS` in `src/copy/terms.ts`): `device` for a laptop or a printer, `firewall` for the firewall of a laptop, `router`, `internet`, and the AWS ones (`ec2-instance`, `security-group`, `internet-gateway`); none ever for an actor. A firewall label, the edge label and the internet label carry their term's name (`Security Group servidor2`, `firewall laptop-lucia`, `router`, `internet`); a device's label is its name (`servidor2`, `tu-laptop`) and an actor's label is its own name. A server of names (an actor of kind `server` with a `dns` role) is drawn only once the term of its role is known, `resolver` for a resolver and `authoritative-server` for an authoritative server, besides the list of its incident (`drawsActor`, `drawn.ts`, which `pieceViews` and `inspect` share, so a server that is not drawn is not inspected); a server with no DNS and a client of the internet (an actor piece) are drawn whenever the incident shows them. An actor of kind `server` (a server of the internet) is not an actor piece: `pieceViews` draws it as a device piece of the variant `internet-server` (term `device`) under the id of its actor, `PIECE_ID.actor`, and `inspect` shows it as a device: what a device is and its dirección IP (never «IP privada», its address is not one), with no «Quién es». In a world with `routers`, every router is a piece of the edge in the variant `router` (`PIECE_ID.router`, labelled by its own name) in place of the lone edge; its box says only what a router is, because what the office router does does not hold for the routers of the provider.
- The inspection of a piece whose variant has a term, or of a packet, starts with `INSPECTION_TEXT.whatIs`, the definition of that term, once the term is known (a packet's term is its ICMP message, and `packet` while that one is not known); a piece without a term has no definition. The icon of a packet's box is the shape of its protocol (`packetShape`: the dot of ICMP, the square of TCP, the hexagon of UDP, with the intruder's border for an intruder), the same the map draws. A device's IP is its dirección IP until the term IP privada is known. A device of an office also has an «En la red» field once `network` is known (it was «Conexión» until chapter 2, whose term `connection` is a TCP one): «Conectado a la red.» or «No está conectado: no forma parte de la red.». A device with no cable keeps its IP, because an IP can be set without a cable: what says it is outside the network is that line (and where the map draws it). A device of a VPC has no such field. Every device, and every server of the internet, also has a «Servicios» field once `service` is known (decided 20 of chapter 2): one line per program that listens, «TCP 3000» or «puerto 3000» while `tcp` is unknown (a line is not repeated while two services only differ by a protocol the player does not know yet), followed by «escucha en todas las IPs» or «escucha solo en 127.0.0.1» only once `listen-address` is known, because that is the hidden cause of a refusal; a device with no program says «Ninguno.». The box never writes the name of a program from its port («SSH» for 22): a service has no name and the default port is a custom. The «IP pública» field (term `public-ip`, known from 01-08 on, so also in chapter 4) is the other way round: a device of a VPC shows its public IP or «No tiene», and an actor of the internet shows its IP, while a device of an office shows no such field at all, because how a packet leaves the office is a black box in chapter 1 and its laptops have no public IP of their own to show.
- A piece is drawn when its term is known (`isDrawn`): the firewall, the edge (or the routers) and the internet wait for `firewall` or `security-group`, `router` or `internet-gateway`, and `internet`, whether or not someone is outside the network. Devices and actors are the base of every map and are always drawn. An office of chapter 1 shows the router and the internet from the incident that introduces them. The inspection of a piece that is not drawn is null.
- What the player has not learned is not shown. A packet that a firewall the player does not know stopped is inspected with `describeReason(reason, world, known)` and `describeResult(trace, known)`: its stop says only that nothing came back, and the device does not list the firewall. Its path ends at the door of the device, with the lost stamp (`hiddenStop`, `src/game/flow-runs.ts`).
- An incident draws only what it lists (`shown`, in the contract of `src/levels/`): a device, a client actor, a server or a router is a piece when its id is in the list, or when the incident has none, and, for a router, once `router` is known. A firewall is drawn with its device, so a firewall of a device that is not drawn is not drawn either, and `inspect` is null for a piece the incident does not show. The lone edge and the internet have no id to list: with a list they are drawn only when it holds something outside the office (an actor, or a router with no interface facing a local network of the office), so 01-06, 01-07 and 01-11 draw no globe and 01-08 to 01-10 do. An incident whose routers wait for a traceroute (`revealsRoutersByTraceroute`) draws a router of its list only once a traceroute the player sent prints its IP (`revealedRouterIds`: the lines as the terminal shows them, so a warning whose term is unknown reveals nobody); `drawsRouter` is where it is decided, so the pieces, the router's box, `routerPlace` and the lines of the terminal agree. In a diagnose, the list is the one of the case the player is on when that case has its own (`shownOf`), so each case draws only what it uses and the map changes with the case; the incident's list serves a case with none. `pieceViews` is the one place that decides it, and the scene lays out only the pieces it is given.
- Every piece has a state, read from the runs: a device is `intruded` while a forbidden flow reaches it, a firewall is `blocking` while its device's firewalls stop a flow, and every other piece is `normal`.
- The task card of a firewall (`FirewallCard`) carries the words of its kind: what it says without rules (`empty`) and the label of its new-rule slot (`slot`). A host firewall says «reglas» and «Regla nueva», never «de entrada»: that is the name of the later AWS term `inbound-rule`, which a Security Group keeps.
- The inspection of a firewall lists its inbound rules and, when its card has an outbound list, labels the two directions («Entrada» and «Salida» for a host firewall, «Reglas de entrada» and «Reglas de salida» for a Security Group) and lists the outbound rules too (the rule that lets everything out reads «deja salir todo el tráfico»); the outbound field shows as soon as the term that opens the outbound rules (`RULES_TERM.outbound`) is known, in any mechanic, and is left out before.
- Every inspection field declares the term it uses; a field whose term is not known yet is left out. A packet shows its message once Echo Request is known; a packet the player sent is inspected like a flow's. The edge and the internet are inspected only while they are on the map.
- The box of a packet in an office also shows, in this order after its message (and, once `dns` is known, after the «DNS» field of a name; «Saludo de tres pasos» or «Consulta y respuesta» sit between the headers and the routers): «Encabezado» (called «Encabezado del Echo Request» when the reply header is shown beside it and `echo-request` is known; term `header`: «IP de origen» and «IP de destino», named by the terms `source-ip` and `destination-ip` once they are known and by «Origen» and «Destino» before, and «TTL al salir» once `ttl` is known, which a traceroute's box leaves out because its last probe has a TTL the player never chose), «Encabezado del Echo Reply» (the same two lines swapped, when the packet was answered and `echo-reply` is known), the routers it crossed (`Trace.hops`), the result, «Tiempo de ida y vuelta» (term `rtt`) and the reason it stopped. The time is shown only when the engine gave `rttMs` and the packet came back to its sender: never for silence, never for a warning the sender generates itself, and not for a warning whose term is unknown, which is seen as silence. The figure is the engine's, with a second line that says it is an example: the game measures nothing.
- The headers of a trip that leaves the office (the trace has `route-leaves-office`) are the ones seen in the local network: their labels end in «(en tu red local)» («Encabezado», «Encabezado del Echo Request», «Encabezado del Echo Reply»), and the last header field carries one muted note (`InspectionField.notes`, drawn as a muted line): «Fuera de la oficina el encabezado cambia: lo verás en el capítulo 2.». Until `nat` is known the box never says how it changes, and never names NAT or the public IP of the office. A trip that stays inside the office or the device has the plain labels and no note.
- The box of a TCP or UDP packet (`packet-fields.ts`, the fields of a packet that `inspection.ts` puts in order) adds, each behind the term it uses and left out until it is known: the ports in the header, written as the sheets write it, in one line `IP:puerto → IP:puerto` in place of the two lines of the IPs (`port` and `ip-port`; each end carries its port once the term of its role in that header is known, `source-port` for the source and `destination-port` for the destination, and shows only its IP before, so at 02-01 the header is `192.168.1.10 → 192.168.1.11:3000` and from 02-02 `192.168.1.10:51514 → 192.168.1.11:3000`; the header of the answer and the one «en internet» are written the same way, so in the answer the destination end is the sender of the request and shows its port with `destination-port`; without `port` or `ip-port` a header has the two lines of the IPs and no port; the end is marked with the term `ip-port`, and an ICMP header never has ports); a TCP or UDP packet calls its two headers «de ida» and «de vuelta», an ICMP one «del Echo Request» and «del Echo Reply»; «Saludo de tres pasos» (`handshake`: SYN, SYN-ACK and ACK, or SYN and the RST of a refusal, «rechazo» while `rst` is unknown) or «Consulta y respuesta» (`udp`) from `Trace.exchange`, as far as the packet got; «DNS» (`dns`); «Ruta que usó» (`route`); and «Traducción» (`nat`). A packet that its sender's own outbound firewall dropped never left, so it has no exchange and no such field.
- The field «DNS» of a `ping` or an `nc` by name says that the device resolved the name before sending the packet («Antes de mandar el paquete, … averigua la IP de …») and then the sentence of the reason of that step: it asked its resolver, or its hosts file answered and nobody was asked. That question is not drawn and leaves no row in the translation table (decided 4), so this line is where it shows; when the lookup failed, the cause is the line where the packet stopped and the field has only the first sentence. The field of a `dig` lists what the resolver answered: its records, whether the answer came from the cache (`dns-cache`) and its TTL (`dns-ttl`, «quedan N s» only once `dns-cache` is known, since it would tell a cached answer from a fresh one). A device with no resolver (`dns-unresolved`) has no field.
- The field «Ruta que usó» has one line per decision of `Trace.routes`, and says whose it is: «tu-laptop: 0.0.0.0/0 · ruta por defecto · siguiente salto 192.168.1.1». The holder of a decision is the sender or a router, never the sender of a packet that enters from outside, whose first decision is the router's. A router the map does not draw (`routerPlace`) has no line, and the IP of a next hop that belongs to such a router is left out, so the box never names or numbers what the player cannot see. «es la más específica: también cubría …» appears only when the route that lost has a shorter prefix; the other routes of the same prefix are a tie, not a less specific route. The default route is written 0.0.0.0/0. A packet that vanished at a router out of sight (`routerOutOfSight`) shows neither this field nor the translation.
- Once `nat` is known the note that the header changes outside the office is gone, the trip that leaves the office also shows the header «(en internet)» after the translation (the public IP and, for TCP and UDP, the public port; no TTL; the request only), and «Traducción» says what the NAT did: `192.168.1.10:51514 → 203.0.113.10:40001`, that it changed the source port too («traducción de puertos» with `napt`) and whether the packet made a row of the table or used one (`translation-table`). An ICMP translation has no ports (`Trace.translation.inside/outside.port` are absent): a ping shows only the IPs and says that ICMP uses none. A packet from outside shows `public end → device` and whether a forward (`port-forwarding`) or a row delivered it.
- A traceroute is one entry of the sent packets and its box says so: it is named «traceroute de <origin> a <destination>» (`tracerouteLabel`), defined by the term `traceroute` and not by the ping, and its header has no TTL line. It lists the hops of its last probe and never says where that probe stopped (no «Dónde y por qué se detuvo»): a traceroute only shows who answered, and a silent hop proves nothing about where the packet died. The box of a server of the internet calls its IP «IP pública» once that term is known, and «dirección IP» before.
- The box of a router says what a router is and, for each local network it faces, its IP there («IP en Piso 1»); an interface that faces another router or a server is left out, so the box never names or numbers a router the map does not draw.
- The field of the routers a packet crossed is «Pasó por» until the term `hop` is known and «Saltos» after. What it lists agrees with what the map draws (`router-place.ts`, built on `drawsRouter` of `pieces.ts`): a router the map draws is named with the IP of its interface, and a stretch through routers it does not draw is written «dentro de la oficina» or «fuera de la oficina» (by `facesALan`), once per stretch, without naming them. The reason a packet stopped (`describeReason`, with `placesOf`) and the lines of a traceroute follow the same rule, so no text names a router the player cannot see. An incident therefore draws the routers that warn (09) or answer a traceroute (10). In an incident whose routers wait for a traceroute, the box of a packet that is not a traceroute and that a router dropped without a warning (`routerOutOfSight`, `flow-runs.ts`) has neither the field of the routers it crossed nor the reason it stopped, before and after a traceroute, and its stamp does not move: it says only the outcome («timeout: pasó el tiempo de espera y no volvió nada»), because the sender of that packet cannot know where it died. The box of the traceroute keeps both. The field lists at most as many hops as a traceroute prints (`TRACEROUTE_MAX_HOPS`) and a last line counts the rest («y 56 saltos más»), because a packet that goes round a loop crosses the same two routers again and again and 64 lines would bury the box.
- In a send incident, and in the case a diagnose is on, every origin of its chips (a device or an actor) opens its terminal and has no lock; the next case brings its own origins (`originPieces`, through `sendingOf`). The terminal shows the trays with a choice to make; a tray with a single chip is fixed, not shown, and its chip is always picked. Its line is in Linux syntax and follows the command of the message chip: `ping <address>`, with `-t <ttl>` (the iputils TTL option) when the message sets one; `traceroute -I <address>` (`-I` makes it send ICMP Echo, as the engine does); and, for a `packet`, «paquete de <origin> a <address>», which names no command and so says nothing about ping. On a macOS laptop `-t` means something else (a timeout), so the line is not meant to be copied there. A message with no `command` keeps the first design: an Echo Request is the ping command and any other message is «paquete armado a mano».
- In an identify incident, every device with a card opens it and has no lock (`identifyPieces`); the other devices stay locked.
- In a diagnose incident no firewall is configurable (`canConfigure` is false there, since the player changes nothing in the world): its pieces show the lock, open no card and stay inspectable. Only the origin of the case opens, with its terminal.
- **A piece has one button on the map, and that button opens one card with a tab for each card the piece has** (V15 = B, decided 22 of chapter 2). `pieceCard(session, pieceId)` lists them in the order it opens them when the incident names none: the terminal of an origin, the questions of an identify incident, the card that classifies, the «Ajustes de red» of a device, its «Servicios» (the listen address of each service the incident offers), the «Tabla de rutas», the «Tabla de traducciones», the «Registros» of a zone the player edits and the «Caché» of a resolver. A piece with one card is not `tabbed` and opens that card as it always did; the tab that opens first is the one the incident names for the piece (`send.opensFirst`, when the piece has that card) or the first of the list. There is no second entry in a label and no `PIECE_ID.settings`: `terminalCard` is non-null for the piece of an origin and null for any other id. A piece is `configurable`, and unlocked, when it has at least one card; one with none is locked. Waiting for the term: the read-only cards wait for theirs (`routing-table` for the routes of a router and of a device that sends, `translation-table` for the translations of the router of the office, `dns-cache` for the cache of a resolver, `dhcp` for the DNS server of a device), so no router, resolver or device of chapter 1 or 4 opens a card they did not have. Only a send incident has these cards.
- A device is `insideBlock` (a mark that is absent otherwise, not `false`) while it is inside the block of the card that classifies (`insideBlockPieces`): its owner from the start and each item the player placed in the first bin. The scene draws it as the outline of the block: `MapPiece` carries the mark and `computeLayout` surrounds the pieces that have it.
- Not here: the session state and its reducer (`src/game/`), drawing the map or a panel (`src/scene/`, `src/ui/`), the Spanish text of a term or a reason (`src/copy/`).
- May import: `@/engine`, `@/copy`, `@/levels`, and other files of `src/game/` (`@/game/<file>`).
