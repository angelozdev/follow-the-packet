# game/view

What the map and the panels show, computed from the session: piece ids, piece labels and locks, where a router stands for the player (`router-place.ts`), the inspection box, and, in `cards/`, a firewall's rules named by chips (`firewall-card.ts`), the terminal of an origin (`terminal-card.ts`), what a traceroute printed (`traceroute-lines.ts`) the card of a device in an identify incident (`identify-card.ts`), the «Ajustes de red» card of a device (`settings-card.ts`) and the card of the case a diagnose is on (`case-card.ts`).

- Every piece is an entity of the catalog in the variant of its model (`EntityRef`): a device and a firewall by their `kind`, the edge by the network's `edge`, an actor by its `kind`. Its term comes from `entityTerm` (`ENTITY_TERMS` in `src/copy/terms.ts`): `device` for a laptop or a printer, `firewall` for the firewall of a laptop, `router`, `internet`, and the AWS ones (`ec2-instance`, `security-group`, `internet-gateway`); none ever for an actor. A firewall label, the edge label and the internet label carry their term's name (`Security Group servidor2`, `firewall laptop-lucia`, `router`, `internet`); a device's label is its name (`servidor2`, `tu-laptop`) and an actor's label is its own name. An actor of kind `server` (a server of the internet) is not an actor piece: `pieceViews` draws it as a device piece of the variant `internet-server` (term `device`) under the id of its actor, `PIECE_ID.actor`, and `inspect` shows it as a device: what a device is and its dirección IP (never «IP privada», its address is not one), with no «Quién es». In a world with `routers`, every router is a piece of the edge in the variant `router` (`PIECE_ID.router`, labelled by its own name) in place of the lone edge; its box says only what a router is, because what the office router does does not hold for the routers of the provider.
- The inspection of a piece whose variant has a term, or of a packet, starts with `INSPECTION_TEXT.whatIs`, the definition of that term, once the term is known (a packet's term is its ICMP message, and `packet` while that one is not known); a piece without a term has no definition. A device's IP is its dirección IP until the term IP privada is known. A device of an office also has a «Conexión» field once `network` is known: «Conectado a la red.» or «No está conectado: no forma parte de la red.». A device with no cable keeps its IP, because an IP can be set without a cable: what says it is outside the network is that line (and where the map draws it). A device of a VPC has no such field. The «IP pública» field (term `public-ip`, known from 01-08 on, so also in chapter 4) is the other way round: a device of a VPC shows its public IP or «No tiene», and an actor of the internet shows its IP, while a device of an office shows no such field at all, because how a packet leaves the office is a black box in chapter 1 and its laptops have no public IP of their own to show.
- A piece is drawn when its term is known (`isDrawn`): the firewall, the edge (or the routers) and the internet wait for `firewall` or `security-group`, `router` or `internet-gateway`, and `internet`, whether or not someone is outside the network. Devices and actors are the base of every map and are always drawn. An office of chapter 1 shows the router and the internet from the incident that introduces them. The inspection of a piece that is not drawn is null.
- What the player has not learned is not shown. A packet that a firewall the player does not know stopped is inspected with `describeReason(reason, world, known)` and `describeOutcome(outcome, known)`: its stop says only that nothing came back, and the device does not list the firewall. Its path ends at the door of the device, with the lost stamp (`hiddenStop`, `src/game/flow-runs.ts`).
- An incident draws only what it lists (`shown`, in the contract of `src/levels/`): a device, a client actor, a server or a router is a piece when its id is in the list, or when the incident has none, and, for a router, once `router` is known. A firewall is drawn with its device, so a firewall of a device that is not drawn is not drawn either, and `inspect` is null for a piece the incident does not show. The lone edge and the internet have no id to list: with a list they are drawn only when it holds something outside the office (an actor, or a router with no interface facing a local network of the office), so 01-06, 01-07 and 01-11 draw no globe and 01-08 to 01-10 do. `pieceViews` is the one place that decides it, and the scene lays out only the pieces it is given.
- Every piece has a state, read from the runs: a device is `intruded` while a forbidden flow reaches it, a firewall is `blocking` while its device's firewalls stop a flow, and every other piece is `normal`.
- The task card of a firewall (`FirewallCard`) carries the words of its kind: what it says without rules (`empty`) and the label of its new-rule slot (`slot`). A host firewall says «reglas» and «Regla nueva», never «de entrada»: that is the name of the later AWS term `inbound-rule`, which a Security Group keeps.
- The inspection of a firewall lists its inbound rules and, when its card has an outbound list, labels the two directions («Entrada» and «Salida» for a host firewall, «Reglas de entrada» and «Reglas de salida» for a Security Group) and lists the outbound rules too (the rule that lets everything out reads «deja salir todo el tráfico»); the outbound field shows as soon as the term that opens the outbound rules (`RULES_TERM.outbound`) is known, in any mechanic, and is left out before.
- Every inspection field declares the term it uses; a field whose term is not known yet is left out. A packet shows its message once Echo Request is known; a packet the player sent is inspected like a flow's. The edge and the internet are inspected only while they are on the map.
- The box of a packet in an office also shows, in this order after its message: «Encabezado» (called «Encabezado del Echo Request» when the reply header is shown beside it and `echo-request` is known; term `header`: «IP de origen» and «IP de destino», named by the terms `source-ip` and `destination-ip` once they are known and by «Origen» and «Destino» before, and «TTL al salir» once `ttl` is known, which a traceroute's box leaves out because its last probe has a TTL the player never chose), «Encabezado del Echo Reply» (the same two lines swapped, when the packet was answered and `echo-reply` is known), the routers it crossed (`Trace.hops`), the result, «Tiempo de ida y vuelta» (term `rtt`) and the reason it stopped. The time is shown only when the engine gave `rttMs` and the packet came back to its sender: never for silence, never for a warning the sender generates itself, and not for a warning whose term is unknown, which is seen as silence. The figure is the engine's, with a second line that says it is an example: the game measures nothing.
- The headers of a trip that leaves the office (the trace has `route-leaves-office`) are the ones seen in the local network: their labels end in «(en tu red local)» («Encabezado», «Encabezado del Echo Request», «Encabezado del Echo Reply»), and the last header field carries one muted note (`InspectionField.notes`, drawn as a muted line): «Fuera de la oficina el encabezado cambia: lo verás en el capítulo 2.». The box never says how it changes, and never names NAT or the public IP of the office. A trip that stays inside the office or the device has the plain labels and no note.
- A traceroute is one entry of the sent packets and its box says so: it is named «traceroute de <origin> a <destination>» (`tracerouteLabel`), defined by the term `traceroute` and not by the ping, and its header has no TTL line. The box of a server of the internet calls its IP «IP pública» once that term is known, and «dirección IP» before.
- The box of a router says what a router is and, for each local network it faces, its IP there («IP en Piso 1»); an interface that faces another router or a server is left out, so the box never names or numbers a router the map does not draw.
- The field of the routers a packet crossed is «Pasó por» until the term `hop` is known and «Saltos» after. What it lists agrees with what the map draws (`router-place.ts`, built on `drawsRouter` of `pieces.ts`): a router the map draws is named with the IP of its interface, and a stretch through routers it does not draw is written «dentro de la oficina» or «fuera de la oficina» (by `facesALan`), once per stretch, without naming them. The reason a packet stopped (`describeReason`, with `placesOf`) and the lines of a traceroute follow the same rule, so no text names a router the player cannot see. An incident therefore draws the routers that warn (09) or answer a traceroute (10). The field lists at most as many hops as a traceroute prints (`TRACEROUTE_MAX_HOPS`) and a last line counts the rest («y 56 saltos más»), because a packet that goes round a loop crosses the same two routers again and again and 64 lines would bury the box.
- In a send incident, and in the case a diagnose is on, every origin of its chips (a device or an actor) opens its terminal and has no lock; the next case brings its own origins (`originPieces`, through `sendingOf`). The terminal shows the trays with a choice to make; a tray with a single chip is fixed, not shown, and its chip is always picked. Its line is in Linux syntax and follows the command of the message chip: `ping <address>`, with `-t <ttl>` (the iputils TTL option) when the message sets one; `traceroute -I <address>` (`-I` makes it send ICMP Echo, as the engine does); and, for a `packet`, «paquete de <origin> a <address>», which names no command and so says nothing about ping. On a macOS laptop `-t` means something else (a timeout), so the line is not meant to be copied there. A message with no `command` keeps the first design: an Echo Request is the ping command and any other message is «paquete armado a mano».
- In an identify incident, every device with a card opens it and has no lock (`identifyPieces`); the other devices stay locked.
- A device the incident offers settings for (`settingsDeviceIds`) has two entries, each its own: its piece (`PIECE_ID.device`) opens the terminal, and its `settings` entry (`PIECE_ID.settings`, with the label «Ajustes de red de <name>») opens the settings card. The entry is the second button of the device's label and not a piece: it has no shape, no place on the map and no inspection, and `parsePieceId` does not read it. `terminalCard` is null for the entry and `settingsCard` for the piece.
- Not here: the session state and its reducer (`src/game/`), drawing the map or a panel (`src/scene/`, `src/ui/`), the Spanish text of a term or a reason (`src/copy/`).
- May import: `@/engine`, `@/copy`, `@/levels`, and other files of `src/game/` (`@/game/<file>`).
