# game/view/cards/terminal

What the terminal of an origin shows, computed from the session: the terminal itself (`terminal-card.ts`: its trays of origin, address and message chips, the line the picks make and the pieces that open one), what a traceroute sent from it printed (`traceroute-lines.ts`) what an `nc`, a `dig` or a `curl` printed (`output-card.ts`) and where the line of the terminal may break (`command-groups.ts`).

- A terminal is data: the screen draws it and reports the player's picks; nothing here draws or changes the session.
- The line of an `nc` is `nc -vz -w 3 <address> <port>` and the line of a `dig` is `dig <name>` with no server (it asks the resolver of the network settings of the device, decided 29); `terminalLine` gives null for an `nc` whose chip has no port. The line of a `curl` is `curl` with its options (`-i`, `-u <user:password>`, `-X PUT`, `-d <body>`, `--connect-timeout <seconds>`, in that order, a body, a `user:password` or a url the shell would read as more than one word in single quotes) and the whole url of the address chip; it is null for a `curl` to a chip that is no url and for the `browser`, which is not a command (the terminal does not offer it as a message: it is the tab `browserCard` describes, `cards/web/`). The `to` tray labels a url chip by its whole url, so the chips of one host are told apart; when the chips give their urls by parts there is no `to` tray and the card carries the `strip` of `cards/web/url-strip.ts` instead.
- `commandGroups(line)` splits the line a terminal shows (any `terminalLine`, and the loop of a burst) into groups that never break inside, and the card breaks the line only between them (S1, decided 2026-10-25): a group is a shell word (a quoted word, a `$( )` and a tag are one word), except that a flag with no value (`-i`, `-vz`, `-I`) stays with the word before it, so `curl -i` is one group, and an option that takes a value (`-u`, `-b`, `-X`, `-d`, `-w`, `-t`, `--connect-timeout`) with its value. A group that is a url is a list of two parts, cut after the last slash of its path, so a url breaks before `http://` (between groups) and after its path and nowhere else; the parts joined with a space between groups give the line back. An option the table does not know breaks from its value: add it there.
- The output of an `nc` or a `dig` (`toolOutput(session, picks)`) is null until one was sent with those picks. It reads the last send of the combination (`session.sent` keeps the last of each) and says which result its trace stands for: for an `nc`, an `open` connection (`replied`, with the IP it reached and the round trip), a `refused` one (`refused`) or a `timeout`; for a `dig`, the reply of the resolver (`Trace.dns` with its status, records and SOA; the game clock of the send moves the date), a `timeout` or a `refused` one (the host at the resolver address answered with Port Unreachable). The lines come from `@/copy` (`ncOutput`, `digOutput`; the source of each line is in `src/copy/tools/CLAUDE.md`). For a `curl`, the lines are those of `curlOutput` (a plain `curl` prints the body; `-i` also the status line, the headers and a blank line; a refusal, `curl: (7) Failed to connect to <host>:<port> after <N> ms: Could not connect to server`, with the round trip of the game as an example); a wait that ran out prints no line, so there is no block and the inspection box says that `curl` waited and gave up with code 28. **Every other result prints nothing** and the card is null: the origin of those lines was not confirmed, so the inspection box is what says what happened. The query that an `nc` by name makes to the resolver is not a send of the player and its lines are not here.
- `toolOutputs(session, picks)` is what the card draws: for a `curl`, one block for each send of the same chips since the world last changed (`Session.history`), each titled «Envío 1», «Envío 2» in the order of the sends (a send that printed nothing has no block but keeps its number, the same count as `expect.nth` of a task), so the first body can be compared with the second; for an `nc` or a `dig` the one block of their last send, with no title. `toolOutput` is the block of the last send only.
- The lines are grouped into segments for the fold (V11 = B): when the output has key lines (the status line of a `dig` and its answer: the header, the `ANSWER SECTION` and its records) every run of the other lines is one `folded` segment and each key line stays a `line`; an output with no key line (an `nc`, a `dig` that got no answer) is never folded. `folded` is how many lines the folded segments hold in all. Folding hides lines, it does not drop them: the segments hold every line printed. The fold itself is the card's, not the session's (`src/ui/incident/map-cards/terminal/`).
- The numbered output of a traceroute is not a `ToolOutput`: it stays `tracerouteLines`, with the same lines and notes as before, and the card draws both.
- Not here: the other cards (`src/game/view/cards/`), piece ids, piece labels and the inspection box (`src/game/view/`), the session state and its reducer (`src/game/`), drawing the terminal (`src/ui/incident/map-cards/terminal/`), the Spanish text (`src/copy/`).
- May import: `@/engine`, `@/copy`, `@/levels`, and other files of `src/game/` (`@/game/<file>`, `@/game/view/<file>`).
