# game/view/cards

What the cards that open beside a piece of the map show, computed from the session: the task card of a firewall, whose kind, title and rules (named by chips) it computes (`firewall-card.ts`), the terminal of an origin and the output of a traceroute sent from it (`terminal/`, which has its own guide), the card of a device in an identify incident (`identify-card.ts`), the «Ajustes de red» card of a device (`settings-card.ts`) and the card of the case a diagnose is on (`case-card.ts`).

- The task card of a firewall (`firewallCard`) has the inbound list as `rules`, `empty` and `slot`, and, only while the ladder has opened the outbound rules of its kind and the incident offers Destination chips (`hasOutboundList`), an `outbound` list: the labels of the two tabs (`TASK_CARD_TEXT.tabs`, the official name of the term of each direction: `entrada` and `salida` for a host firewall, `regla de entrada` and `regla de salida` for a Security Group), the outbound rules named by the destination chips (`OutboundRow`, read `what → to`), what it says without rules and the label of its slot. Without it the key is absent, not empty. What a host firewall says without outbound rules states that someone configured it so and never suggests that a closed outbound side is what a firewall has by default. A rule no chip names (a world may carry the rule that lets everything out, and an incident that is not a filter offers no chips) is never an error: its row is written from the rule itself with the terms the player knows (`describeRule`, a `{ id, text }` row), so `firewallCard` and `inspect` never throw for a legitimate world.
- A card is data: the screen draws it and reports the player's picks; nothing here draws or changes the session.
- The output of a traceroute (`tracerouteLines(session, picks)`) is null until a traceroute was sent with those picks. It has one line per probe, as `traceroute` of the engine ran them: the hop number, the IP that answered (a star when nobody did), the time and, on the last line of a Destination Host or Network Unreachable, its mark (`!H` or `!N`, what the command prints), plus the notes that explain the star, the marks and that the times are examples, and the reason when the sender could not send at all. What the player does not know is left out: no time before `rtt`, and a warning whose term is unknown is a star, as the map shows no warning. A router the map does not draw is not named by its IP: the line says «dentro de la oficina» or «fuera de la oficina».
- The card of a device in an identify incident (`identifyCard(session, pieceId)`; `identifyPieces` gives the piece ids that open one) has the device's name as its title, the card's question (or null), and each tray with its name, whether it is `segmented`, the chip `saved` as the answer (the `initialPick` until the player confirms another) and its chips. Each chip carries the `lines` the card shows when the player presses «Listo» with it picked: its own `feedback` and then, for a chip that is not the tray's `initialPick`, the `initialPickRule`; so the rule of the note shows by every path but the note's own chip, whose feedback already is the rule. It also carries `correct`, true when a task expects that chip, so the card writes a right line as a note and a wrong one as an error. The card draws these; it decides none of them.
- The settings card (`settingsCard(session, entryId)`, opened from the entry `PIECE_ID.settings(deviceId)`; `settingsDeviceIds` lists the devices that have one) has a title («Ajustes de red de <device>»), the piece of the device it sits beside (`pieceId`) and, for each setting the incident offers for the device, a tray: its id, its name (the official name of its term, as plain text), its chips and `picked`, the id of the chip whose value is what the setting has in the world now (`settingValue` of `@/levels`, the one the session compares with when it applies a setting: the gateway or the DNS server of the device, the chip with no value when it has none, or the listen address of the service the setting names), or null when no chip stands for it. The card has no draft: a pick is a move of the session, so `picked` always says what the world is. The terminal shows no setting: it does not know them.
- The case card (`caseCard(session)`; null unless the incident is a diagnose) has the number of the case and how many there are, the case's report, for each origin of the case its `name` and the tests it can send (every address with every message: the line the terminal prints for them, as `terminalLine`, and whether the player already sent it in this case), the cause question and its options in the order the case gives them, and `closed` (the right cause was named) and `last`. Each option is `untried`, `wrong` or `right` by what the player named, and carries its feedback only once named: a wrong one shows its own and leaves the others to try, the right one closes the case. Nothing about which option is right is in the card until it is named. `caseSummary(session)` is how the cases went for the result card: how many there are and the numbers (from 1) of those that were not right at the first try.
- The terminal of an origin works the same in a diagnose: the chips are those of the case the session is on.
- Not here: piece ids, piece labels and the inspection box (`src/game/view/`), the session state and its reducer (`src/game/`), drawing a card (`src/ui/incident/map-cards/`), the Spanish text (`src/copy/`).
- May import: `@/engine`, `@/copy`, `@/levels`, and other files of `src/game/` (`@/game/<file>`, `@/game/view/<file>`).
