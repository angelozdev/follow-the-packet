# game/view/cards

What the cards of a piece show, each one a part of a tab of the panel of its piece (`../panel/`), computed from the session: the task card of a firewall, whose kind, title and rules (named by chips) it computes (`firewall-card.ts`), the terminal of an origin and what a traceroute, an `nc`, a `dig` or a `curl` sent from it printed (`terminal/`, which has its own guide), the card of a device in an identify incident (`identify-card.ts`) and the one that classifies other devices (`identify/`, which has its own guide), the «Ajustes de red» and «Servicios» cards of a device (`settings-card.ts`), the tables of a piece (`table-card.ts`), the card of the case a diagnose is on (`case-card.ts`), the cards of the web (`web/`, which has its own guide: the browser of an origin, the «Sitios» card of a web server and the fields of the inspection box that tell a request) and the cards a piece opens from its one button, each as a tab (`piece-card.ts`).

- The task card of a firewall (`firewallCard`) has the inbound list as `rules`, `empty` and `slot`, and, only while the ladder has opened the outbound rules of its kind and the incident offers Destination chips (`hasOutboundList`), an `outbound` list: the labels of the two tabs (`TASK_CARD_TEXT.tabs`, the official name of the term of each direction: `entrada` and `salida` for a host firewall, `regla de entrada` and `regla de salida` for a Security Group), the outbound rules named by the destination chips (`OutboundRow`, read `what → to`), what it says without rules and the label of its slot. Without it the key is absent, not empty. What a host firewall says without outbound rules states that someone configured it so and never suggests that a closed outbound side is what a firewall has by default. A rule no chip names (a world may carry the rule that lets everything out, and an incident that is not a filter offers no chips) is never an error: its row is written from the rule itself with the terms the player knows (`describeRule`, a `{ id, text }` row), so `firewallCard` and `inspect` never throw for a legitimate world.
- A card is data: the screen draws it and reports the player's picks; nothing here draws or changes the session.
- The output of a traceroute (`tracerouteLines(session, picks)`) is null until a traceroute was sent with those picks. It has one line per probe, as `traceroute` of the engine ran them: the hop number, the IP that answered (a star when nobody did), the time and, on the last line of a Destination Host or Network Unreachable, its mark (`!H` or `!N`, what the command prints), plus the notes that explain the star, the marks and that the times are examples, and the reason when the sender could not send at all. What the player does not know is left out: no time before `rtt`, and a warning whose term is unknown is a star, as the map shows no warning; a router with no route, which has no term of its own until `net-unreachable` is known, is a star until the first warning term (`host-unreachable`) is known, then shows its `!N` mark and a note that names no term. A router the map does not draw is not named by its IP: the line says «dentro de la oficina» or «fuera de la oficina».
- The card of a device in an identify incident (`identifyCard(session, pieceId)`; `identifyPieces` gives the piece ids that open one) has the device's name as its title, the card's question (or null), and each tray with its name, whether it is `segmented`, the chip `saved` as the answer (the `initialPick` until the player confirms another) and its chips. Each chip carries the `lines` the card shows when the player presses «Listo» with it picked: its own `feedback` and then, for a chip that is not the tray's `initialPick`, the `initialPickRule`; so the rule of the note shows by every path but the note's own chip, whose feedback already is the rule. It also carries `correct`, true when a task expects that chip, so the card writes a right line as a note and a wrong one as an error. The card draws these; it decides none of them.
- The settings card (`settingsCard(session, pieceId)`) is the «Ajustes de red» of a device. It has a title («Ajustes de red de <device>»), the piece of the device it sits beside (`pieceId`) and, for each gateway or DNS server setting the incident offers for the device, a tray: its id, its name (the official name of its term, as plain text), its chips and `picked`, the id of the chip whose value is what the setting has in the world now (`settingValue` of `@/levels`, the one the session compares with when it applies a setting), or null when no chip stands for it. The tray of a DNS server shows only once `dhcp` is known. A device that has a DNS server the incident gives no way to set shows it as a line (`dns`: the label, the address with the server behind it when it is drawn, and «Lo prestó DHCP.») once `dhcp` is known, so the card of 02-10 is read only; the card is null when there is no tray and no line. The card has no draft: a pick is a move of the session, so `picked` always says what the world is. The terminal shows no setting: it does not know them.
- The services card (`servicesCard(session, pieceId)`) is the «Servicios» of a device: a tray for the listen address of each service the incident offers a setting for, named with the service it sets (its protocol, once known, and its port), with the chips and `picked` as above. Null when the incident offers the device no listen address.
- The sites of a web server are a table of kind `sites` (`TableKind` gains it; `sitesCard`, in `web/`, builds it with the helpers `table-card.ts` exports): the piece that owns a table of sites opens it as the tab «Sitios» (`PieceTabId` gains `sites` and `browser`, `OPENS_FIRST` is a full `Record` of the cards an incident may name to open first). The tab of the browser (`browserCard`, in `web/`) comes right after the terminal in `pieceCard`.
- A table (`TableCard`, in `table-card.ts`) is data for the `Table` of `@/design`: its columns, its rows (cells of text, whether each is code, the `entryId` the session removes a row by, and whether the last packet sent used it), a muted `note` and, only when the incident lets the player edit it (a table of `send.tables` or, in a diagnose, a table of the fix of the case in play: `tableSpec` reads `tablesOf(session.incident, currentCase(session))`, so a case with a fix makes the router or the server that owns the table open it, and a case with none opens nothing; once the session no longer accepts edits, `canEdit` false because the case is closed or the incident is, the card stays as the same table read only, with no trays, no button, no `tableId`, no `entryId` on its rows and no problem, so it never offers a control that would do nothing), the trays of the entry, the label of the button that adds one, the `tableId` of the moves and the `problem` the engine gave to the last entry it refused (`Session.entryProblem`, said in the words of `TABLE_TEXT.problems`: one text for each code of `RECORD_PROBLEMS`, `ROUTE_PROBLEMS` and `CHIP_PROBLEMS`, by `problemText`, which the registry (`../registry/`) uses for the line of a refusal). `routesCard` is the routing table the engine derives (`routingTableOf`) with the column of the next hop called «Por dónde sale» until `next-hop` is known (decided 23); a next hop is written with its name when the map draws that router and as «dentro» or «fuera de la oficina» when it does not. `translationsCard` reads `Session.history`, not `Session.sent`: `sent` keeps only the last send of each combination of chips and would lose rows. It lists first the fixed rows of the router, one for each port forward of the world (`forwardRows` of the engine, which gives the device end and the public end; the first cell reads `TCP · fija`, so the mark is a word and not only a colour), then the TCP or UDP sends that made a row, in order. A fixed row has the id `fixed|…` so it never shares a key with a row a packet made, and it is marked as used by the packet a forward delivered, like any row a packet used. `recordsCard` lists the A and CNAME records of the zone (a TTL column once `dns-ttl` is known) and says in one muted line that the zone has records of its own that are not edited. `cacheCard` lists what a resolver holds at the second of the game clock, which the engine computes (`cacheAfter` replays the sends since the world last changed), so the seconds left are the engine's; it is empty after the TTL runs out. The row used by the last packet is the one the engine chose for the holder of the table (`Trace.routes`) or the translation that packet used; a traceroute marks none.
- The card of a piece (`pieceCard(session, pieceId)`) lists the cards the piece has as tabs (`PieceTab`: the id, the label and the card itself), whether it is `tabbed` and which tab opens `first`. It is the only place that decides which cards a piece has and in what order; each builder (`terminalCard`, `browserCard`, `identifyCard`, `classifyCard`, `settingsCard`, `servicesCard`, `routesCard`, `translationsCard`, `recordsCard`, `cacheCard`) returns null when the piece does not have its card. The read-only cards wait for their terms (see `src/game/view/CLAUDE.md`).
- The card of a piece that is a server of the internet carries `roles`, the line that says its roles in words with the terms the player knows (`MAP_TEXT.rolesLine`); the screen draws it once above the card, whatever its tabs.
- The cards of a piece are the parts of the panel of the piece (`../panel/`): each card kind belongs to one of its six tabs. `PieceTab.label` is the name of the part (`PIECE_TEXT.parts`), not the name of a tab of the panel.
- The case card (`caseCard(session)`; null unless the incident is a diagnose) has the number of the case and how many there are, the case's report, for each origin of the case its `name` and the tests it can send (every address with every message: the line the terminal prints for them, as `terminalLine`, and whether the player already sent it in this case), when the case has a fix, `repair` (its tasks, each with `done` from `taskHolds` over the packets sent since the world last changed, and the tables to edit, each with the id of the piece that opens it and its title, taken from the very `TableCard` that piece opens, so the two cannot drift; the table itself is a tab of the router's or the server's piece card, never a card of the case, and the terminal is the card of the origin, so the case offers both in the same session; `repair` is null for a case with no fix), the cause question and its options in the order the case gives them, and `closed` (the right cause was named and, in a case with a fix, the tasks hold) and `last`. Each option is `untried`, `wrong` or `right` by what the player named, and carries its feedback only once named: a wrong one shows its own and leaves the others to try, the right one closes the case (a case with a fix stays open until its tasks hold too). Nothing about which option is right is in the card until it is named. `caseSummary(session)` is how the cases went for the result card: how many there are and the numbers (from 1) of those that were not right at the first try.
- The terminal of an origin works the same in a diagnose: the chips are those of the case the session is on.
- Not here: piece ids, piece labels and the inspection box (`src/game/view/`), the session state and its reducer (`src/game/`), drawing a card (`src/ui/incident/map-cards/`), the Spanish text (`src/copy/`).
- May import: `@/engine`, `@/copy`, `@/levels`, and other files of `src/game/` (`@/game/<file>`, `@/game/view/<file>`).
