# scene/traffic/flight

The flight of a packet on the map, with animations on: the pure times of the trip, the SMIL elements that move the packet and its label along its route, and the list of the sends of the player that are in the air. The still drawing of a packet, its label and the stage that mounts the flight are one folder up (`../`). Hidden from screen readers like the rest of the traffic.

- `flight-plan.ts`: the pure times of a flight, no DOM. `flightPlan(path, end, wait, changesAt)` says, in seconds from the moment a packet leaves, how long it takes to reach the end of the path (`turn`: 120 px per second, `PACKET_SPEED`), how long it is in the air (`travel`: twice the path when it is answered or a router warns), how long it is on the map (`duration`: the travel and the half second of its ending, `PACKET_END_SECONDS`), when each shape shows (`glyphs`: the one going, the ring or warning coming back, the warning of the sender's own device, which does not travel and appears at the end of the path), the windows of the labels (`labels`: the first leg until the router that translates the ports and the last from there, and coming back the reply with the ports swapped, the last leg first) and the ending (`burst` when blocked, `shrink` when lost, `appears`). `distanceAt` and `routeOf` say where the packet is at a second; `motionOf` gives the `keyTimes` and `keyPoints` of its `animateMotion`; `flowCycles` gives a live flow its copies (a packet every `FLOW_PERIOD` 3.5 s, shifted `FLOW_OFFSET` 1.1 s for each flow before it, and `ceil(duration / 3.5)` copies, each with a period of that many periods and shifted by one, when its trip lasts longer than the period); `sendCycle` and `answerBackAt` give a send its cycle and the second where its answer is back at its sender, which is when a send that waits for it may leave; `keyframes` turns the values of an attribute at some seconds into `keyTimes` and `values` that start at 0 and end at 1; `labelRide` gives the points where a label rides, above the packet and inside the drawing, and the `keyTimes` and `keyPoints` that keep it with the packet. The numbers are its own (120, 3.5, 1.1, 0.5).
- `use-sends-in-flight.ts`: `useSendsInFlight(sent, secondsOf)` and `flightKeyOf`, described under `traffic.tsx` (`../traffic.tsx`); it holds the only timer of the folder (`setTimeout` for the next trip to end, re-armed when the list changes) and no test file of its own: `flight.test.tsx` and `flight.browser.test.tsx` test it through `Traffic` and `FlightLabels` (`../traffic.tsx`), drawn by a `Stage` component that calls `useSendsInTheAir` once, as `MapStage` does, and `../../map-stage.browser.test.tsx` through the real stage.
- `flight.tsx`: `FlightBody` (a packet: a group that one `animateMotion` carries along its route with `keyPoints`, `keyTimes`, `calcMode="linear"` and `repeatCount`, drawn around the point where the group stands, with each shape in a group of opacity 0 that a discrete `animate` shows during its span, `animateTransform` scaling the shape that shrinks and the ring that bursts) and `FlightTags` (the labels: another `animateMotion` over the centers of `labelRide`, with a label for each window, so the one that shows changes at the router). The shapes are `PacketGlyph` (`../still-trace.tsx`) and the label `PacketTag` (`../packet-tag.tsx`), the ones of the still map.
- SMIL, as measured in Chromium on 2026-10-19 (the Vitest browser tests do it again every run): React 19 passes `keyPoints`, `keyTimes`, `calcMode`, `repeatCount`, `begin`, `dur` and `path` to the DOM as they are (`flight.test.tsx`). A numeric `begin` is read on the timeline of the whole svg, not from the mount of its element: a group mounted when the timeline is at 10 s with `begin="0s"` has already ended. So a live flow keeps its numeric `begin` and `repeatCount="indefinite"` (its phase is that of the timeline, wherever it was mounted) and a send is `begin="indefinite"` with `beginElementAt(wait)` called on each of its animations when its group is mounted (a callback ref that returns a cleanup, which `endElement`s them: `main.tsx` is under StrictMode, which runs the callback, its cleanup and the callback again). The callback is a `useCallback` of the wait, and its identity must stay: when it changes React runs the old cleanup and the new callback and the send would begin again at every re-render of the stage (`flight.test.tsx` and `flight.browser.test.tsx` render a flying send again and see it keep its start). `getStartTime()` of an animation throws `InvalidStateError` once its interval has ended; read it before moving the clock. A browser test stops the clock with `svg.pauseAnimations()` and puts it at an instant with `svg.setCurrentTime(t)`, and `getComputedStyle` of a group shows the opacity a discrete `animate` gives it at that instant.
- Tests: `flight-plan.test.ts` (the times, pure), `flight.test.tsx` (jsdom: what is drawn, with which attributes; the SMIL probe; the sends of one combination with fake timers) and `flight.browser.test.tsx` (Chromium: the packet is where the plan says at an instant, a send starts from zero after the clock has moved on and under StrictMode, a send sent again leaves the first on its way and starts a second, the label changes at the router, the ending of each kind, the label inside the drawing; it mounts both `svg`s as `MapStage` does); the order of the label over a stamp and the stamp under the pointer are in `../../map-stage.browser.test.tsx`.
- Not here: the still trace and the label of a packet (`../still-trace.tsx`, `../packet-tag.tsx`), the stage that mounts the flight and the labels that fly (`../traffic.tsx`), where a path, a shape or a label stands (`../../marks/`), anything that decides whether a packet moves or where it ends (`src/engine/`).
- May import: `@/copy`, only types from `@/engine`, other files of `src/scene/` (`@/scene/<folder>/<file>`).
