# scene/traffic

The look of a packet and the packets of the map, in SVG, in the units of the layout (one unit is one pixel): the trace of each packet, its label and, in `flight/`, its flight. It takes what the pure step of `../marks/still-packets.ts` decided (paths, shapes, labels) and the plan of `flight/flight-plan.ts` (times) and draws it; it decides no outcome. Hidden from screen readers by the `aria-hidden` of the svg that holds it (`../map-stage.tsx`): what a screen reader hears of a packet is its stamp and the box of its stamp, which say the same words as its label.

- `flow-look.ts`: `flowLook` turns each packet's result (`intruder`, `reached`, `replied`, `blocked`, `warning`) into its look (its message, whether it is an intruder, how its run ends, `PacketEnd`) and the state of its stamp. A packet that ends in a warning the player knows (`warning`, which `src/game/` leaves empty while the term of the warning is unknown) looks like that warning and leaves the stamp of a warning: `warned` when a router sent it, so it travels back along the path from that router, and `self-warned` when the sender's own device did, so it does not travel and the path ends at that device's doorstep. A datagram that reached its host and got a Port Unreachable from it (`warning.by` is `host`) is not an arrival: `flowLook` checks the warning before `reached`, so the packet goes to the host, the warning comes back from it along the same path (`warned`) and the stamp is the warning's, because no program received the datagram; with the term unknown the warning is hidden and it still looks arrived. Each packet is drawn with the shape of its protocol (`packetShape`). Only a firewall the player knows blocks: a packet that reaches no device, that no known firewall stopped and that no warning answered is lost. When a firewall the player does not know stopped it (`hiddenStop`), its path ends at the door of the device that has the firewall instead of in front of it, and the stamp is the lost one. The same flag is set for a ping a router out of sight dropped without a warning (01-10, before and after a traceroute): the path ends at the doorstep of the sender, never along the routers, and the stamp is the lost one and stays there. Nothing here decides whether a packet gets in.
- `still-trace.tsx`: `StillTraffic`, the layer of the still map (`g.map-traffic`), drawn by `Traffic` (`traffic.tsx`) only when `still` is true, over the pieces and under the HTML layer. It draws a `StillTrace` for each packet (`g.map-trace`, with `data-packet` and `data-end`): the line of its path (`path.map-trace__line`, ink; `--blocked` in the danger colour when a firewall the player knows stopped it; `--lost` dashed when it died on the way) and its shapes (`PacketGlyph`, `g.map-packet` with `data-role` `going`, `answer` or `appears`, and `data-shape`, plus `map-packet--intruder` for an intruder). The shape is `CatalogIcon` of the packet entity of the catalog (the icon of `@/scene/entities/icons/packet`, which it reuses by composition: the shape of the protocol, filled to ask and a ring to answer, a second border of the same shape for an intruder, a triangle with `!` for a warning), drawn in a box of 40 px (`GLYPH_BOX`). The stylesheet gives the color (`.map-packet` ink, `--intruder` danger, through `currentColor`) and the width of the stroke (`vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`, as the pieces), and takes the paper fill the pieces give the catalog icon off a packet (`.map-packet .map-piece__icon .entity-icon { fill: none }`), because the second border of an intruder is a path with no fill of its own and would paint paper over its shape. Over all the traces, in a layer of their own, `g.map-packet-tags` holds the labels (`PacketTag`) of the packets that have a place for one.
- `packet-tag.tsx`: `PacketTag`, the label of a packet with ports: `g.packet-tag` with a `rect` on its box and a `text` in the monospaced font of the map, 12 px (the font comes from the `.packet-tag text` rule of `src/styles.css`, and `.packet-tag` takes the pointer away from the label), whose `textLength` is the width the layout computes for the text (`labelWidth` less the padding). `textLength` fixes the width of the text whichever font draws the arrow `→`, which the font served for the map does not have: measured in Chromium on 2026-10-19, `getComputedTextLength()` still answers the natural width of the font (84.6 px in `serif`, 92.7 in `sans-serif` for `TCP 51514 → 80`) and the drawn width (`getStartPositionOfChar` to `getEndPositionOfChar`, `getBBox`) is the `textLength` (101 px) in every font. The group holds the text of the label and nothing else (no `title`), so `.packet-tag` reads as the label.
- `traffic.tsx`: `Traffic`, what `MapStage` draws in its `svg` for the packets: `StillTraffic` when `still` is true and, when it is not, the packets in flight (`g.map-flights`). `FlightLabels` is the other half of the flight: the labels of the packets with ports (`g.map-flight-tags`), for an `svg` of its own that `MapStage` draws after the HTML layer, so the labels fly over the stamps and the labels of the pieces (the spec: «la etiqueta sigue al paquete aunque pase sobre un sello»), while the packets go under them. That `svg` is `aria-hidden`, as large as the drawing and at its place, and `.map-stage__flight-labels` gives it `pointer-events: none` (the `.packet-tag` rule of `src/styles.css` gives the same to the labels), so every stamp, button and card under a label stays pressable and hoverable. Both halves have the same clock: two `svg` roots have two SMIL timelines, and measured in Chromium on 2026-10-19 the two that `MapStage` mounts together read the same `getCurrentTime()` (0 µs apart over five frames), so a label stays with its packet; a test that stops the clock stops both (`pauseAnimations()` and `setCurrentTime(t)` on each). Each live flow gets `flowCycles` copies and each send one `sendCycle`; a flow is keyed by its id and its copy and a send by its combination and its send number (`flightKeyOf`), so a new send number mounts a new group. The session keeps one run for each combination of chips, the last one, so a repeat would replace the packet still in flight: `useSendsInFlight` (`flight/use-sends-in-flight.ts`) keeps each send beside the newer ones of its combination until its own trip ends (`plan.duration` after it was first seen, on one timer), and `MapStage` calls it once (`useSendsInTheAir` in `traffic.tsx`: the hook with the layout, and nothing when animations are off, which also makes it forget what it kept) and hands the one list to `Traffic` and to `FlightLabels` as `inTheAir`, so a label stays with its packet and the two can never expire apart (`Traffic` keeps `sent` for the still map, which draws one trace for each combination). It remembers the run a send left with, not its path, which is worked out again at the layout of each render; keeps the flights in the order they first stood, so none moves in the DOM (a moved SMIL element would begin again) when another joins or goes; drops at once the flights of a combination that leaves the sent list (the world changed), and leaves the last send of a combination in the list after its trip, finished and invisible, as before. A packet that starts at a piece the map does not draw is not drawn.
- `flight/` holds the flight of a packet: the times (`flight-plan.ts`), the SMIL elements (`flight.tsx`) and the list of sends in the air (`use-sends-in-flight.ts`), with their own guide.
- Tests: `still-trace.test.tsx` and `packet-tag.test.tsx` (jsdom: what is drawn, where, with which classes), `packet-tag.browser.test.tsx` (Chromium: the width in several fonts, the place of the rectangle and the text, the font and colors) and `flow-look.test.ts` (pure). The colors, the dashes and the paper fill of the shapes need the stylesheet, so they are checked in `../map-stage.browser.test.tsx`; the flight has its tests in `flight/`.
- Not here: where a path, a shape or a label stands, and which label is left out (`../marks/still-packets.ts`, `../marks/packet-tags.ts`), the stamps (`../overlay/`), the pieces (`../entities/`), the shape of a packet or a stamp as an icon (`../entities/icons/`), anything that decides whether a packet moves or where it ends (`src/engine/`, run by `src/game/`).
- May import: `@/copy`, only types from `@/engine`, the icon of the catalog through `../entities/device.tsx` (`CatalogIcon`) and other files of `src/scene/` (`@/scene/<folder>/<file>`).
