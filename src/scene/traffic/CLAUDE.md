# scene/traffic

The look of a packet: `flow-look.ts` turns each packet's result (`intruder`, `reached`, `replied`, `blocked`, `warning`) into its look (its message, whether it is an intruder, how its run ends, `PacketEnd`) and the state of its stamp. The 2D map draws the packets and their stamps with it (`../map2d/traffic/`, `../map2d/marks/`).

- A packet that ends in a warning the player knows (`warning`, which `src/game/` leaves empty while the term of the warning is unknown) looks like that warning and leaves the stamp of a warning: `warned` when a router sent it, so it travels back along the path from that router, and `self-warned` when the sender's own device did, so it does not travel and the path ends at that device's doorstep. A datagram that reached its host and got a Port Unreachable from it (`warning.by` is `host`) is not an arrival: `flowLook` checks the warning before `reached`, so the packet goes to the host, the warning comes back from it along the same path (`warned`) and the stamp is the warning's, because no program received the datagram; with the term unknown the warning is hidden and it still looks arrived. Each packet is drawn with the shape of its protocol (`packetShape`).
- Only a firewall the player knows blocks: a packet that reaches no device, that no known firewall stopped and that no warning answered is lost. When a firewall the player does not know stopped it (`hiddenStop`), its path ends at the door of the device that has the firewall instead of in front of it, and the stamp is the lost one. The same flag is set for a ping a router out of sight dropped without a warning (01-10, before and after a traceroute): the path ends at the doorstep of the sender, never along the routers, and the stamp is the lost one and stays there. Nothing here decides whether a packet gets in.
- Not here: deciding whether a packet gets in (`src/engine/`, run by `src/game/`), the shape of a packet or a stamp (`src/scene/entities/icons/`), piece labels (`src/scene/overlay/`).
- May import: `@/copy`, `@/levels`, only types from `@/engine`, and other files of `src/scene/` (`@/scene/<file>`).
