# scene/traffic

Traffic: each live flow sends a packet every few seconds along the path of its trace, each packet the player sent travels once per send, and every one leaves a stamp at the end of its path, all drawn with the catalog (`@/scene/entities`). With "Sin animaciones" each path is drawn still. The stamps of flows and sent packets are spread together.

- The clock lives in `use-packet-runs.ts`: a live flow leaves on its schedule, a sent packet once for each new send number. `flow-look.ts` turns each packet's result (`intruder`, `reached`, `replied`, `blocked`, `warning`) into its look (its message, whether it is an intruder, how its run ends) and the state of its stamp. A packet that ends in a warning the player knows (`warning`, which `src/game/` leaves empty while the term of the warning is unknown) looks like that warning and leaves the stamp of a warning: `warned` when a router sent it, so it travels back along the path from that router, and `self-warned` when the sender's own device did, so it does not travel and `pathFor` ends the path at that device's doorstep. A datagram that reached its host and got a Port Unreachable from it (`warning.by` is `host`) is not an arrival: `flowLook` checks the warning before `reached`, so the packet goes to the host, the warning comes back from it along the same path (`warned`) and the stamp is the warning's, because no program received the datagram; with the term unknown the warning is hidden and it still looks arrived. Each packet is drawn with the shape of its protocol (`packetShape`) and a packet with ports carries the labels of its legs (`MapFlow.ports`, from `src/game/`): `traffic.tsx` finds where along the path the router that translates them stands (`translationDistance`) and the packet wears the label of its leg (`tagAt`), also on the way back. Only a firewall the player knows blocks: a packet that reaches no device, that no known firewall stopped and that no warning answered is lost. When a firewall the player does not know stopped it (`hiddenStop`), `pathFor` ends its path at the door of the device that has the firewall instead of in front of it, and the stamp is the lost one. The same flag is set for a ping a router out of sight dropped without a warning (01-10, before and after a traceroute): `pathFor` ends the path at the doorstep of the sender, never along the routers, and the stamp is the lost one and stays there. Nothing here decides whether a packet gets in.
- Not here: deciding whether a packet gets in (`src/engine/`, run by `src/game/`), the shape of a packet or a stamp (`src/scene/entities/`), piece labels and the task card anchor (`src/scene/overlay/`).
- May import: `@/copy`, `@/levels`, only types from `@/engine`, and other files of `src/scene/` (`@/scene/<file>`, `@/scene/entities`).
