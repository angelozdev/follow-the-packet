# scene/layout

Where things go on the 2D map, as pure functions: no React, no DOM, no clock, no randomness. The same input gives the same boxes. `metrics.ts` measures labels, `graph.ts` is the graph of links and `stations.ts` names where a packet stops; the layout of the map builds on the three (`map-layout.ts` and `arrange/`).

- `metrics.ts` measures labels. Every label of the map is monospaced, so its width is its characters and not a table of glyphs: `ceil(characters × CELL) + LABEL_PAD`, with `CELL` 7.2 px (0.6 em at 12 px) and `LABEL_PAD` 14 px (6 px of padding and 1 px of border on each side). `LOCK_WIDTH` (22 px) and `ALERT_WIDTH` (23 px) are added when the label wears the lock or the alert. Task M21 measured them in Chromium on 2026-10-20 with the fallback monospaced font of the browser of the tests (the lock 21.6 px: a space and the 14.4 px icon; the alert 22.2 px: the 15 px badge and a space) and rounded each up to the whole pixel that keeps the drawn label within the model: 26 and 22 had been derived from the CSS and not measured, and the lock was 4.4 px under the drawing. In a label of two lines the alert goes before the first line and the lock after the last, so the box is as wide as the widest line with what it wears (`labelOf` in `arrange/pieces.ts`). Widths are whole pixels rounded up, so the model errs wide against the cell: `label-width.browser.test.tsx` (in `src/ui/incident/geometry/`) compares the real width of every label of every registered incident, with the lock and the alert in every combination, with the model: the drawn label is never more than half a pixel over it (the fallback font advances 0.602 em, not 0.6) and at most 2 px under it. `characterCount` counts what the player sees: an arrow takes one cell, and so does an accented letter, composed or not.
- `labelLines` splits the label of a firewall into the name of its term and the name of its resource; `labelBox` gives the box of those lines: as wide as the longer one, 20 px tall for one line (`LABEL_HEIGHT`) and 16 px more for each other (`LINE_HEIGHT`), so 36 px for two. `MAX_NAME_LENGTH` (32) is the longest name a world may have, which makes a label of 245 px; a test walks every registered world.
- Where the 0.6 em comes from (read on 2026-10-12, spec «El ancho de las etiquetas»): the file of IBM Plex Mono v20 that Google Fonts serves for `index.html` (subset `latin`, `https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/ibmplexmono/v20/-F63fjptAgt5VM-kVkqdyU8n1i8q131nj-o.woff2`), read with `fontTools` 4.63.0: `unitsPerEm` 1000, `isFixedPitch` 1, and 221 of its 229 glyphs advance 600 (the other 8 are combining marks, which advance 0). The same tool read Menlo (`/System/Library/Fonts/Menlo.ttc`), which advances 0.602 em, and SF Mono (`SFNSMono.ttf`, the font of `ui-monospace` on macOS), which advances 0.618 em. That is why the map's font stack leaves `ui-monospace` out (Task M11), and why a browser test compares this model with the DOM.
- The arrow `→` (U+2192) is not in the file that is served, so the browser draws it with another font of unknown width; the label of a packet fixes its width with `textLength` in SVG (Task M13), and here the arrow counts as one cell.
- `graph.ts` is the graph of the map: `Graph` has nodes (a station name and its point) and edges (two station names and, optionally, the `bends` where the drawn link turns, listed from `from` to `to`; a link can be walked either way). `route(graph, stations)` completes the legs between the stations it is given with the shortest way along the links (Dijkstra; a bend counts in the length of its link) and returns the points, the bends included. Between two ways of the same length the one that goes through the node that comes first in `nodes` wins, at every link of the way, whatever the order of the edges; so the same graph always gives the same way. It throws when a station is not in the graph or when no links join two of them. `lengthOf` and `positionAt` measure and walk the points `route` returns. `Point` is this folder's own type.
- `stations.ts` is `stationsFor`, the 2D replacement of `pathFor`: the stations a packet stops at, as names and not coordinates (`startsOnMap` replaces the one of `layout.ts`). It takes `Drawn`, what the map draws by id (the layout builds it): each drawn device with the id of its bus (a floor, a subnet, or one name the layout gives the bus of an office with neither), the clients and the servers, the routers along the main line from the office outward, the gateways of another office (off that line), `edge` (the lone edge of a world with no routers) and `internet`. It reads the `Trace` for every outcome and decides none. It names a station only when the map draws a piece for it, and never twice in a row. `station` holds the builders of the names, so the layout names its nodes with the same code; the tests spell the names out. The vocabulary (spec «Piezas, enlaces y estaciones», plus what the plan did not name):

  | Station | Where | Says |
  |---|---|---|
  | `door:<id>` | the edge of a device, a server or a client that faces its link | it arrived at the piece, or it starts there |
  | `doorstep:<device>` | 62 px from the edge, on the link | it never left the device |
  | `firewall:<device>` | the bar | a firewall the player knows stopped it |
  | `road:<device>` | where the link meets the bus | it was lost in the road of its network |
  | `bus-end:<bus>` | a stub of 40 px past the last device of that bus | nobody has the IP (a device sending in a VPC) |
  | `router:<id>`, `internet` | the icon | it died at that router or in the internet |
  | `edge` | the lone edge | it crossed the edge of a world with no routers |
  | `mid:<a>-<b>` | the middle of the link between two stops of the main line | a router the map does not draw dropped it |
  | `line-end` | a stub past the last stop of the main line | only inside a `mid`: it died past everything drawn |

  `mid`: the stops of the main line, in order, are `edge`, `router:<id>` for each router, `internet` and `door:<server>` for each server. `a` is the last stop the packet crossed and `b` the next one; from the road of the sender, `a` is `bus:<bus>` (where the trunk of its bus leaves it) and `b` the first stop; with nothing drawn beyond, `b` is `line-end`. A packet that crossed something off the line (a client, a gateway of another office) has no `mid` and ends there. The layout must give a node to every `mid` it can need, `bus:<bus>` and `line-end` included.
- `map-layout.ts` is `layoutMap(world, pieces, { width })`, the layout of the map: the first of the row (`NORMAL`), the compact row (`COMPACT`) and the snake (`layoutStack`) whose natural width fits the width it is given, and the `mode` that says which (`row`, `row-compact` or `stack`). A width under 360 px counts as 360. With `blockTab` it leaves 18 px over the first row of each floor, and over each outline of a block that starts lower, for the tab of an outline (the floor grows by 18 px for each, and what stands under it moves down); no width changes. Without it the layout is the one it has always been, whatever the pieces mark `insideBlock`. The room is asked for by the caller and not found by the layout, on purpose: the layout of a world with an outline must keep the place of everything else (`a world drawn whole keeps the size and the place of everything else`), and the caller knows whether the incident has a block to name. The room over the first row of every floor does not wait for an outline, so the rows do not jump when the player places a device inside the block; an outline that starts below the first row of its floor does move what is under it when it appears (no registered incident has one: in 02-04 the owner is the first row of its floor and the other item is alone on its floor). The row modes keep their natural width, which can be less than the width given (the drawing is centered in its box); the snake is as wide as the width. The mode flips only where the natural width of the row is: at the width of the compact row and at the width of the normal one. It reads no state of the game.
- `arrange/` holds the layout of the map built on the three (its own guide): `layoutRow` puts the pieces in a row, with the boxes of the groups, the links, the nodes of the graph that `route` walks, the points of the stations, the marks where the stamps stand and the boxes of the labels and the icons. `layoutStack` is the same map as a snake. Both give a node to every station that `stationsFor` can name, and `mainLine` (exported by `stations.ts`) lists the stops its `mid` nodes join.
- `captions.ts` is `captionBoxes(groups)`: the box of the name of every group that has one, in the top left corner of the group, 14 px (`GROUP_PAD`) from both edges, in the strip of 18 px (`CAPTION`) that the group keeps over its first row, as wide as every part of its name joined by a space takes at 10 px a character (`CAPTION_CELL`). `captionWidth(name)` gives that width for the name of one group, and the snake reads it, so its bus never stands where a box would end before its name does. The width is a ceiling and not a measure: the caption is bold capitals in the body font with a tracking of 0.06 em, whose advance the browser decides, and the real text of the names (Task M11) is not here; `captionBoxes` takes all the parts, known or not, so the box is as wide as the name can ever be. A caller that checks overlaps or places labels adds the boxes to the layout (`layoutMap` does not hold them). A group with no name, the outline of a block, has none; the name of the outline is the tab, which `blockTabBoxes(groups, name)` places: the box over the top left corner of each outline, 18 px tall (`BLOCK_TAB`) and as wide as the name is as a label, standing in the room that `layoutMap` leaves for it when it is given `blockTab`. The spec places the name «arriba a la izquierda» of its box; the numbers are Task M8's.
- A packet whose sender is not drawn has no stations; `startsOnMap` says so first. A packet from the internet to a public IP that no router owns ends in the internet, since no bus is its own.
- Not here: drawing (React and SVG), measuring the DOM (a browser test does that), the Spanish text of a label (`src/copy/`), where a card opens (`cards/`).
- May import: `@/copy`, only types from `@/engine`, and other files of `src/scene/`. Its tests also read `@/engine` (to run `simulate`), `@/levels` and `@/testing`.
