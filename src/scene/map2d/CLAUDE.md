# scene/map2d

The 2D (SVG) map of the game, built beside the 3D one until the 3D is deleted. It lives here, and not in `src/scene/`, because that folder has 11 of its 12 files and the flag of Task M9 takes the last one; when `three` goes (Task M24 of `docs/plans/2026-10-12-follow-the-packet-2d-map.md`) its sub-folders move up into `src/scene/` with the same names. The design is in `docs/specs/2026-10-12-follow-the-packet-2d-map-design.md`.

- Sub-folders, each with its own guide: `layout/` (pure functions that decide where everything goes, in pixels), `marks/` (pure functions that decide what stands on the layout: the fan of stamps with its «+N», the labels of packets, the pairs of boxes that overlap and the reading order of the buttons), `cards/` (pure functions that decide where the card beside a piece opens and how it sits in the layer), `entities/` (what is drawn in SVG: the networks as the boxes of the groups, the pieces, the links and the dots of the marks, and the buttons of the stamps), `overlay/` (what stands on the HTML layer: the names of the groups, the name of the block, the labels of the pieces, the stamps and the card), `traffic/` (what is drawn in SVG of the packets: their traces, their flight and their labels) and `catalog/` (the canvas of the `/entidades` page: every piece, packet and stamp in every variant and state, in a grid of 5 columns; Task M16).
- `map-stage.tsx` is `MapStage`, the map in its box. It measures its sheet with `useElementSize`, draws nothing until the sheet has a width above 0 (so `layoutMap` is never called with 0 or `NaN`, and `NoRoomError`, which only a width that no registered world reaches can raise, is not caught), then lays the map out at that width and draws it at one unit to the pixel: an `svg` (`aria-hidden`, `viewBox` `0 0 <width> <height>` of the layout, `.map-stage__svg`) and the HTML layer (`.html-layer`, the layer that already existed, `overflow: clip`) over the whole sheet. In the svg: the boxes of the groups (with a filled tab on each outline of a block once `cidr` is known and the incident has a block to name), the links, the dots of the marks, the pieces and the packets (`Traffic`, `traffic/traffic.tsx`, with the fans the stage already computed: when `still` is true the traces and the labels of the packets, `StillTraffic` from `stillPackets(runs, layout, fans)`; with animations on the packets in flight, drawn with SMIL); on the layer: the names of the groups, the name of the block on its tab, the label of every piece, the stamps and, last, the card when the screen gives one (`overlay`) and `activePieceId` names a piece the layout holds an icon box for (`CardAnchor`, `overlay/card-anchor.tsx`); and, after the layer and only with animations on, a second `svg` (`.map-stage__flight-labels`, `aria-hidden`, absolutely placed at the frame of the drawing, `pointer-events: none`) with the labels that fly beside the packets (`FlightLabels`), so they pass over the stamps without taking a click from them. The stage computes the fans once (`fanStamps(stampsByPlace([...flows, ...sent], layout.drawn), layout.marks)`: the live flows first and then what the player sent) and hands them to `MapLabels`, which walks the reading order of the map with the labels and the stamps in it, and to `stillPackets` (`placeTags` takes the fans). When the incident has a block to name (`blockName` is not null) the layout leaves 18 px over the first row of each floor, and over each outline that starts lower, for the tab (`layoutMap` with `blockTab`), whatever the player knows and whatever the player has placed inside the block, so the size of the map depends neither on the terms nor on the outline: the room is there before `cidr` is known and before the outline appears, and an outline that starts on the first row of a floor, which is the case of the only incident with a block (02-04), moves nothing when it appears. `frame.ts` (`frameOf`) says where the drawing stands in the sheet: centered to a whole pixel when the sheet is wider than the drawing, against the left edge when it is not (below 360 px the drawing keeps 360 px and the stage scrolls sideways), and `TITLE_BAR` (56 px) from the top, under the bar that floats over the map. Whoever puts something on the layer adds that frame to the pixels of the layout (the names of the groups, the labels, the stamps and the card do).
- The box of the stage is the sheet. Above 900 px `.incident--map2d .map-stage` fills `.incident__map` and scrolls down when the drawing is taller than the window (the title bar belongs to `.incident__map`, so it stays); the sheet is at least as tall as the box, so the layer covers the whole box. At 900 px and under the row of the map is `auto` and its column `minmax(0, 1fr)` (`.incident--map2d`, set by `incident-screen.tsx` when `MAP_RENDERER` is `2d`) and the stage is as tall as its drawing, `TITLE_BAR` plus `size.height`, so the page scrolls. The column must be `minmax(0, 1fr)`: a plain `1fr` cannot be narrower than the svg inside it, so the sheet would never see a smaller width and the map would not shrink when the window narrows (below 360 px the stage, not the page, is what scrolls sideways).
- The card (Task M15). `MapStage` draws it inside the layer, so it scrolls with the drawing, and gives it what it needs: the frame, the layer (the width of the sheet and, as its height, the larger of the height that `useElementSize` measured for the sheet and `TITLE_BAR` plus `size.height` plus the room under the drawing) and the height of the window (`useViewportSize`). While a card is open the svg leaves `ROOM_UNDER_THE_DRAWING` (222 px: 200 of card, the margin of 6 and the 16 of `GROUND_REACH`) under itself as its `margin-bottom`, so the sheet and the layer, which covers it, are 222 px taller than the drawing and a docked card finds its 200 px under the lowest piece whether the stage scrolls (above 900 px) or the page does (900 px and under); with no card the stage ends at the drawing, as before, so nothing moves until a card opens. The card is never taller than the window allows (`cardSlot` caps `--card-room` with `sideHeight` of the window height, and its body scrolls) and `CardAnchor` brings it into view when it opens and when another piece opens (`useRevealCard`: `scrollIntoView` with `block: 'nearest'`, and the title bar and the dock margin as scroll margins), which scrolls the stage and the page and never the layer, which clips, and puts them back where they were when the card goes. Wherever the stage scrolls, a piece low in a drawing taller than the window and a card docked under it are brought into view by that scroll, not by the placement: the placement works in the pixels of the layer. The card is `.map-card`, absolutely placed in the layer at its anchor with `z-index: 30` (over the labels, the stamps and the labels that fly, under the inspection box, 35, and the title bar, 40), and it wears the CSS variables of the 3D card. The stylesheet keeps what the stage styles from reaching what a card holds: the names of the groups are the direct children of the layer (`.html-layer > .caption`, not every `.caption` of the stage, which would take the `.caption` of the trays of a card out of its flow), and a card, as everything inside `.map-stage`, reads the map's `--font-mono` (no `ui-monospace`), which is the face of the page whenever IBM Plex Mono loads.
- `pieces.tsx` is the choice of the piece for a `MapPiece`: `PieceShape` draws one piece (device, firewall, edge, internet or actor, with its variant and the state the game gives it) in the box the layout holds for it, inside a `g.map-piece` that is `aria-hidden` and wears its kind and variant (and its id), and `Pieces` draws every piece the layout holds a box for (`layout.icons`), the one whose card is open selected. Its tests are `pieces.test.tsx` (jsdom: the catalog has a piece in every variant, no two variants draw the same shapes, the antennas, hidden from screen readers, the layer, the boxes of the networks) and `pieces.browser.test.tsx` (the states, which need real geometry, CSS and animation clock). `MapStage` takes `MapPiece` (with its variant) and draws `Pieces` over the links and under the layer; the boxes of the networks are drawn through `GroupBoxes`.
- `game-scene.tsx` is `GameScene2D`, what `src/scene/index.ts` chooses when `MAP_RENDERER` (`../renderer.ts`, from the build flag `VITE_MAP_RENDERER`, `3d` unless it is `2d`) says `2d`. It takes the props of the 3D scene and, besides, `known` (the terms the player knows, which `incident-screen.tsx` passes from the session; none known when absent), because the name of a group shows only what the player has met and `GameSceneProps` carries no terms. From the props it draws `world`, `pieces` (with the label of each), `blockName`, `activePieceId` and `still` (the piece whose card is open is selected, and animations are off when asked), and passes `inspectTrigger` and `onActivate` to the labels; `flows` and `sent` are the packets whose stamps it draws and whose traces and labels (when `still`) or flight (when not) it draws, and `inspectStampsTrigger` (required, as the stage takes every prop) is what inspects the list of a «+N»: it takes the ids of the packets of the place and `incident-screen.tsx` makes it `trigger(PIECE_ID.stamps(ids))`, since `scene` cannot import `@/game`; `overlay` is the card the screen opens beside its piece, which it hands to the stage.
- The 3D files (`game-scene.tsx`, `map-canvas.tsx`, `road.tsx`, `ink.tsx`, `layout.ts` and the folders `entities/`, `traffic/`, `overlay/` and `catalog/` of `src/scene/`) do not change while this folder exists, except the pure files a task of the plan names.
- Draws what the game computed, like the rest of `src/scene/`: it never decides an outcome.
- Not here: panels and screens (`src/ui/`), the Spanish text (`src/copy/`), anything that moves a packet (`src/engine/`).
- May import: `@/copy`, `@/levels`, `@/design`, only types from `@/engine`, and other files of `src/scene/` (`@/scene/<file>`, `@/scene/map2d/<folder>`).
