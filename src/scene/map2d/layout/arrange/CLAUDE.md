# scene/map2d/layout/arrange

How the pieces of the 2D map are arranged, as pure functions: no React, no DOM, no clock, no randomness. `layoutRow(world, pieces, density)` is the map in a row, left to right, and `layoutStack(world, pieces, width)` the same map as a snake for a width where no row fits; `layoutMap` (one folder up) picks one of the two by what fits. They never read what the player sent (no stamps, no packets): the size of the map depends on the pieces (and, for the snake, on the width) only. The design is in the spec (`docs/specs/2026-10-12-follow-the-packet-2d-map-design.md`, «La fila», «Piezas, enlaces y estaciones», «Los sellos»).

- `geometry.ts`: the types of the layout (`RowPiece`, `Group`, `Link`, `Mark`, `RowLayout`, and `MapLayout`, a `RowLayout` that says its `MapMode`), `NORMAL` and `COMPACT` (the only two numbers that change with the density: the trunk, 44 or 32 px, which only moves the head where the trunks join, and the lane, 66 or 52 px, which is the only thing that makes the compact row narrower), and every measure with its name. `RowPiece` is the structural type of what the game gives the scene (`MapPiece` is assignable to it): kind, anchor, label, lock, state, `inOffice` and `insideBlock`.
- `pieces.ts`: `classify` says what the pieces stand for in the world (devices that hang from a bus, servers, clients, the routers along the main line from the office outward, the gateways of another office, the lone edge, the internet); `drawnOf` builds the `Drawn` that `stationsFor` reads; `labelOf` gives the lines and the box of a label, with the alert before the first line and the lock after the last, as in the button; `busOf` names the bus of a device (its subnet, its floor, or the kind of the world).
- `groups.ts`: floors, rows, buses and trunks. `floorsOf` lists the floors that have a drawn device, with the parts of their names (the name of the LAN, and its block, which declares the term `cidr`: `shownParts` leaves a part out until its term is known, and the text itself, «Oficina», «VPC», the separator, comes from `src/copy/` in Task M11). `stackFloors` puts the boxes one under the other (a box per floor, 14 px apart; the box of the office or the VPC around them from two floors, and around any subnet), a row of 100 px for each plugged device (or the `row` extent the caller gives: how far a row reaches over and under its link, which the snake uses), the bus of each floor, the outline of each run of rows marked `insideBlock` and, under all the boxes, the devices with no cable. `trunkLane` and `trunkOf` give the trunks of several floors: parallel, 8 px apart, centered on the main line, joining at the head.
- `assemble.ts`: turns the roads of the map (lists of waypoints, named or not) into the graph that `route` walks, adds the middle of each pair of stops (`mid`) by splitting the link it falls on at half its length, and places the marks. `graphOf` gives the `Graph` of a layout.
- `row.ts`: `layoutRow`, which builds the roads and the marks and hands them to `assemble`; it also exports the pieces `stack.ts` builds on (`surveyOf`, `placeDevices` and `placeBus` with their label placement, `placeLanes` with its side, `joinGateway`, `stopsNamed`, `legsOf`).
- `stack.ts`: `layoutStack`, the snake (see «The snake»).

## The row

Left to right, measured in px from the left edge of the drawing (`OUTER`, 16 px of margin on the four sides):

- **A device** is a row of 100 px: its label (right-aligned, 6 px from the icon), its icon of 48 px, and its link to the bus of its floor. All icons stand in one column, all buses at one `x`, and the link has the same length for every device: 102 px (the doorstep at 62 px plus the 40 px between marks) when no firewall is drawn, and, with a firewall, enough for the bar (91 px from the edge, its door side at 84), its label (under the link, past the bar, 10 px under it) and a stamp. The label of a firewall is two lines (`labelOf`); the bar is 14 px by 40 px (`BAR_THICKNESS` 14, from 6 by the student's ruling of 2026-10-19, so that the joints of the wall and the passage between the posts of the pieces of `../../entities/` fit: the door side stays at 84 px, the other side moves 8 px, and what derives from the thickness moves with it: `BAR_AT` (91) and `BAR_MARK_BACK` (29) by 4 px, `FIREWALL_MARK_AT` (120) and `FIREWALL_LABEL_AT` (142) by 8 px).
- **Stations of a device**: `door` (the edge of the icon), `doorstep` (62 px from it), `firewall` (the bar), `road` (where the link meets the bus). Every device has the four, with its bar drawn or not (`hiddenStop` still names the firewall of a device). The bus is a vertical line through the junctions of its rows; `bus-end` is a stub of 40 px past the last one; `bus` is the point where its trunk leaves it (the point of the bus nearest to its lane on the main line).
- **The main line** is horizontal, through the center of the rows (the middle between the first row and the last): the trunks join at the head, 44 or 32 px from the bus (they run parallel, 8 px apart, from an elbow 8 px from the bus), and a plain line goes on from the head to the first stop, whose center stands 172 px (`STOP_PITCH`) from the bus whatever the density; then the stops in order (the lone edge, or the routers from the office outward, then the internet), each an icon of 48 px with its label above it, centered, its bottom edge 47 px above the line (`STOP_LABEL_RISE`: clear of the 3-casilla fans, which reach 43 px). The first router (or the lone edge) stands on the right border of the box of the office: it is inside and outside of it. The distance between two stops is at least `STOP_PITCH` and never lets two labels touch (the label of the first stop starts 47 px from the bus, clear of the fans of the roads and of the end of the bus). Why 172: the mark of a stop stands 22 px before its icon (46 px before its center), and the middle of the link that `mid` names (where a packet ends when a router the map does not draw dropped it) must stand 40 px from it; the same holds for the first leg, from the bus (where the mark of `road` stands) to the first stop.
- **Servers and clients** are lanes of 100 px on the right of a vertical bus (`join:servers`), centered on the main line, servers first and then clients, in the order of the world: a lane of 66 or 52 px from the bus to the door of the icon, and the label 6 px to the right of it. The bus is 72 px (`SERVERS_GAP`) from the last stop, or from the end of its label if that is farther, and 72 px from the bus of the office when there is no stop. `line-end` hangs 92 px below the last lane; with no lanes it is the end of a stub of 92 px past the last stop; with nothing on the line at all, the trunks end at it. It is drawn on every map (`kind: 'stub'`): a packet dropped past the last thing drawn travels a drawn link.
- **A device with no cable** (`connected: false`) stands under the boxes, in the column of the rest, with its short cable that reaches no bus (`kind: 'unplugged'`). Its station names are the same.
- **The other office** (`gateways`, the routers after the first that face a floor the map draws) stands in a second group under the office: the boxes of the floors it faces, with their own bus, and the router at the right of that bus, on the same `x` as the first router, joined to it by the private link (`kind: 'private'`, vertical). The main line does not move for it.

## The snake

`layoutStack(world, pieces, width)` is as wide as the width it is given, 360 px at least (`MIN_WIDTH`; `layoutMap` counts a narrower width as 360), and wider only when a name is too long for its lane at that width. The name of 30 characters (`servidor-autoritativo-noticias`) fits 360 px as the spec says, `24 + 48 + 44 + 6 + 230 + 8 = 360` (`SNAKE_BUS_WALL`, the icon, `SNAKE_LANE`, the gap of the label, the label of 230 px and `SNAKE_LABEL_WALL`), when it is drawn without a lock, as the game draws a server of names (it opens a card); with the lock (256 px, the worst case of the tests) it needs 392 px. It draws the same stations, marks, pieces and groups as the row (a test compares them), keeps the three limits above and adds none. Measures are in `geometry.ts` and `stack.ts`; the numbers below are the ones they give.

- **The office** hangs to the left of a bus at the right of the map: the rows of the row, with the name of the device over its icon (left-aligned with it, 47 px over its link, clear of the fans) and a link as long as the width leaves, 102 px at least (more for a long name, which stays inside the outline of the block, and for a firewall: 160 px, the bar mark at 120 plus 40). A row is 118 px tall (71 px over the link, 67 for the name and 4 of room for the outline of the block, and 47 under it) and 158 px when the device has a firewall, whose label stands 47 px under the link, centered under the bar and kept inside the outline of the block. The boxes, the outline of the block and the devices with no cable are those of the row, with these rows.
- **The trunks** leave the bus at its last row, run right to a channel outside every box, run down it to the line and turn left to join at the head: with two floors they are parallel and 8 px apart, the topmost floor on the outside (its drop farthest right and its lane lowest), so none crosses another. The last drop stands 43 px from the right wall, so the fan of a mark on it fits. The line stands low enough under the office for the middle of the first leg (`mid:bus:<bus>-<stop>`) to stay clear of the corner where the trunk meets the line, of the stub of the bus and of the rows with no cable.
- **The line** runs from right to left under the office, 114 px (`LINE_PITCH`) apart from the next line: the name of a stop stands under its icon, 47 px from the line (not over it as in the row: the first router does not stand on the border of the office, and a gateway comes down from above). A stop goes in the first place that keeps the 172 px of `STOP_PITCH` along the line, 8 px between labels, its label inside the map, its mark (46 px behind it) on the same stretch and the middle of its leg on a straight stretch, 22 px from a corner; when the line has no such place, it turns down at the margin and goes on in the next line, which runs the other way. A line can stay with no stop when the next one does not fit in the first stretch after the turn (the middle of its leg would fall in the corner): it goes in the line after, and the empty one is only a long link.
- **Servers and clients** hang from a bus at the end of the line, the first lane 114 px under the line and the others 100 px apart, a lane of 44 px (`SNAKE_LANE`, the spec's; the row's lanes are 66 and 52) from the bus to the door, toward the inside of the map: to the right of the bus when the line runs right to left, and to the left, with the name on the left of the icon, when it runs left to right. The bus stands where the 72 px after the last stop and the room for the longest name allow: 24 px at least from the wall at the end of the line (`SNAKE_BUS_WALL`, so the fan of one casilla on it stands 6 px inside the map) and the far end of the longest name 8 px at least from the other wall (`SNAKE_LABEL_WALL`), on whichever side the lanes hang; so a name can stand 8 px from the wall, where the rest of the map keeps 16. With no lanes, `line-end` is the end of a stub of 92 px past the last stop, along the line.
- **The other office** stands under the first one, with its own bus, and its gateway on a row 38 px under its boxes, above the first router, joined to it by the private link (114 px, vertical); the name of the gateway stands on its left. The first router keeps far enough from the trunk of that office for the middle of the leg from its bus (which goes through the gateway) to clear the icon of the gateway.
- **Spec figures that do not hold.** 01-08 is 1049 px in a row (spec 845), and the snake is the usual mode at 1280 px (a map of 900).

## The marks

A mark is where the stamps of a place stand: `{ station, point, axis, casillas }`. `point` is where the stamp of the middle casilla is; `axis` is the axis along which the fan opens, across the link (`y` on a link that runs left to right, `x` on a bus end); `casillas` is how many stamps of 36 px (25 px apart) fit, at most 3: it drops when a label or an icon, or the fan of a mark placed before it, is in the way. Every stop of the line and the middle between two stops hold 3 (a test fixes it in nine maps); the marks that hold less are listed under «Known limits». Where a mark stands:

- A place that is a piece or a bar (`door:<id>`, `router:<id>`, `internet`, `edge`, `firewall:<id>`): 22 px before the edge of the piece, on the side of its link that faces the bus or the office; for the bar, 22 px before its edge toward the bus (where a packet that reaches the device comes from).
- A place that is a point of a link (`doorstep`, `road`, `bus-end`, `mid`, `line-end`): on that point.
- Two marks of one link are at least 40 px apart, and the fan of one never covers a label or an icon, nor the fan of another: the tests sweep nine maps in both densities (and, for the snake, ten maps at seven widths). The exceptions are listed under «Known limits».

## Known limits

The spec's numbers do not all fit together; these are the places where the tests do not hold, and why. Each is exempted by name in `row.test.ts`, and nothing else is.

- **Two buses that share a trunk** (the subnets of a VPC, the other office): `mid:bus:<bus>-<stop>` of each stands on its own lane of the trunk, 8 px from the other, and the vertical bend of the trunk of a floor above or below the main line runs 8 px from the bus. Their marks come within 40 px of each other and of `road`, and their fans overlap. With one bus the leg is clear.
- **`bus-end`** (a stub of 40 px past the last row) touches the fan of `road:<id>` of the last row: a fan is 86 px long and a stub 40.
- **The bus of the lanes**: the lane leaves 44 px (30 in the compact row, 22 in the snake, whose lane is 44) from the bus to the mark of its door, less than a fan. The marks that lie on that bus (`mid:<stop>-<server>`, `mid:<stop>-line-end`, `mid:<server>-<server>`, `mid:<server>-line-end`, and `line-end` of a map with lanes, which hangs 92 px under the last lane) can hold 1 casilla and their fans can touch the one of the door of a lane. They are the marks of a packet that an undrawn router dropped past the last stop, or that a server sent.
- **The natural width** is what these measures give, not what the prototype of the spec measured. 01-08 is 1049 px wide in a row and 1035 in the compact row (spec: 845 and 819). With the labels of the spec, which carry no lock, it is 997 and 983 (the locks are 52 and 56 px: `LOCK_WIDTH` of `metrics.ts`, which Task M21 measures); with the first leg and the distance between stops that come from the labels alone and not from `STOP_PITCH`, 859 and 845 (the 172 px floor costs 138 px there). The 14 and 26 px that are left are not explained by anything in the repository. At 1280 px (a map of 900 px) 01-08 does not fit in a row, nor in the compact one, and `layoutMap` gives it the snake.
- **Five lanes** (the names of chapter 2 with every router drawn: three routers and five lanes) put `mid:internet-door:<server>` 28 px (normal row) or 35 px (compact row) from `mid:door:<server>-door:<next>` on the bus of the lanes, under the 40 px of «Two marks of one link». No test of the row covers that map and no registered incident has five lanes; the snake keeps them apart.

Not here: where the stamps go once there are some (`marks/`, Task M6), the choice of mode (`../map-layout.ts`), the Spanish text of a group, a label or a card (`src/copy/`), drawing. May import: `@/copy` (types and `labelLines` through `metrics.ts`), `@/engine` (types only) and `@/scene/map2d/layout/<file>`. Its tests also read `@/engine`, `@/levels` and `@/testing`.
