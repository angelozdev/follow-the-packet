# scene

The isometric paper-and-ink map drawn with React Three Fiber: the entity catalog (`entities/`), where each piece sits (`layout.ts`: it gives a spot only to the pieces it is given; one column per subnet, or per floor of an office that declares them, with a slab and a label for each floor that has a piece drawn (the name floats just beyond the front edge of its slab, clear of the label of the firewall of a device that stands alone on the floor), or a single row for an office with one local network; a device with no cable stands past the last slab; and, while the map shows anything outside the office, the routers one after another along the road to the left, then the internet, then the servers (those of names too, side by side along the road beyond the globe) and the actors), the outline of the block (`Layout.blocks`: one dashed rectangle around each run of device spots, side by side in the row, that the pieces mark `insideBlock`, so an unmarked piece between two marked ones splits the run and no outline ever surrounds a piece that is not inside; `game-scene.tsx` draws it as the `block` variant of the network piece), the road (`road.tsx`), labels and the cards that open beside a piece on an HTML layer (`overlay/`), live traffic (`traffic/`) and the canvas of the `/entidades` page (`catalog/`). `map-canvas.tsx` is the canvas both maps are drawn on, with its camera already facing the point it is given (`target`, the origin by default) in its first frame, so no label is ever placed for another view and then moved; `ink.tsx` holds the paper box with ink edges that every piece is built from and the frame of every icon (`InkIcon`, also exported for the cards' icons in `src/ui/card-icon.tsx`); `theme.ts` names the colours and line widths the map takes from the design tokens (`@/design`).

- A card opens beside its piece, on the side `cardPlacement` and `terminalSide` pick in `layout.ts`. A firewall's card sits 110px from it, on the side with more room, and docks in the corner of the map, like a terminal, when neither side keeps it inside the map (a phone). The terminal of a device or an actor sits 70px from it (just past its mat and its label) and opens on the side where it stays inside the map and covers no label, not even its own (the label of a house hangs to the left of it), so in an office it opens toward the end of the row with no other device. If neither side is clear (an origin drawn outside the office, on the left of the row), it docks in the bottom left corner of the map, 6px from both edges and hanging from its bottom: the map is a band from the upper left to the lower right, so that corner has no label and no stamp unless the window is short. A docked card that grows (a terminal that lists a traceroute) must not climb into the band, so `dockRoom` gives it the height from the corner up to the feet of the lowest piece it spans (`placement.rooted` holds where each piece stands), `use-card-side.ts` writes it as `--card-room`, and the body of the card scrolls. A card beside its piece is centered on it, but a window or a map shorter than the card must not cut its band or its foot off: `cardTop` and `dockOffset` give the highest (just under the title bar that floats over the top of the map, `TITLE_BAR`) and the lowest (6px from the bottom edge) the card may sit, `sideHeight` the height it may take between the two, and the body scrolls when it is taller. A map too narrow to hold the card keeps the side with more room. `cameraPosition` and `CAMERA_ZOOM` are the map's one camera, which the layout tests use to project positions to pixels.
- The map keeps its size: `CAMERA_ZOOM` and the size of a piece never change from one incident to another, and the camera looks at the middle of what is drawn, so the office changes place when the pieces do. The label of a server of the internet floats higher than a device's, above the stamp of a ping that reaches the server, and `bounds` reaches past the server for the half of its label that hangs beyond it, so the camera centers the label too. `computeLayout` takes the pieces (`LayoutPiece`: kind and anchor, and, for a router, `inOffice`: whether it faces a local network, which `game` reads from the engine so the road lists the routers from the office outward) that `game` decides to draw, or `true` or `false` for the whole world with or without the outside; it never reads the list of an incident. A device piece anchored to a server of the internet stands for that server, and an edge piece anchored to a router of the world stands for that router. `spreadStamps` moves apart stamps that would touch: ones that end closer than a unit on the ground, and ones a little farther apart whose circles overlap on the screen along the diagonals of the isometric view (the tests that stay at the doorstep and the one that ends on the road in front of it). `spreadTags` is the same spread for the label of a packet with ports in a still scene: it moves the labels of packets whose paths have close middles (two `nc` to one machine follow one path) a label's width apart along the screen's horizontal (`TAG_PX_X` 176 px plus 8, against the 172 px measured for «paquete al puerto 3000»), and `Traffic` hands each label its shift (`PacketTags.apart`). The same function also keeps a label off the name of a floor (`names`, the point where `layout.floors` floats each one): a label that lies over a name moves along the same horizontal to the side of the name where it stands, just far enough to leave a gap of `TAG_GAP_PX`, and the labels it then touches move on outward (`clearOfNames`). A name never moves, so this is not a spread of a group: it runs after `spreadApart`, which would pull the labels back to the middle of their group. It reads two measures that the CSS and the camera decide and the layout cannot read: the size of a floor name (`NAME_PX_X` × `NAME_PX_Y`, 31 × 39 px for «Piso 1», which wraps at its space) and how far below its point on the ground the middle of the label hangs (`TAG_DROP_PX`, the 24 px of its CSS less the 9 px that the packet floats above the ground); both measured on 2026-10-12 at 1280×800 and 1440×900 in 02-01, where the label of a lone `nc` lay over «Piso 1» and now stands 10 px to its right, and, with two `nc`, the left label stands 10 px to its left and the right one does not move. A label that has no neighbour and lies over no name does not move. A moving packet does not use any of it: its label follows it. Both go through one function (`Footprint`: the size of what is spread), so a third kind of thing that floats over the map is a third footprint and not a third mechanism. `pathFor` goes through the drawn routers of `Trace.hops`, in order, ends at the router that drops the packet when that one is drawn (a router drops a packet with a TTL of 0, with no route, with no host, for a private IP and, for a packet from outside, when its NAT has no row to deliver it: `nat-no-translation`), and otherwise half way between the last drawn stop it crossed and the next one, and keeps a loopback ping, an invalid address and a ping from a device with no cable or no default gateway inside the device. A packet that is handed to a gateway that is not a router ends at the door of that device. `startsOnMap` says whether a packet starts at a piece that is drawn; one that does not is not drawn. A packet with ports wears a label (`PacketTags`): `translationDistance` says how far along the path the router that translates its ports stands (or, when the map does not draw that router, where the path leaves the office), and `tagAt` picks the label of the leg a packet is in at a distance, in plain text, and the one with the ports swapped for the answer coming back.
- Draws what the game computed: piece labels, locks, states and every packet's outcome come in as props; it never decides an outcome.
- Not here: panels and screens (`src/ui/`).
- May import: `@/levels`, `@/copy`, `@/design`, and only types from `@/engine`.
