# scene

The map of the game, drawn in SVG and animated with SMIL. The design is in `docs/specs/2026-10-12-follow-the-packet-2d-map-design.md`.

- Sub-folders, each with its own guide: `layout/` (pure functions that decide where everything goes, in pixels), `marks/` (pure functions that decide what stands on the layout: the fan of stamps with its «+N», the labels of packets, the pairs of boxes that overlap and the reading order of the buttons), `entities/` (what is drawn in SVG: the networks as the boxes of the groups, the pieces, the links and the dots of the marks, and the buttons of the stamps; its `icons/` holds the icons of the catalog, which the interface shares), `overlay/` (what stands on the HTML layer: the names of the groups, the name of the block, the labels of the pieces and the stamps), `traffic/` (what is drawn in SVG of the packets: their look, their traces and their labels; its `flight/` holds their flight), `camera/` (the pan and zoom of the finished drawing: pure calculations and the hook that puts them on the sheet) and `catalog/` (the canvas of the `/entidades` page: every piece, packet and stamp in every variant and state, in a grid of 5 columns; Task M16).
- `map-stage.tsx` is `MapStage`, the map in its box. It takes the `gap` it is given (the size of the free gap, which the screen measures on `.incident__map` and passes through `GameSceneProps.gap`; with a gap the stage wears `map-stage--under-panels`) or, with none, measures its stage (`div.map-stage`) with `useElementSize` and the stage is the gap, so a test can mount the stage alone; it draws nothing until that width is above 0 (so `layoutMap` is never called with 0 or `NaN`, and `NoRoomError`, which only a width that no registered world reaches can raise, is not caught), lays the map out at that width and draws it at one unit to the pixel inside the sheet (`div.map-stage__sheet`, the common parent of the three layers): an `svg` (`aria-hidden`, `viewBox` `0 0 <width> <height>` of the layout, `.map-stage__svg`) and the HTML layer (`.html-layer`, the layer that already existed, `overflow: clip`) over the whole sheet. In the svg: the boxes of the groups (with a filled tab on each outline of a block once `cidr` is known and the incident has a block to name), the links, the dots of the marks, the pieces and the packets (`Traffic`, `traffic/traffic.tsx`, with the fans the stage already computed: when `still` is true the traces and the labels of the packets, `StillTraffic` from `stillPackets(runs, layout, fans)`; with animations on the packets in flight, drawn with SMIL); on the layer: the names of the groups, the name of the block on its tab, the label of every piece and the stamps; and, after the layer and only with animations on, a second `svg` (`.map-stage__flight-labels`, `aria-hidden`, absolutely placed at the frame of the drawing, `pointer-events: none`) with the labels that fly beside the packets (`FlightLabels`), so they pass over the stamps without taking a click from them. The stage computes the sends in the air once (`useSendsInTheAir`, `traffic/traffic.tsx`: what the player sent and, besides, each earlier send of a combination that a newer one replaced, until its own trip ends) and gives the one list to `Traffic` and to `FlightLabels` (`inTheAir`), so a packet and its label never part; it computes the fans once (`fanStamps(stampsByPlace([...flows, ...sent], layout.drawn), layout.marks)`: the live flows first and then what the player sent) and hands them to `MapLabels`, which walks the reading order of the map with the labels and the stamps in it, and to `stillPackets` (`placeTags` takes the fans). When the incident has a block to name (`blockName` is not null) the layout leaves 18 px over the first row of each floor, and over each outline that starts lower, for the tab (`layoutMap` with `blockTab`), whatever the player knows and whatever the player has placed inside the block, so the size of the map depends neither on the terms nor on the outline: the room is there before `cidr` is known and before the outline appears, and an outline that starts on the first row of a floor, which is the case of the only incident with a block (02-04), moves nothing when it appears. `frame.ts` (`frameOf`) says where the drawing stands in the sheet: centered to a whole pixel when the sheet is wider than the drawing, against the left edge when it is not (below 360 px the drawing keeps 360 px, the sheet is as wide as the drawing, and the camera fits it into the box), and always against the top edge (`y` is 0: the strip of the screen is a zone of its own and floats over nothing). Whoever puts something on the layer adds that frame to the pixels of the layout (the names of the groups, the labels and the stamps do).
- The camera (`camera/`, its guide says how) is the one transformation of the sheet: the three layers move and zoom as one, so a label never leaves its piece, and what the tests read of the map (`MAP_MARKS`, the `g.map-piece` of a piece) is read from the DOM in screen pixels, which the zoom has already scaled. It fits the drawing into the box when the map is first drawn and each time the box changes size: at zoom 1 when the drawing fits, smaller (never under the minimum zoom) when it does not, centered sideways and against the top edge. The box of the stage does not scroll by itself: the stage is clipped to its box (the library gives it `overflow: hidden`) and the camera moves what shows in it; until the camera brings a focused or chosen piece into view, a native scroll of the stage, which `focus()` or a scroll into view can still make, is taken by the camera at once, so the stage is never left scrolled. The camera has its three buttons («Acercar», «Alejar» and «Ajustar») in the top-right corner of the box (`CameraButtons`, rendered by `MapStage` after the stage), drags with the mouse and zooms with the wheel; a drag past a few pixels never chooses a piece, and no preview opens while it lasts. `MapStageProps.controls` is an optional ref to `CameraControls` (`zoomTo`), which the tests use to set an exact zoom, and `onCameraMove` (also `GameSceneProps.onCameraMove`) runs when the camera moves, so the screen can close the preview of the inspection box.
- The free gap is the cell `.incident__map` of the grid, between the zone of Lucía and the panel and between the strip and the bottom strip; the map is laid out at its width and the camera fits the drawing into it. Above 900 px a stage with a gap (`map-stage--under-panels`) is not the gap but the whole window under the top strip: it reaches out of `.incident__map` to the left edge of the window (`--lucia-width`) and to the right one (`--panel-width`), and down to the bottom of the window (`calc(100vh - var(--strip-room))` tall), so the drawing is also drawn under the card of Lucía, the panel and the bottom strip, which lie over it (`z-index` 1 for the zone of Lucía and the panel and 45 for the bottom strip; the panel, the bottom strip and each card of Lucía have a background of their own, and the gaps between her cards show the map) and take the click there; the sheet starts at the left edge of the gap (`margin-left: var(--lucia-width)`), so the camera counts its pixels from the corner of the gap, and is at least as tall as the stage, so the layer covers all of it. A stage with no gap is as big as its box, `.incident .map-stage` filling `.incident__map`. At 900 px and under the stage is as tall as its drawing, `size.height`, so the page scrolls (the camera sees a box as tall as the drawing and leaves it at zoom 1). The column of the map must be `minmax(0, 1fr)`: a plain `1fr` cannot be narrower than the svg inside it, so the stage would never see a smaller width and the map would not shrink when the window narrows.
- No card opens on the map: the player's choice is read in the panel (`src/ui/shell/`), so the layer holds only the names of the groups, the labels, the stamps and the labels that fly. The stylesheet keeps what the stage styles from reaching what is not the map: the names of the groups are the direct children of the layer (`.html-layer > .caption`, not every `.caption` of the stage), and what is inside `.map-stage` reads the map's `--font-mono` (no `ui-monospace`), which is the face of the page whenever IBM Plex Mono loads.
- `pieces.tsx` is the choice of the piece for a `MapPiece`: `PieceShape` draws one piece (device, firewall, edge, internet or actor, with its variant and the state the game gives it) in the box the layout holds for it, inside a `g.map-piece` that is `aria-hidden` and wears its kind and variant (and its id), and `Pieces` draws every piece the layout holds a box for (`layout.icons`), the one the player chose selected. Its tests are `pieces.test.tsx` (jsdom: the catalog has a piece in every variant, no two variants draw the same shapes, the antennas, hidden from screen readers, the layer, the boxes of the networks) and `pieces.browser.test.tsx` (the states, which need real geometry, CSS and animation clock). `MapStage` takes `MapPiece` (with its variant) and draws `Pieces` over the links and under the layer; the boxes of the networks are drawn through `GroupBoxes`.
- `game-scene.tsx` is `GameScene`, which `src/scene/index.ts` exports. It takes `GameSceneProps` (defined there and exported by the barrel), among them `known` (the terms the player knows, which `incident-screen.tsx` passes from the session; none known when absent), because the name of a group shows only what the player has met. From the props it draws `world`, `pieces` (with the label of each), `blockName`, `activePieceId` and `still` (the piece the player chose is selected, and animations are off when asked), and passes `inspectTrigger` and `onActivate` to the labels; `flows` and `sent` are the packets whose stamps it draws and whose traces and labels (when `still`) or flight (when not) it draws, and `inspectStampsTrigger` (required, as the stage takes every prop) is what inspects the list of a «+N»: it takes the ids of the packets of the place and `incident-screen.tsx` makes it `trigger(PIECE_ID.stamps(ids))`, since `scene` cannot import `@/game`.
- `index.ts` is the layer's barrel: `GameScene` (of `game-scene.tsx`), `CatalogScene` (of `catalog/`), the icons (`EntityIcon`, `StampIcon`, `InkIcon`), `MAP_MARKS`, `pieceDomId` and the type `GameSceneProps`.
- The runs the map draws (`DrawnRun`, and `SentRun` for what the player sent) are given by `src/game/`, and a send by name is two of them: its question and the packet that asked, which carries `after`, the id of its question (`SentRun.after` in `overlay/map-stamps.tsx`). The scene treats the question as one more UDP packet: its path, its shapes, its label with ports and its stamp (at the door of the resolver, a place like any other for `fanStamps`) are drawn by the same code, and only the flight reads `after` (`traffic/`).
- `html-layer.ts` names, for the UI that floats over the map, what it has to keep clear of: `MAP_MARKS` (the labels, stamps and group names the player reads). The scene does not know the inspection box: the box reads it from the DOM and takes the corner of the map where no mark stands (`src/ui/incident/use-inspection.ts`).
- Draws what the game computed: piece labels, locks, states and every packet's outcome come in as props; it never decides an outcome.
- Not here: panels and screens (`src/ui/`), the Spanish text (`src/copy/`), anything that moves a packet (`src/engine/`).
- May import: `@/copy`, `@/levels`, `@/design`, `@panzoom/panzoom` (only in `camera/`), only types from `@/engine`, and other files of `src/scene/` (`@/scene/<file>`, `@/scene/<folder>/<file>`).
