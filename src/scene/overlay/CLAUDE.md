# scene/overlay

HTML drawn on the map's HTML layer: piece labels as buttons, the name of each floor (`floor-label.tsx`, plain text in the caption style), the anchor that keeps a card beside its piece (`card-anchor.tsx`; `use-card-side.ts` picks the side, or the dock in the map's bottom left corner, from the placement of `layout.ts`, and the card's gap and width reach `.map-card` as the CSS variables `--card-gap` and `--card-width`; the dock offset reaches it as `--card-x` and `--card-y`, how high the top of a card beside its piece may rise as `--card-top`, and the height a card may take as `--card-room` (for a docked card the room under the pieces it spans, or, with none above it, the height of the map less its margins; for a card beside its piece the height of the map between its title bar and its bottom margin: `.map-card` caps its height with it, its body scrolls, and `clamp` in `.map-card--left` and `.map-card--right` keeps the card between the title bar and the bottom margin), which the hook writes on the element in the same frame that drei moves the anchor, because a state would lag a frame and show the card displaced when the origin changes or the map pans; one `<Html>` always holds the card, so changing position never remounts it and its picks stay), and the type of the props of an inspect trigger (`inspect-trigger.ts`).

- A label shows a text the game already made safe for the ladder; it never decides what a piece is or whether it can be changed. It shows the lock of a fixed piece and the alert «!» of a device under attack, both also read as text, so neither can cover another label. A label has one button, however many cards its piece has: the card that button opens carries a tab for each (`src/ui/incident/map-cards/piece-card.tsx`), so a label never grows and has no second entry. A label gets the props of its trigger from the screen through the scene (a function from the piece id to them: the scene declares their type and imports nothing from the UI) and spreads them, so hovering, focusing or clicking it inspects the piece, and its own click calls the trigger's click and then opens its card; the trigger's `data-pinned` mark draws the ring of a pinned label. A resource's own name in the label (`[[servidor2]]`) is drawn as a name tag (`NameTag` of `@/design`), apart from the entity; the label's look (`map-button`) is in the design system's stylesheet, except the ring of a pinned label: one rule in `src/styles.css` serves it and the pinned stamp (`.stamp`) together, with an outline in forced colours.
- The HTML layer clips what hangs past its edge (`.html-layer`, `overflow: clip` in `src/styles.css`) and is never a scroll container: with `overflow: hidden` a browser scrolls it to show an element that takes the focus (the terminal of a laptop at the right edge of a 390 px map scrolled it 55 px), and from then on every label and card stood off its piece. `a card opened on a narrow map never scrolls the layer of the labels, so the labels keep their place over the drawing`, in `src/ui/incident/geometry/card-layout.browser.test.tsx` (until Task M21 in `tests/e2e/card-layout.spec.ts`), guards it.
- The `.map-card` class that `card-anchor.tsx` gives the holder of a card is `MAP_CARD` in `html-layer.ts`: the inspection box watches that element to choose its place, so renaming the class would leave the box blind to the card. The anchor knows nothing of the box.
- Not here: the cards themselves and every other panel (`src/ui/incident/`, `src/ui/incident/map-cards/`), moving packets and stamps (`src/scene/traffic/`).
- May import: `@/copy`, `@/levels`, `@/design`, only types from `@/engine`, and other files of `src/scene/` (`@/scene/<file>`).
