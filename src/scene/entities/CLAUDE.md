# scene/entities

What the 2D map draws in SVG, in the units of the layout (one unit is one pixel): the art that nobody operates, hidden from screen readers by the `aria-hidden` of the svg that holds it (`../map-stage.tsx`) and, for a piece, by its own (`../pieces.tsx`). It takes what the layout decided (`../layout/`) and draws it; it decides nothing. The pieces are the set the student approved on 2026-10-19: every entity and every variant has a design of its own, and the variants of a family share its base shape and differ by a visible mark, shape first and colour only as a second cue. The icons of the catalog, which the pieces and the interface share, are in `icons/` (its guide).

- `group-boxes.tsx`: `GroupBoxes`, the box of every group of the layout drawn as the network piece it is (`NetworkPiece`, with `data-group` and `data-kind`). It decides who wears the wall: an `office` does, a `floor` does not when an office or a VPC box holds it, and a `floor` that none holds is the office (the spec: with one floor, its box is the office's), and a `subnet` is a floor inside a VPC. The box has no name: a name is text and stands on the HTML layer (`../overlay/group-captions.tsx`). It takes the boxes of the tabs by group id (`tabs`) and gives each to its outline.
- `network.tsx`: `NetworkPiece`, the three variants of the network. Office: a dotted rounded box with a thick wall over its top edge (`.map-wall`); a floor is the same box inside it, without the wall. VPC: a dashed box whose top edge is a row of waves, a path of its own. Block: square corners, a thin dashed line of ink and, when the caller gives it a box, a filled tab (`.map-tab`; the text of its name is not drawn here: it is on the HTML layer, `../overlay/group-captions.tsx`). `MapStage` gives the tab to each outline of a block once `cidr` is known (`blockTabBoxes`): it stands over the top left corner of the outline, 18 px tall, in the room the layout leaves for it. The waves rise 10 px inside the box, under the 14 px that the box keeps over its caption.
- `device.tsx`: `DevicePiece` and `CatalogIcon`. A laptop, a printer, an EC2 instance and the server of the internet keep the shapes of the catalog (`src/scene/entities/icons/`, which this folder imports and never edits), drawn as large as the box the layout holds: `CatalogIcon` is a nested `svg` at the box whose icon takes all of it, so the 24 units of the icon are 48 px, and every shape under `.map-piece` has `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`, so the lines keep the width of the stroke scale. The state layers stay inside the 6 px (`LABEL_GAP`) between the icon and its label (on the door side the layout leaves 4 px before the box of the first stamp, so the mat, which the approved page draws 6 px wide, enters it by 1.5 px: the tip of the stamp's circle, only while the card is open; shake and pulse fit in the 4): a selected device stands on a shaded mat 5.5 px wider than its box (`.map-piece__mat`, drawn before the icon), a device with a forbidden packet shakes 2 px with animations (`animateTransform`) and, without them, wears a second border 3 px outside the shape (`.map-piece__border`; `SHAPE_BOUNDS` is the extent of each icon on its 24 units, and a browser test keeps the border outside the drawn shape and inside the box).
- `firewall.tsx`: `FirewallPiece`, the bar across the link of its device, in the 14 × 40 px box of the layout (`BAR_THICKNESS`, `BAR_LENGTH`). The firewall of a laptop is a solid wall of bricks, five courses with their joints; a Security Group is two posts in a T that face each other, with a passage of 8 px where the link goes through (both are one `.map-bar` path, so the bar keeps its shape in every state). While it blocks, the bar is filled with danger (`.map-bar--blocking`); while selected it is drawn with the heavy line without animations (`.map-bar--selected`) and, with them, it pulses (`animateTransform` scale, up to 1.35 about its center: 2.5 px more on each side of the door, inside the 4 px that the layout keeps clear of the fan of stamps).
- `edge.tsx`: `EdgePiece`, the stop of the main line, in its box of 48 px: a router is a low box with two antennas in a V (`.map-antenna`) and three ports; an Internet gateway is a taller box with no antennas and an arched door. The box is filled, so the line that runs through the stop does not show through it.
- `internet.tsx`: `InternetPiece`, the globe of the catalog, as large as its box and filled, so the line does not cross it.
- `actor.tsx`: `ActorPiece`: you are a cube with a screen on its left face, the attacker a cube of ink with edges of paper and a mask of two slots, and a home the front of a cube under a gabled roof with a door.
- `stamp.tsx`: the two buttons of the stamps, plain DOM that the layer places (`../overlay/map-stamps.tsx`), not art: this is the one file here that is not SVG, kept here because it is the button of an entity of the catalog. `StampButton` is the stamp a packet leaves (`button.stamp.stamp--<state>`, its mark `aria-hidden`, named by `stampLabel`) and `StampMore` the «+N» (`button.stamp.stamp--more`, `stamp--tight` from ten hidden stamps: 14 px instead of 18, so the number stays inside the ring up to +99; named by `MAP_TEXT.moreStamps`). When it hides a stamp with a problem it carries a dot, the student's decision J16 (a «+N» shows a danger dot when it hides a problem): `span.stamp__dot`, `aria-hidden`, on its lower right edge, in the danger colour with a paper outline, and its name adds `MAP_TEXT.hiddenProblem`: the ring stays ink and the dot is told by its shape and its name, not by colour alone. Both take the props of the trigger, which inspect the packet or the list. `MORE_SPECIMENS` lists the «+N» the catalog page shows (`+2`, `+12`, and `+2` with the dot); the 2D catalog (`../catalog/`, Task M16) draws them, each with its caption (`CATALOG_TEXT.more`).
- `links.tsx`: `LinkLines`, a polyline for every link of the layout, whatever its kind (`.map-link`, with `data-kind`), the `line-end` stub included, and `MarkDots`, the dot at every place where a stamp can stand (`.map-mark`, with `data-station`), which shows whether or not the place has stamps.
- Colour and width come from the stylesheet (`src/styles.css`), by role tokens and the stroke scale, never from a value in a component. The numbers a component names are geometry that the stylesheet cannot say for an attribute: the radius of a box (read from `--radius` and `--radius-tile`, or the 14 px of a group box), the paths of the pieces, the amplitude and period of the shake and the scale and period of the pulse.
- A state is never shown by colour alone: a bar that blocks keeps its shape and is also filled; a selected bar is heavier or pulses; a device with a forbidden packet shakes or wears a second border; an office has its wall, a VPC its waves and a block its square corners and its tab, and they are also told by their lines (dots, dashes, thin dashes).
- `icons/` holds the icon of every entity of the catalog (`EntityIcon`, `StampIcon`, `InkIcon`), which `device.tsx` draws as large as the box of a piece (`CatalogIcon`) and the packets reuse.
- Not here: the name of a group, the labels of the pieces, where a stamp stands and which ones the fan shows, and the cards (`../overlay/`, `../marks/`), where anything goes (`../layout/`), the packets (`../traffic/`), the choice of the piece for a `MapPiece` (`../pieces.tsx`).
- May import: `@/copy`, `@/design`, only types from `@/engine`, the icon of the catalog (`@/scene/entities/icons/entity-icon`), the type of the props of a trigger (`@/scene/overlay/inspect-trigger`) and other files of `src/scene/` (`@/scene/<folder>/<file>`).
