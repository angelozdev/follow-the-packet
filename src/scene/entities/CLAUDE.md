# scene/entities

The entity catalog: one file per entity of the map (`network`, `device`, `firewall`, `edge`, `internet`, `actor`, `packet`, `stamp`). Each file exports the entity's map piece and its 2D icon; `entity-icon.tsx` draws the icon of any `EntityRef`. The design of every entity (its card: concept, shape, variants, states, label, movement and accessibility) is in `docs/specs/2026-10-09-follow-the-packet-chapter-1-design.md`, section "Catálogo de entidades".

- A network is drawn as its slab (`office`, `vpc`) or, for the `block` variant, as a dashed outline on the ground with no fill, around the pieces the player placed inside a block of IPs. A device is drawn in the variant of its model (`laptop`, `printer`, `ec2-instance`) or as the server of the internet (`internet-server`), a narrow tower with three bays and an antenna stub, not the wide box with a lid and one door of the EC2 instance. It is a variant of the device piece, never a sibling entity.
- A piece takes its position, its variant and its state as props and decides nothing: whether a packet got in, whether a device is under attack or whether a card is open comes from the caller.
- Lines are ink (`INK`) on paper (`PAPER_FILL`), as wide as the stroke scale says (`LINE`); vermilion (`DANGER`) is only for danger: blocked, intruder and alert. A state is never shown by colour alone: it also changes the shape or the text.
- A packet is drawn by its protocol: ICMP is a dot, TCP a square and UDP a hexagon (`PACKET_SHAPES`), each filled when it asks and a ring when it answers, so an answered packet comes back as the ring of its own protocol. An intruder is the same shape in vermilion with a second border of the same shape around it (the shape says the protocol and the border says the role, for every protocol); no packet is drawn as a rhombus. A packet that has ports wears a label beside it (`PacketTag`, on the HTML layer, with no pointer and hidden from screen readers, since the stamp already tells it): its protocol and ports in the leg where the packet is, which changes at the router that translates them, and the reply wears the ports swapped; the still drawing shows the label of the leg where the packet stands. A packet with no ports (a ping, or any packet of a VPC) wears none. A warning (Destination Host Unreachable, Time Exceeded) is the `warning` variant of the same piece, an ink triangle with an exclamation mark cut out of it, and is never an intruder's: a router's warning comes back along the path from the router that sent it (`warned`) and the sender's own device generates the other one, which does not travel and appears at the end of the path (`self-warned`). A blocked packet bursts and leaves ✕; a lost one shrinks away and leaves a dashed ?; a warning leaves a !. A stamp tells a screen reader its packet and how it ended (`stampLabel`).
- An icon is the piece seen from the front, in one colour (`currentColor`, drawn with `InkIcon` from `@/scene/ink`), hidden from screen readers because the text beside it names it. Every variant and every stamp state has its own shape.
- The `block` variant of `NetworkPiece` is the outline of a block of IPs and may wear a `name` (the block in CIDR notation), drawn with `MapTag`, the label in the frame of `packet-tag` that a packet with ports wears too; it is one element, not an outline with a label beside it, and without a `name` (the catalog specimen) it draws only the outline.
- Every animation has a still version for "Sin animaciones", chosen by the `still` prop.
- The rest of the game reaches the catalog only through `index.ts` (`@/scene/entities`); files inside import each other directly (`./x`).
- Not here: where a piece sits (`src/scene/layout.ts`), the traffic clock (`src/scene/traffic/`), labels as buttons (`src/scene/overlay/`).
- May import: `@/copy`, only types from `@/engine`, and the scene's drawing helpers (`@/scene/ink`, `@/scene/theme`, `@/scene/layout`, `@/scene/edges`, `@/scene/html-layer`, `@/scene/overlay/inspect-handlers`).
