# scene/camera

The camera of the 2D map: one transformation of `div.map-stage__sheet`, the common parent of the three layers (the `svg`, the HTML layer and the `svg` of the flying labels), so they move and zoom as one and every label stays on its piece. The map is drawn once, at one unit to the pixel, and the camera moves the finished drawing; nothing here draws on the map. The design is in `docs/specs/2026-10-29-follow-the-packet-shell-layout-b-design.md` («El mapa en B», contract points 2 to 5 and 8).

- `camera-view.ts` is pure: no React, no DOM. A `View` is the zoom (`scale`) and where the top-left corner of the sheet lands (`x`, `y`, in screen pixels from the top-left corner of the gap). `fitView(gap, drawing, limits, start?)` is the fit: the drawing at zoom 1 when it fits the gap, otherwise smaller, never under `limits.min`; centered sideways to a whole pixel when it is narrower than the gap and against the left edge when it is not, and always against the top edge; when it does not fit the gap even at the minimum zoom, on each axis where it does not (the other axis keeps the fit above) it puts the `start` it is given (a point of the sheet: the center of the icon of the first origin, `MapStage` finds it in the layout from `startPieceId`) at the center of the gap only as far as the drawing allows: on that axis the view stops at the edge of the drawing, so no blank lies beyond it inside the gap, and a piece near the edge ends nearer to that edge than to the center of the gap (the student's ruling of 2026-10-30, «Parar en el borde»; 02-06 at 1280×600 starts with the drawing flush with the top of the gap and `tu-laptop` in view); with no `start` the drawing stays against the left and top edges; a gap with no height yet (a stage as tall as its drawing, before the drawing is there) does not limit the height. `zoomAt(view, point, scale, limits)` zooms around a point of the gap, which stays where it is, and keeps the scale between the limits. `clampView(view, gap, drawing)` keeps at least `KEEP_IN_VIEW_PX` (96) of the drawing, all of it when it is smaller, inside the gap on each axis, so the drawing never leaves the window whole; «Ajustar» always brings it back. `touchView(view, before, after, limits)` is the view after the fingers on the stage moved from the points `before` to the points `after` (in the gap): the middle of the first two moves the drawing, and the ratio of the distance between them zooms around the point where the middle was, between the limits; one finger only pans, and a third finger is ignored. `viewOfPan` and `panOfView` go between a `View` and the numbers of the library (see below). The `drawing` of these functions is a `Box`: where the drawing stands in the sheet (`frameOf`) and its size.
- `use-camera.ts` is `useCamera({ stage, gap, drawing, limits, start, still, controls, onMove })`: it makes the library's `Panzoom` on the sheet once the map is drawn, fits the drawing at that moment and each time the stage or the gap changes size (the fit is not kept: a resize of the window fits again), and gives back what `MapStage` puts on the map: the `ref` of the sheet, the `ref` of the stage (which listens to the wheel), `onPointerDown`, `onKeyDown` and `onClickCapture` for the stage, `fit`, `zoomIn` and `zoomOut` for the buttons, and `quietly`. The gap is the one it is given (the free gap of the screen, which can be smaller than the stage: above 900 px the stage is the whole window under the top strip and the sheet starts at the top-left corner of the gap, so every pixel of a `View` and of the wheel is counted from that corner) or, with none, the box of the stage read from the DOM at that moment (a stage as tall as its drawing changes size in the same render as the drawing, and the size the hook has in state is one frame late). `controls` is an optional ref to `CameraControls` (`zoomTo(scale)`, which zooms around the center of the gap with no limits, for a test that needs an exact zoom). `onMove` runs each time the camera writes a transform.
- `camera-buttons.tsx` is `CameraButtons`: «Acercar» (+), «Alejar» (−) and «Ajustar», three `Button` of the variant `small` with their words in `CAMERA_TEXT`, in the top-right corner of the gap (`.camera-buttons`, positioned over the box that holds the stage, after the stage in the reading order, so Tab walks the map first and the buttons last; they stay over the stage whatever the order, with `z-index`). They are not inside the stage, so a press on them never starts a drag. «Acercar» and «Alejar» zoom by 1.25 around the center of the gap and stop at the limits; «Ajustar» is the fit.
- The gestures: a drag with the primary button pans, from the first pixel, exactly as far as the pointer goes (the library's own arithmetic; `noBind` is set and the project binds `pointerdown` on the stage itself so the other buttons never start a pan, and `pointermove`, `pointerup` and `pointercancel` on the document). The library's `handleStartEvent` is replaced by one that does nothing: the default stops the propagation of the `pointerdown` and cancels it, which would keep the box of the inspection from seeing it and the label from taking the focus. A press that ends more than `DRAG_THRESHOLD_PX` (5 px) from where it began is a drag, and the `click` that follows it is swallowed in the capture phase (`onClickCapture`), so a drag that ends on a piece never chooses it; one that moves less is still a click. A `click` with `detail` 0 (the keyboard) is never swallowed. The wheel, plain or with Ctrl, zooms around the pointer by `exp(-deltaY × 0.002)`, ten times more with Ctrl (what a browser sends for a pinch of the trackpad), and a wheel by lines counts 16 px a line; the library's own `zoomWithWheel` steps a fixed amount for each event, which a trackpad's stream would make too fast. The listener is not passive, so the page does not scroll or zoom. The keys act when the key goes down in the stage, that is, with the focus on a label or a stamp of the map (Tab leaves the map: the keys never trap the focus, and the three buttons of the camera are after the stage, outside it): the four arrows pan by `KEY_PAN_PX` (64) screen pixels the way a page scrolls (ArrowRight shows what lies to the right, so the drawing moves left), `+` and `-` zoom by the same 1.25 as the buttons around the center of the gap, and nothing else is taken (not Space, which presses a button, and not a key with Ctrl, Meta or Alt, which the browser keeps for its own zoom). They do nothing with the focus anywhere else, in an editable field or in a button of the camera, because the listener is on the stage and no editable field lies inside it; with a system window open the map is `inert` and cannot hold the focus. A finger is a touch `pointerdown` on the stage and counted by `pointerId`: the library never sees it (its pinch adds the change of distance to the zoom, with no limits, and does not pan while it pinches), `touchView` does the work on each `pointermove` and the project's limits and the 96 px kept in view apply to it as to any move. Who keeps one finger is decided by `touch-action` of the stage in `src/styles.css`, and the controller asks the stylesheet (`getComputedStyle(stage).touchAction`) instead of keeping a second copy of the 900 px: above 900 px it is `none`, so the browser leaves every finger to the page and one finger pans the map and two fingers pan and zoom it; at 900 px or less (the stacked layout, whose page scrolls) it is `pan-x pan-y`, so one finger scrolls the page as it always did (the controller ignores a lone finger there) and the browser does not pinch-zoom the page over the map, and two fingers pan and zoom the map (checked in Chromium on 2026-10-30: the page does not scroll under two fingers; a real phone was not tried, and if one scrolls the page under two fingers, cancel the `touchmove` while two fingers are down). The mouse and the pen keep the library's own `handleDown` and `handleMove`.
- `quietly(trigger)` wraps a trigger of the inspection box so that hovering or focusing a piece or a stamp opens no preview while the mouse is down on the stage (a drag) or in the 250 ms after a wheel, and `onMove` (the screen gives `close` of `use-inspection.ts`) closes the preview that is open when the camera moves (once for a glide, when it starts). The marks that the box of the inspection keeps clear of are read from the DOM in screen pixels, so the box follows the zoom with no change.
- The limits are `ZOOM_LIMITS`, 0.7 to 2. The label font of the map is 12 px, so at 0.7 it is 8.4 px. Looked at on 2026-10-30 with captures of the map at 0.5, 0.6, 0.7 and 0.75: at 0.5 and 0.6 the letters of a name merge, from 0.7 on they read. 0.7 is also the highest minimum that still fits 04-01 whole in the box of 1280×800 (it needs 0.71), the case the spec names. The maximum is 2.
- `@panzoom/panzoom` (4.6.2, `.claude/rules/prefer-established-libraries.md`) writes `transform` and `transform-origin` on the sheet, and `overflow: hidden` and `user-select: none` on the stage (and an empty `touch-action`, so the stylesheet decides it). Its model is `scale(s) translate(x px, y px)` about the center of the element, with `x` and `y` in unscaled pixels; `viewOfPan` and `panOfView` convert it to a `View`. Its own limits are opened wide, because the project's limits live in `zoomAt`. A change goes through `reset` with the three numbers (zoom and pan in one write): `zoom` and `pan` one after the other would write twice. The library writes in an animation frame; the camera writes the same transform at once when it moves on its own and jumps (`place`: a button, the wheel, the keys, the fingers, a reveal of the focus, and a fit or a reveal of a choice with `still`), so the DOM is up to date when the call returns, and the write of the library a frame later finds the same value and does nothing (`onMove` runs once per change, from whichever writes first); a drag, which only the library knows, is written in its frame, so a test waits for the transform. The library also pans in a timeout at its creation, which would undo a fit made before it: the fit is given as its start values.
- The glide: with animations on, «Ajustar», the fit that follows a change of size of the stage or of the gap, and the bringing into view of what the player chooses (`revealChosen`) move the sheet with a CSS transition of the transform, 250 ms `ease-out` (`GLIDE_MS` and `GLIDE_EASING`, given to the library as its `duration` and `easing`, so its own `reset({ animate: true })` writes `transition` and then the transform in the same frame and the browser does the in-between frames; `apply(view, smooth)` is the one place that decides, and it glides only when `smooth` and not `latest.current.still`). Everything else jumps: a drag (the library's `pan` with `animate: false`, which clears the transition), the wheel, the fingers, the keys, `Acercar` and `Alejar` (a key held down or a button pressed again would only restart a glide that never arrives), and the bringing into view of the focus of Tab, which has to open the preview of the piece on the drawing where it now stands. A jump writes `transition: none` on the sheet before the transform, so a transition left on the style by a glide that ended never makes a drag or a wheel lag behind the pointer, and a glide in progress is cut short at the view it was heading to. `still` is the one switch (`GameScene` gets it from `useStillMode`: the player's «Sin animaciones» once chosen, the system's `prefers-reduced-motion` until then), read when the camera moves, so turning it on makes the next move jump. The first fit of a camera (the one the hook makes in the same commit that makes the controller) jumps: the library writes its own first frames from its start values in the next animation frames, and a glide there would run from them to the fit each time a screen opens. No preview opens while the sheet runs an animation (`sheet.getAnimations()` is not empty): `quietly` ignores the `pointerenter` of a piece then, and the focus of Tab first ends the glide at its view (`land`) and then brings the piece in and opens the preview, so the preview is always placed on a drawing that stands still. A reveal reads the box of the element in the sheet from the scale the DOM has at that moment (`getBoundingClientRect` of the sheet over its `offsetWidth`), not from the library's, which is already the scale the glide is heading to. The tests of the glide are `camera-glide.browser.test.tsx` in `src/ui/incident/geometry/`: they watch the `transitionrun` of the sheet, pause the transition they find and put it halfway, so they read an in-between view without waiting for time.
- Bringing a piece into view: `bringIntoView(view, box, gap)` (pure) shifts the view by the least that puts a box of the sheet inside the gap, on each axis, and keeps the zoom (a box wider than the gap is brought in by its start; an axis on which the gap has no size yet moves nothing: the screen measures its box a frame after the drawing is there, so a map stacked under 901 px, which is as tall as its drawing, has no height for that frame, and a focus that landed in it used to pan the whole drawing away and the refit pans it back, which closed the preview that the focus had just opened and, because a released box does not reopen for the element that still has the focus, left it closed). The controller turns the element into a box of the sheet from its rectangle and the zoom the DOM has, so it is the same before and after the library writes, and applies the shift. Two things call it: `controls.revealChosen()` (the screen calls it each time the player chooses something, whatever the path: a click, Enter, the newest line, a row of the registry, «Ver fila», Terminal, Navegador, the other origin of the terminal) brings what wears `data-pinned` (the label of a piece or the stamp of a packet) into the gap, and the focus of a label or a stamp (`quietly` wraps `onFocus` of every trigger: Tab, or the focus that comes back to a piece) brings the focused element into the gap before its preview opens, so the preview is placed on the drawing where it now stands. A focus that comes with the mouse down (a click on a label) brings nothing: the click that follows it chooses the piece, and the choice brings it.
- A native scroll of the stage (`focus()` on a label that lies out of the whole stage, a scroll into view) is taken by the camera: the stage's `scroll` event applies the scroll to the view and puts the stage back at 0, so the box is never left scrolled and «fit» undoes everything. A label under a panel but inside the stage is not scrolled by the browser at all; the camera brings it into the gap.
