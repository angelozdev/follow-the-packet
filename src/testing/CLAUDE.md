# testing

Helpers for tests only: network fixtures (`fixtures.ts`: the lab 03 VPC and the chapter 1 office), incidents (`incidents.ts`: a registered filter incident by its id, a send incident on the office, one on the office with routers whose chips ping, ping with a TTL and traceroute, an identify incident as 01-01 plays it: the cards of laptop-visita and the printer, whose tray starts with the IP of Lucía's note picked, a diagnose incident (seven cases on the office of the map that grows, each from the world before the incident plus its own changes, each with its chips, and the right cause in a different place), and a filter incident with a host firewall on your laptop with both lists empty, Source and Destination chips, and an outbound rule as its solution (`outboundFilterIncident`), a filter incident with only the inbound rules of a host firewall open: laptop-lucia has a firewall with no inbound rules that lets everything out, Source chips for your laptop and the printer, and an inbound rule as its solution (`hostFirewallIncident`, built from `outboundFilterIncident`), a send incident with a setting: a reinstalled laptop with no default gateway, whose card offers none, the router, the printer and an IP nobody has, and whose terminal offers only the address and the ping; `namesSendIncident`, a send incident on `officeWithNames()` that offers what chapter 2 adds to the contract: an `nc` and a `dig`, a name as an address, the DNS server and the listen address as settings, a table of routes and one of records, a clock, the card a piece opens first and tasks that expect an answer and a status; `classifyIncident`, an identify incident whose card of your laptop classifies the printer and laptop-andres around 192.168.1.0/24; and `repairableDiagnoseIncident`, the diagnose whose fifth case is the loop of the final watch of chapter 1 (the router of the provider sends everything back to the office router, so the ping of laptop-marta to the server ends in Time Exceeded) and has a fix: the table of routes of the provider, with the default route and the range of the server as destinations and the office router and the backbone as next hops, the task that the ping comes back, and a reference solution that takes the default route away, places the range of the server through the backbone and pings again), the VPC worlds of chapter 4 with the packets of their flows (`chapter4Worlds`: 04-01 and 04-02 each at its start, once solved and with each one-chip rule placed, which the engine tests need and cannot import from `@/levels`), the office worlds of chapter 1 with the packets that can be sent in each (`chapter1Worlds`: every incident at its start, once solved and with each choice of each of its settings, and every case of a diagnose incident in its own world; the packets are those of the flows and of the send chips, a traceroute through its probe), readers for the repository's `syllabus.md` and for the chapter map and the levels of the chapters doc (`repo.ts`) and the Vitest setups (`setup.ts` for the `unit` project and `browser-setup.ts` for the `browser` project).

- `map-pieces.ts` (`mapPiece`, `FLOORS_LABEL_PIECES`, `MapPieceFixture`) builds the pieces of the map as the game hands them to the scene, for the tests of `src/scene/map2d/` that draw labels and pieces: `mapPiece(entity, anchorId, label, extras)` gives one (its id is its kind and its anchor, unlocked and normal unless `extras` says otherwise) and `FLOORS_LABEL_PIECES` is the set of the office with two floors that the labels are tested with. A browser test imports it from its file (`@/testing/map-pieces`), as it does `@/testing/fixtures`, because the barrel also reads files with Node.
- `mapRun(world, packet, id, extras)` (also in `map-pieces.ts`) builds the run of a packet as the game hands it to the scene, for the tests of the stamps of `src/scene/map2d/`: the trace the engine simulates, the label the packet wears, the piece id `packet:<id>`, and the facts of its stamp as the trace gives them (reached, answered, stopped by a firewall; no warning, no hidden stop, no intruder), with `extras` to change any of them or to give it a `send`. `triggerSpies()` gives the props of a trigger with a spy for each of its five handlers. Both are imported from their file, as `mapPiece` is, because a jsdom test cannot load the barrel, which reads files with Node.
- `officeWithNames()` is the office of the map that grows with the DNS of chapter 2: `tu-laptop` uses the resolver `servidor-dns`, which asks `servidor-autoritativo` (zone `lucia.example`, with `www` and an alias `blog`) and `servidor-autoritativo-noticias` (zone `noticias.example`, down); `NAME_SERVERS` holds their ids, IPs and the zones. They sit behind `router-backbone`, which gets one interface for each.
- `dig(from, name)` builds the `dig` of a name (a UDP datagram to port 53 with `lookup: 'dig'` and the name as destination) and `withResolverCache(net, entries)` gives the resolver of `officeWithNames()` an initial `dns.cache`, measured against the clock of the game.
- `tcpService(port, listen)` builds a TCP `Service` (on all addresses unless told otherwise) for the tests that give a device or a server a program.
- `EVERY_TERM` is every registered term, for the tests that do not care what the player knows yet; a test about what is hidden builds its own set or its own incident.
- `CHAPTER_1_TERMS` is the terms the incidents of chapter 1 introduce: for a test of chapter 1 that sends packets and must not see a term of a later chapter (`EVERY_TERM` includes them, so the box of a trip would show a route or a translation). A test that needs a session with it builds the incident with `introduces: [...CHAPTER_1_TERMS]`.
- `CHAPTER_2_TERMS` is the terms the incidents of chapter 2 introduce, which a player of 04-01 knows because chapter 2 comes before it in game order: for a test that pins what a box of the VPC shows to the terms of chapters 1 and 4 (`inspection.test.ts` removes them from the session of 04-01 it reads).
- The browser project (`pnpm test:browser`, `vitest run --project browser`) runs every `src/**/*.browser.test.{ts,tsx}` in headless Chromium through `@vitest/browser-playwright`, for what needs a real layout or real CSS. `browser-setup.ts` loads `@/styles.css` (the tokens and the primitives) once per file and cleans up Testing Library after each test, so a browser test uses the `render` of Testing Library as in jsdom. `pnpm test` runs only the `unit` project (which excludes the browser tests), `check:fast` has no browser step, and a bare `vitest` runs both projects: always pass `--project`. A failing browser test leaves its screenshot under `.vitest/`, which git ignores.
- Wait in a browser test with `expect.element`, `expect.poll` or the fake timers of Vitest, never with a fixed time: lint rejects `setTimeout`, `setInterval` and `waitForTimeout` in `*.browser.test.*`.
- Browser tests are typed with the browser matchers only. `tsconfig.browser.json` (the second step of `pnpm typecheck`) holds the `*.browser.test.*` files, `browser-setup.ts` and `vite.config.ts`, and `tsconfig.json` excludes the browser tests: `vitest/browser` (also imported by `@vitest/browser-playwright`, so by `vite.config.ts`) declares its matchers for the whole type program, and next to those of jest-dom, which the unit tests use, one of the two sets won by the order of the files. A helper that imports `vitest/browser` has to be added to the `include` of `tsconfig.browser.json` and to the `exclude` of `tsconfig.json`; the globs list `.ts` and `.tsx` separately because tsconfig globs have no braces.
- The `toHaveTextContent` of `expect.element` is not the one of jest-dom (checked in Chromium on 2026-10-17 with Vitest 5.0.3). It compares the whole text of the element with the string: `toHaveTextContent('hello')` fails on `hello world`. With a RegExp it never matches, and the types now reject it (a type error, proven with a throwaway file on 2026-10-17). A negated check with a string that is not the whole text checks nothing: `.not.toHaveTextContent('hello')` passes on `hello world`, and the types accept it. For a part of the text or a pattern use `toMatchTextContent('hello')` or `toMatchTextContent(/hel+o/)`, which under `.not` fails when the text matches, as it should.
- A browser test reads the tokens through computed style (`getComputedStyle(document.documentElement).getPropertyValue('--color-page')`), not through `@/design`, which this layer may not import.
- Experiment of the wheel (2026-10-17, Vitest 5.0.3): `userEvent.wheel(element, { delta: { y: 100 } })` of `vitest/browser` moves an `overflow: auto` container inside the iframe as the real wheel does. The container saw one trusted `wheel` event (`isTrusted`, `deltaY` 100, `deltaMode` 0), its `scrollTop` went from 0 to 100 (to 400 after three more steps of 100), and `scrollIntoView` was never called. The scroll is read with `expect.poll`. No command of `browser.commands` was needed, so `browser-commands.ts` does not exist, and the five tests with the wheel of the spec list stay in the layer (c).
- Experiment of the width (2026-10-17, Vitest 5.0.3): `await page.viewport(width, height)` resizes the iframe of the test and the media queries read it: after `page.viewport(1280, 800)` `window.innerWidth` is 1280, `(max-width: 900px)` does not match and `.incident` has two columns (`900px 380px`); after `page.viewport(390, 844)` it is 390, the query matches and `.incident` has one (`390px`). The tests of 390 px use `page.viewport`, not an iframe of their own. The default viewport of the project is 414 × 896, which is already under 900 px: a test of the desktop layout sets `page.viewport(1280, 800)` first, and a test that changes the viewport puts the initial one back afterwards.
- Production code never imports `@/testing`.
- May import: `@/engine`, `@/copy`, `@/levels`.
