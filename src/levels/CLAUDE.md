# levels

The game chapters and their levels (`chapters.ts`), the incident contract and its chips (`contract/`: the schema and types of an incident, the rule two chips name and the chips a rule has, the packet and every combination the send chips make, and the check that no combination leaves an office), the registry that loads incident files (`NN-<chapter>/NN-<slug>.ts`, or `NN-<chapter>/<block>/NN-<slug>.ts` when a chapter groups its incidents in block folders) with `import.meta.glob`, the world chain (`world.ts`) and the term ladder (`ladder.ts`).

- `CHAPTERS` holds the 33 chapters of `docs/research/2026-10-09-follow-the-packet-chapters.md` in game order, each with its level (`tier`, one of `TIERS`: 0 Bases to 4 Especialista), its title, the course modules it plays (maybe none) and whether it is optional; `chapters.test.ts` checks them against that doc. A chapter's number is its place in the game, not a course module: chapters 1 and 4 keep their numbers, folders and incident ids. In code a level of the roadmap is a tier, because `level` already names an incident (`LevelEntry`, `#/level/…`).
- An incident starts from its own base world or from the reference solution of the incident before it plus its changes; never from the player's own solution. The incident before it is the previous one of its chapter or, for the first incident of its chapter that has changes and no base, the last incident of the chapters before it in game order (the first of chapter 2 starts from the solved world of the last of chapter 1, and a chapter with no incidents is skipped); the first incident of the game with changes and no base fails with a clear message.
- `lesson` (a lesson of `syllabus.md`) and `concept` (a note of `concepts/`) are optional in the contract: only an incident of a chapter whose `modules` is not empty must cite them (`registry.test.ts`, `cites a lesson of syllabus.md and a concept note that exists`), and what it cites must exist. A chapter with no course modules, such as chapter 3, leaves both out; the note is born when the student verifies the topic, not for a test.

- Every change an incident (or a case of a diagnose) makes to a device, a router or a server of the internet is named in its report, in the feedback of its right explanation or in its takeaway (for a case, its report or the feedback of its right cause), as `.claude/rules/explained-behavior-changes.md` says. `explained-changes.test.ts` compares the world before and after the changes (`worldBeforeChanges` and `startWorld`, and `startWorld` and `caseWorld`) with `changedNames`, and its list `UNEXPLAINED_CHANGES_PENDING` holds the gaps not fixed yet (none today). `changedNames` compares every field of a device and of a server of the internet, and does not count a new interface of a router that faces a piece only the changes add (the wiring of a new server or router), though any other change to that router still counts. When every device of a floor changes the same way (01-06 sets the gateway of the five devices of floor 1), naming the floor is enough.
- An incident has a mechanic. `filter`: the player places rules with Type and Source chips (and, when the incident offers `chips.to`, outbound rules with Type and Destination chips), and tasks read live flows (a flow is `required`, `forbidden` or `ambient`: an ambient flow belongs to no task, never fails and is expected by the probe battery, so traffic that an earlier incident already lets through, such as the rule of 01-12 in 01-13, does not cost the middle star). `send`: the player sends packets with origin, address and message chips (`packetFromChips`), and each task expects the result of one combination: `expect.outcome` is any engine `Outcome` (`replied`, `timeout`, `unreachable`, `ttl-exceeded`, `no-route`…), `reached` (the packet got to the destination, with or without a reply) or `any` (it was sent), and `expect.by`, when it is there, names whoever answered, warned or dropped it (a device, a router, an actor or a firewall); its flows are only ambient. A send move never changes the world, but a setting move, a table move and a wait do or can: `send.settings` offers a tray of chips `{ id, label, value }` per setting of a device: its default gateway (the kind a setting has when it names none; `value` an IP or `null`, none), its DNS server (`dns-server`, the same values) or the listen address of one of its services (`listen-address`, which names the `service`; `value` `all` or `loopback`), and the move `setSetting` (`{ deviceId, settingId, chipId }`) is applied by `applyMove` as the engine's `setGateway`, `setDnsServer` or `setListenAddress` according to the kind (the same world comes back when the chip is already in effect, when the incident does not offer it, or, for a listen address, when the device has no service on that protocol and port). `settingValue(world, setting)` is what a setting has in the world now, by its kind: `applyMove` compares the chip with it and the card of settings picks its chip by it. A send incident may also offer tables the player edits (`send.tables`: routes of a router and records of a server), a clock (`send.clock`, the steps of «Esperar») and the card a piece opens first (`send.opensFirst`), and a task may expect what the DNS answered (`expect.answer`, `expect.status`); `contract/CLAUDE.md` says how. The moves on them: `placeEntry` (`{ tableId, picks }`, one chip picked in each tray) is applied as the engine's `addRoute` or `addRecord` (a record gets the time to live of the game) and `removeEntry` (`{ tableId, entryId }`) as `removeRoute` or `removeRecord`, and the same world comes back when the table is not the incident's, a pick is no chip of its tray, the chips make no record, the engine does not take the entry or no row has that id; `placementProblem(world, incident, move)` says why an entry the incident offers did not go in, as the code of `recordProblem` or `routeProblem` when the engine refused it, or `chipProblem` when the chips make no record although every tray has a pick (an address record whose value is no IP: `a-record-needs-ip`, `EntryProblem`), and nothing for one it does not offer or whose trays are not all picked. `wait` (`{ seconds }`, a step of the clock) changes no world: the session moves its clock. The ids of the rows of a table are `routeEntryId` (the destination) and `recordEntryId` (name, type and value), which the rows the view draws will use for their remove button (`contract/tables.ts`). `identify`: the player says something about a device on its card, picking a chip for each tray (`identify.cards`: per device, trays of chips with an `id`, a `name`, and the chips `{ id, label, feedback? }`); each task expects one chip of one tray (`expect: { deviceId, trayId, chipId }`), nothing travels (no flows), and the answer is a move `answerCard`. An identify incident may also have a card that classifies other devices of the world into two bins around a CIDR block (`identify.classify`), and the answer for each of them is saved under its own device. A tray may start with a chip picked (`initialPick`, with the line `initialPickRule` that the card adds to the feedback of every other chip it picks) and may be `segmented` (a yes or no, exactly two chips); a card may have a `question`. An answer move never changes the world.
- `diagnose`: the player is on duty and works case by case (`diagnose.cases`, in the contract): each case has a `report`, its own send chips (sent from a terminal, as in a `send`), two `hints` and a cause question with three options, one right. A case starts from the world the incident starts from plus its own `changes`, never from the case before (`caseWorld` in `world.ts`), and the player changes nothing in the world: a diagnose ends as it started (`solvedWorld`), so the incident after it continues from its start world. It has no tasks and no solution; the generic tests that read them filter with `isGuided`. The ladder reads the report, hints, question and options of every case and the message chips of every case (`incidentTexts`, `chipLabels`).
- A message chip may carry a `command` (`packet`, `ping`, `traceroute`, `nc`, `dig`, `curl` or `browser`) and, only for a `ping`, a `ttl` from 1 to 255 (the range of the engine's packet). A `ping` and a `traceroute` send an Echo Request. A `traceroute` builds no single packet (`packetFromChips` gives null): `probeFromChips` gives the Echo Request it repeats with a growing TTL. An `nc` is a TCP packet to the `port` of its chip and a `dig` a UDP datagram to port 53 that reads no hosts file, with the address chip, which may be a name, as its destination; neither has an ICMP type, and an ICMP message has no port. A `curl` and a `browser` are TCP packets that carry an HTTP request: they take the host and the port from the url of the address chip (`urlOfChip`), and the `options` of a `curl` (`-i`, `-d`, `-X PUT`, `--connect-timeout`) fix its method and body. A chip with no `command` is the first design: its type decides what the terminal writes.
- The ladder reads, besides the chips of the send, the name and the chips of the settings, the labels of the chips of the tables and of the steps of the clock, and the tasks and tables of the fix of a case (`incidentTexts`, which also reads the url of each address chip that is a url and the name of each user a browser signs in as; `chipLabels` is the message chips (but not the ones that sign in, whose label is the name of a user) and the Type and Source chips of a rules card, the chips that must name a term, because a setting chip is a value, a user is a name and an address, a url included, is only an address). The ladder opens what the player may change (`configurableParts`, per kind of firewall): the inbound rules of a host firewall once `rule` is known (01-12), those of a Security Group once `inbound-rule` is known (04-01); the outbound rules of a host firewall once `outbound` is known, and those of a Security Group once `outbound-rule` is known. `RULES_TERM` says which term opens each direction of each kind.
- In an office with no routers, no flow and no combination of chips (a traceroute through its probe) sends a packet from a device to an address outside it (`officeLeaks`): the game does not model the office's NAT. A loopback address (`127.x.x.x`) is the device itself and never leaves it, and `localhost` is not an address, so neither is a leak. An office with routers has a modelled way out, so it has no leaks. The registry test checks it on each incident's start world, and checks with `unknownExpectedIds` that every `expect.by` names a device, a router, an actor or a firewall of that world, and with `unknownCardDeviceIds` that every card of an identify incident names a device of it, with `unknownTableOwnerIds` that every table the player edits has an owner of its kind in the world (a router for routes, a server of the internet for records; in a diagnose, the tables of the fix of each case in the world of that case), with `unknownSettingDeviceIds` that every setting belongs to a device of it and with `unknownClassifiedDeviceIds` that the owner and the items of a card that classifies are devices of it.
