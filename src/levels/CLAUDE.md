# levels

The game chapters and their levels (`chapters.ts`), the incident contract and its chips (`contract/`: the schema and types of an incident, the rule two chips name and the chips a rule has, the packet and every combination the send chips make, and the check that no combination leaves an office), the registry that loads incident files (`NN-<chapter>/NN-<slug>.ts`, or `NN-<chapter>/<block>/NN-<slug>.ts` when a chapter groups its incidents in block folders) with `import.meta.glob`, the world chain (`world.ts`) and the term ladder (`ladder.ts`).

- `CHAPTERS` holds the 33 chapters of `docs/research/2026-10-09-follow-the-packet-chapters.md` in game order, each with its level (`tier`, one of `TIERS`: 0 Bases to 4 Especialista), its title, the course modules it plays (maybe none) and whether it is optional; `chapters.test.ts` checks them against that doc. A chapter's number is its place in the game, not a course module: chapters 1 and 4 keep their numbers, folders and incident ids. In code a level of the roadmap is a tier, because `level` already names an incident (`LevelEntry`, `#/level/…`).
- An incident starts from its chapter's base world or from the reference solution of the incident before it plus its changes; never from the player's own solution.
- Every change an incident (or a case of a diagnose) makes to a device or a router is named in its report, in the feedback of its right explanation or in its takeaway (for a case, its report or the feedback of its right cause), as `.claude/rules/explained-behavior-changes.md` says. `explained-changes.test.ts` compares the world before and after the changes (`worldBeforeChanges` and `startWorld`, and `startWorld` and `caseWorld`) with `changedNames`, and its list `UNEXPLAINED_CHANGES_PENDING` holds the gaps not fixed yet.
- An incident has a mechanic. `filter`: the player places rules with Type and Source chips (and, when the incident offers `chips.to`, outbound rules with Type and Destination chips), and tasks read live flows (a flow is `required`, `forbidden` or `ambient`: an ambient flow belongs to no task, never fails and is expected by the probe battery, so traffic that an earlier incident already lets through, such as the rule of 01-12 in 01-13, does not cost the middle star). `send`: the player sends packets with origin, address and message chips (`packetFromChips`), and each task expects the result of one combination: `expect.outcome` is any engine `Outcome` (`replied`, `timeout`, `unreachable`, `ttl-exceeded`, `no-route`…), `reached` (the packet got to the destination, with or without a reply) or `any` (it was sent), and `expect.by`, when it is there, names whoever answered, warned or dropped it (a device, a router, an actor or a firewall); its flows are only ambient. A send move never changes the world, but a setting move does: `send.settings` offers, per device that sends, a tray of chips `{ id, label, value }` for its default gateway (`value` an IP or `null`, none), and the move `setSetting` (`{ deviceId, settingId, chipId }`) is applied by `applyMove` as the engine's `setGateway` (the same world comes back when the chip is already in effect or the incident does not offer it). `identify`: the player says something about a device on its card, picking a chip for each tray (`identify.cards`: per device, trays of chips with an `id`, a `name`, and the chips `{ id, label, feedback? }`); each task expects one chip of one tray (`expect: { deviceId, trayId, chipId }`), nothing travels (no flows), and the answer is a move `answerCard`. A tray may start with a chip picked (`initialPick`, with the line `initialPickRule` that the card adds to the feedback of every other chip it picks) and may be `segmented` (a yes or no, exactly two chips); a card may have a `question`. An answer move never changes the world.
- `diagnose`: the player is on duty and works case by case (`diagnose.cases`, in the contract): each case has a `report`, its own send chips (sent from a terminal, as in a `send`), two `hints` and a cause question with three options, one right. A case starts from the world the incident starts from plus its own `changes`, never from the case before (`caseWorld` in `world.ts`), and the player changes nothing in the world: a diagnose ends as it started (`solvedWorld`), so the incident after it continues from its start world. It has no tasks and no solution; the generic tests that read them filter with `isGuided`. The ladder reads the report, hints, question and options of every case and the message chips of every case (`incidentTexts`, `chipLabels`).
- A message chip may carry a `command` (`packet`, `ping` or `traceroute`) and, only for a `ping`, a `ttl` from 1 to 255 (the range of the engine's packet). A `ping` and a `traceroute` send an Echo Request. A `traceroute` builds no single packet (`packetFromChips` gives null): `probeFromChips` gives the Echo Request it repeats with a growing TTL. A chip with no `command` is the first design: its type decides what the terminal writes.
- The ladder reads, besides the chips of the send, the name and the chips of the settings (`incidentTexts`; `chipLabels` stays the message chips, because a setting chip is a value). The ladder opens what the player may change (`configurableParts`, per kind of firewall): the inbound rules of a host firewall once `rule` is known (01-12), those of a Security Group once `inbound-rule` is known (04-01); the outbound rules of a host firewall once `outbound` is known, and those of a Security Group once `outbound-rule` is known. `RULES_TERM` says which term opens each direction of each kind.
- In an office with no routers, no flow and no combination of chips (a traceroute through its probe) sends a packet from a device to an address outside it (`officeLeaks`): the game does not model the office's NAT. A loopback address (`127.x.x.x`) is the device itself and never leaves it, and `localhost` is not an address, so neither is a leak. An office with routers has a modelled way out, so it has no leaks. The registry test checks it on each incident's start world, and checks with `unknownExpectedIds` that every `expect.by` names a device, a router, an actor or a firewall of that world, and with `unknownCardDeviceIds` that every card of an identify incident names a device of it.
- Not here: game-session state (`src/game/`), simulation (`src/engine/`), visible UI text (`src/copy/`).
- May import: `@/engine`, `@/copy`.
