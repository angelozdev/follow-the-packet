# levels/03-the-web-inside/asking-for-a-page

Block 1 of chapter 3, «Pedir una página»: the incidents that teach what a URL says, how a request and a response look, who answers and how one server gives many sites. All four are built (03-01 and 03-02 in Task 7 of the block plan, 03-03 and 03-04 in Task 8), and `chapter-3-dead-ends.test.ts` checks that no sequence of up to three moves leaves any of them with a task that can never hold.

- `01-an-address-for-everything.ts` (title «Una dirección para cada cosa»): a `send` incident from `laptop-lucia` to its own app with one message, the `curl` (`MESSAGE.curl`), and eight address chips given by parts (`URLS_BY_PARTS`: `http://localhost:<3000 or 80><"/pedidos" or "/pedido">?<id=7 or id=8>`), so the terminal draws the strip of the url, one tray for each part, and no tray of addresses or messages. It introduces `http`, `url`, `url-path`, `query-string` and `curl`. Its only change is `APP_ANSWERS_HTTP` (`shared.ts`): the program of port 3000 of `laptop-lucia`, which since 02-01 only accepted connections, now answers HTTP; the report names `laptop-lucia` and says so, and `changedNames` of the two worlds is exactly `[laptop-lucia]`. It draws `PAGE_PIECES`: `laptop-lucia` with its firewall, and nothing else, because its own app answers its own `curl` through the loopback.
  - Two tasks (`TASK.orderSeven`, `TASK.orderEight`), each `replied` with exactly the body of the order (`bodyTask`: `Pedido 7: 2 cafés.` and `Pedido 8: 1 té.`). The other six urls are the contrast: the port 80 is refused (`curl: (7) Failed to connect to localhost:80 …` in the output, and the box says that no program listens there and that `laptop-lucia` refuses it), and a path the app does not serve (`/pedido`) is answered with the body of its 404, «No hay nada en esa URL.» (it said «camino» until 03-05, which changed it: «camino» is the route of the packet in chapter 1 and «URL» is a term the player has since 03-01). A plain `curl` prints only the body, so the output card is where the player reads it; the box shows the `URL` field in its parts (from this incident) and not the request or the response, whose terms arrive in 03-02.
  - The right explanation is the second option (the sheet: 01 = 2nd): the question is «¿Qué parte de la URL eligió el pedido 7?» and the wrong ones are the path (it names the resource, the same in both `curl`) and the port (it picks the service). The three options say the same thing in the same shape and are close in length (the right one is the shortest, 30 characters against 36 and 37): the sheet's own texts made the right one the longest, and `chapter-3.test.ts` now asks that the longest is at most 1.4 times the shortest and that the right one is never the longest.
  - The takeaway says what the sheet's «Rigor» asks and `chapter-3.test.ts` pins: the parts of a url and what each picks, what the port 80 and a wrong path give, that the fragment after `#` does not reach the server, that the HTTP message is no packet but travels in the TCP connection the greeting opened, that `localhost` is `127.0.0.1` with no IPv6 in the game (a real `curl` may try `::1` first; the takeaway defines IPv6 and `::1` in one clause, the only text of the chapter that names IPv6), that the output is the one of `curl` 8.21.0 as read from its source and not tried on a machine, that which of `curl` and `curl-minimal` the Amazon Linux 2023 image has and what Amazon's patches change are unverified, and that a Mac prints other errors.
- `02-ask-and-answer.ts` (title «Pedir y contestar»): a `send` incident from `laptop-lucia` to its own app with three messages, the plain `curl` (a GET), `curl -d producto=café` (a POST) and `curl -X PUT -d producto=té` (a PUT), and four address chips given whole (`ORDER_URL`: the order 7, the order 8, the orders and the order 9), so the terminal draws the plain tray «Destino» and the tray of messages and no strip. It introduces `http-request`, `http-response`, `http-method` and `idempotent`. Its only change is `APP_TAKES_ORDERS` (`shared.ts`): the same program of port 3000 of `laptop-lucia` now also creates orders (POST) and replaces them (PUT); the report names `laptop-lucia` and says so. The order of `DELETE` is not in the world of the block: the texts name it, the app does not serve it. It draws `PAGE_PIECES` again.
  - Three tasks (`TASK.getEight`, `TASK.posts`, `TASK.puts`): the GET reads the order 8 (the last plain `curl` to `?id=8` is `replied`, with no body to match, because a PUT to the order 8 may have changed what it says and the task must not depend on that); the second of two equal POST to the url without `?id` is `replied` (`nth: 2`, with no body to match, so it holds only after the second post and a third does not undo it; the task and the feedback of the right option say «otro pedido» and name no number, because the numbers depend on how many orders the server already holds); the second of two equal PUT leaves the order 7 as the first left it (`Pedido 7: producto=té.`, `nth: 2`). The session keeps the orders from one send to the next (`Carried.apps`) and the orders go back to their start only when the world changes, so a GET of the order 7 after a PUT reads the replaced order and no task depends on it. The tray offers every message with every address, so the player can also POST to the address of an order: the app creates an order, as an Express app would (the query is data the app reads, not part of the route), and the report says so in one sentence that names the app of `laptop-lucia` and uses the path and query string of 03-01. That is why the posts task is relative: a POST to `?id=7` shifts the numbers of the orders, so the second POST to the orders no longer leaves the order 10. `chapter-3-dead-ends.test.ts` explores every send the incident offers up to three moves from the start and asserts that none of the states reached is a dead end: from each one, playing the solution closes every task.
  - The box shows the request and the response behind their terms (the lines of the protocol, the exception declared in rule 3 of the chapter spec) and no status code: `status-code` is a term of 05. The output card repeats its block for each send of the same chips, titled «Envío 1» and «Envío 2».
  - The right explanation is the third option (the sheet: 02 = 3rd): «Se cortó la red al mandar un POST. ¿Es seguro repetirlo?» The wrong ones are «Sí, porque la respuesta del servidor será la misma que la primera vez.» (idempotent speaks of the effect and not of the answer) and «Sí, porque HTTP evita los duplicados al repetir una petición.» (HTTP prevents nothing); the options keep the sheet's claims, lengthened so that the right one is not the longest. `chapter-3.test.ts` checks that no text says that idempotent means the same answer and none says that HTTP prevents duplicates (the text of the wrong option that holds that claim is the one text left out of the second check).
  - The takeaway says GET, PUT and DELETE are idempotent and POST is not, that idempotent speaks of the effect and not of the answer (a second DELETE can answer that the order no longer exists and leave the server as the first left it; the number is a term of 05), that it does not mean that nothing changes (a PUT changes the order and is idempotent) and that a client does not repeat a non-idempotent request by itself (RFC 9110 §9.2.2). It names no proxy: the term arrives in 07.
- `03-who-answers.ts` (title «Quién contesta»): a `send` incident from `tu-laptop` and from `laptop-lucia` with three messages, the `browser`, `curl --connect-timeout 3` and the plain `curl`, two whole urls (`LUCIA_URL`: the order 7 of her orders app on port 3000 and the root of her reports app on port 3001, both by her IP) and the rules card of her firewall (`LUCIA_RULES`), so `tu-laptop` and `laptop-lucia` each open a Terminal and a Navegador tab. It introduces `process`, `web-server` and `browser`. Its changes are `APPS_AS_PROCESSES` and `REPORTS_CLOSED` (`shared.ts`): her two apps are processes (`app-pedidos` and `app-reportes`) and Lucía closed the terminal where the reports app ran, so that process stopped; the report names `laptop-lucia` and says who closed it, and `changedNames` of the two worlds is exactly `[laptop-lucia]`. It draws `LAN_PIECES`: `tu-laptop` and `laptop-lucia`, each with its firewall.
  - Two tasks: the browser of `tu-laptop` receives the page of the orders app (`TASK.page`, `replied` with the body of the order 7: it holds only after the player writes the rule TCP 3000 from the block `192.168.1.0/24`, because the firewall of `laptop-lucia` lets in only the SSH of `tu-laptop`), and the `curl` of `laptop-lucia` to the reports app is refused by `laptop-lucia` (`TASK.reportsRefused`). The contrast is the three results the player sees: a silence (the browser and the `curl --connect-timeout 3` before the rule die in the firewall of `laptop-lucia`: the browser says «No se pudo conectar» and the `curl` prints nothing and the box says it waited 3 seconds and gave up, code 28), a refusal (`curl: (7) Failed to connect to 192.168.1.11:3001 …`: the process stopped, so `laptop-lucia` answers with a RST before there is any HTTP, and the box has no request and no response) and a page. The reports `curl` is the one of `laptop-lucia`, not of `tu-laptop`: a packet of `laptop-lucia` to her own IP does not cross her firewall, while the one of `tu-laptop` to the port 3001 dies there (silence) whatever the rule, so only from `laptop-lucia` can the player see the refusal.
  - The rule is the player's move: `TCP · 3000` from `192.168.1.0/24` (the block of the first floor, which holds `tu-laptop`) is the one the incident asks for; `TCP · 3001` and `TCP · 1-1023` do not open the port 3000 and leave the browser in silence, and `chapter-3.test.ts` pins that the rule opens the port 3000 and no other. The page is the order 7 (`http://192.168.1.11:3000/pedidos?id=7`), because the app serves no other page.
  - The right explanation is the first option (the sheet: 03 = 1st): «Con la app de reportes cerrada, ¿por qué curl no recibió ninguna respuesta HTTP?» (never «código»: the player has just read curl's `(7)` and `(28)`) The wrong ones are «Porque el firewall de laptop-lucia descartó el paquete, y curl se quedó esperando una respuesta que nunca llegó» (that would be silence; here there was a refusal) and «Porque el servidor web de la app de reportes devolvió un error HTTP, y curl lo mostró en la terminal» (no web server answered on that port). `chapter-3.test.ts` checks that no text says that a stopped app and a firewall are the same thing.
  - The takeaway says what a process is and that a service stops listening when its process ends, that a process is not a port, what a web server and a browser are, the three results (response, refusal, silence) and that a rule of the firewall names TCP and a port, not «HTTP».
- `04-one-server-many-sites.ts` (title «Un servidor, varios sitios»): a `send` incident from `tu-laptop` with two messages, `curl -i` and the `browser`, three whole urls with no port (`SITE_URL`: `www.lucia.example`, `blog.lucia.example` and the IP of `servidor-nuevo`) and the table of sites of `servidor-nuevo` (`SITES_TABLE`: the name `blog.lucia.example` or `blogs.lucia.example`, serving «El blog»). It introduces `http-header`, `host-header` and `virtual-host`. Its only change is `SHOP_SERVER` (`shared.ts`): `servidor-nuevo` becomes a web server on port 80 with one site, the shop, which is also its default site; the report names `servidor-nuevo` and says so, and `changedNames` of the two worlds is exactly `[servidor-nuevo]`. It draws `SITE_PIECES`: `tu-laptop`, `servidor-dns`, `router-oficina` and `servidor-nuevo`.
  - Three tasks, all with `curl -i` (the browser is offered to look, a task is tied to one message): `www` receives the shop, `blog` receives the blog and the IP alone receives the default site, the shop. Before the player adds the site, `blog` receives the shop: the request carries `Host: blog.lucia.example`, `servidor-nuevo` has no site with that name and answers with its default site (the cause is told in the feedback of the right option and in the takeaway). The player adds the site `blog.lucia.example` serving «El blog»; `blogs.lucia.example` (one letter too many) is the wrong chip, and a site with it leaves `blog` with the shop. The card only adds sites and a name keeps its first site, so «La tienda» is not offered as what `blog` serves: placed under the right name it could never be taken away and the task could never hold (`chapter-3.test.ts` tries every chip of the card). The IP alone never gets a site, before or after: its `Host` is the IP, which is no site's name. The box shows the `Host` header of each request, and the output of `curl -i` is the status line, the headers as the server sent them, a blank line and the body (curl 8.21.0, `src/tool_getparam.c:2131-2133` and `lib/http.c`, read by T5).
  - The right explanation is the second option (the sheet: 04 = 2nd): «blog es un alias de www. ¿Cómo sabe servidor-nuevo qué sitio dar?» The wrong ones are the IP (it is the same for both names: «distinta para cada nombre y para cada sitio») and the CNAME (the web server does not see the DNS: «que el servidor web lee del DNS»). `chapter-3.test.ts` checks that no text says the server knows the site by the IP or by the CNAME (the texts of the two wrong options that say it are left out).
  - The takeaway says what an HTTP header is, that `Host` carries the name of the url as written (also when it is an alias), that a server with many sites uses it to pick one (a virtual host each), that a name with no site gets the default site and that `Host` is required in HTTP/1.1. It names no status code and no SNI: those terms arrive in 05 and 12.
