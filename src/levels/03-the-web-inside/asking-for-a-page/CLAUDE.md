# levels/03-the-web-inside/asking-for-a-page

Block 1 of chapter 3, «Pedir una página»: the incidents that teach what a URL says, how a request and a response look, who answers and how one server gives many sites. 03-01 and 03-02 are built (Task 7 of the block plan); 03-03 and 03-04 close the block.

- `01-an-address-for-everything.ts` (title «Una dirección para cada cosa»): a `send` incident from `laptop-lucia` to its own app with one message, the `curl` (`MESSAGE.curl`), and eight address chips given by parts (`URLS_BY_PARTS`: `http://localhost:<3000 or 80><"/pedidos" or "/pedido">?<id=7 or id=8>`), so the terminal draws the strip of the url, one tray for each part, and no tray of addresses or messages. It introduces `http`, `url`, `url-path`, `query-string` and `curl`. Its only change is `APP_ANSWERS_HTTP` (`shared.ts`): the program of port 3000 of `laptop-lucia`, which since 02-01 only accepted connections, now answers HTTP; the report names `laptop-lucia` and says so, and `changedNames` of the two worlds is exactly `[laptop-lucia]`. It draws `PAGE_PIECES`: `laptop-lucia` with its firewall, and nothing else, because its own app answers its own `curl` through the loopback.
  - Two tasks (`TASK.orderSeven`, `TASK.orderEight`), each `replied` with exactly the body of the order (`bodyTask`: `Pedido 7: 2 cafés.` and `Pedido 8: 1 té.`). The other six urls are the contrast: the port 80 is refused (`curl: (7) Failed to connect to localhost:80 …` in the output, and the box says that no program listens there and that `laptop-lucia` refuses it), and a path the app does not serve (`/pedido`) is answered with the body of its 404, «No hay nada en ese camino.». A plain `curl` prints only the body, so the output card is where the player reads it; the box shows the `URL` field in its parts (from this incident) and not the request or the response, whose terms arrive in 03-02.
  - The right explanation is the second option (the sheet: 01 = 2nd): the question is «¿Qué parte de la URL eligió el pedido 7?» and the wrong ones are the path (it names the resource, the same in both `curl`) and the port (it picks the service). The texts of the three options are the sheet's, so the right one is the longest by a few characters.
  - The takeaway says what the sheet's «Rigor» asks and `chapter-3.test.ts` pins: the parts of a url and what each picks, what the port 80 and a wrong path give, that the fragment after `#` does not reach the server, that the HTTP message is no packet but travels in the TCP connection the greeting opened, that `localhost` is `127.0.0.1` with no IPv6 in the game (a real `curl` may try `::1` first), that the output is the one of `curl` 8.21.0 as read from its source and not tried on a machine, that which of `curl` and `curl-minimal` the Amazon Linux 2023 image has and what Amazon's patches change are unverified, and that a Mac prints other errors.
- `02-ask-and-answer.ts` (title «Pedir y contestar»): a `send` incident from `laptop-lucia` to its own app with three messages, the plain `curl` (a GET), `curl -d producto=café` (a POST) and `curl -X PUT -d producto=té` (a PUT), and three address chips given whole (`ORDER_URL`: the order 7, the orders and the order 9), so the terminal draws the plain tray «Destino» and the tray of messages and no strip. It introduces `http-request`, `http-response`, `http-method` and `idempotent`. Its only change is `APP_TAKES_ORDERS` (`shared.ts`): the same program of port 3000 of `laptop-lucia` now also creates orders (POST) and replaces them (PUT); the report names `laptop-lucia` and says so. The order of `DELETE` is not in the world of the block: the texts name it, the app does not serve it. It draws `PAGE_PIECES` again.
  - Three tasks (`TASK.getSeven`, `TASK.posts`, `TASK.puts`): the GET returns the order 7 (`Pedido 7: 2 cafés.`); the second of two equal POST leaves the order 10 (`nth: 2`, so it holds only after the second post, and a third post does not undo it); the second of two equal PUT leaves the order 7 as the first left it (`Pedido 7: producto=té.`, `nth: 2`). The session keeps the orders from one send to the next (`Carried.apps`) and the orders go back to their start when the world changes, so the report asks the player to read the order 7 first: a PUT replaces it, and the GET task reads what the order says when the player asks.
  - The box shows the request and the response behind their terms (the lines of the protocol, the exception declared in rule 3 of the chapter spec) and no status code: `status-code` is a term of 05. The output card repeats its block for each send of the same chips, titled «Envío 1» and «Envío 2».
  - The right explanation is the third option (the sheet: 02 = 3rd): «Se cortó la red al mandar un POST. ¿Es seguro repetirlo?» The wrong ones are «Sí, la respuesta será la misma.» (idempotent speaks of the effect and not of the answer) and «Sí, HTTP evita los duplicados.» (HTTP prevents nothing); the texts of the three options are the sheet's. `chapter-3.test.ts` checks that no text says that idempotent means the same answer and none says that HTTP prevents duplicates (the text of the wrong option that holds that claim is the one text left out of the second check).
  - The takeaway says GET, PUT and DELETE are idempotent and POST is not, that idempotent speaks of the effect and not of the answer (a second DELETE can answer 404 and leave the server as the first left it), that it does not mean safe (a PUT changes the order and is idempotent) and that a client does not repeat a non-idempotent request by itself (RFC 9110 §9.2.2). It names no proxy: the term arrives in 07.
