# levels/03-the-web-inside

Chapter 3, «La web por dentro» (level 0, Bases), in seventeen sub-chapters grouped in seven blocks, each in its own folder: one file per incident (`NN-<slug>.ts`, a default export built with `defineIncident`, `chapter: 3`), and `shared.ts` with what the incidents of the chapter share. The chapter names no course module (`modules: []`), so its incidents cite no lesson and no concept note. A block folder is born with its first incident and its own `CLAUDE.md`: `asking-for-a-page/` (block 1, «Pedir una página», 03-01 to 03-04).

- `shared.ts` starts from chapter 2, not from a world of its own: 03-01 starts from the solved world of the last incident of chapter 2 (02-14 is an on-call incident and changes nothing, so it is the world of 02-13). `DEVICE`, `IP`, `NAME`, `ORIGIN`, `PORT` and `sendMoveFrom` are those of `../02-ports-and-names/shared` (one id and one name per piece, never a copy that can drift); the chapter adds what its incidents need. Every name is under `.example` and every public address is in the blocks RFC 5737 reserves for documentation, as in chapter 2.
- The actions a block does to the world are named constants, each with a JSDoc that says what changes and to whom (the report of the incident names the device, as `.claude/rules/explained-behavior-changes.md` asks). `APP_TAKES_ORDERS` is 03-02 (the same program also takes POST and PUT: `POST /pedidos` creates the next order with the body it carries and `PUT /pedidos?id=<n>` replaces the content of that order). `APP_ANSWERS_HTTP` is 03-01: `setWebService` on the program of port 3000 of `laptop-lucia`, which since 02-01 only accepted connections. Its site, `orders`, has no name, so it is the default site and answers to any name; it serves `GET /pedidos?id=<n>` (the order book `Pedido {id}: {content}.` with the orders 7, `2 cafés`, and 8, `1 té`) and answers anything else with «No hay nada en ese camino.». The texts of the orders and of the 404 are data of the app; the engine writes no sentence.
- `MESSAGE` holds the `curl` chips: the plain one (a GET), `curlPost` (`-d producto=café`, a POST) and `curlPut` (`-X PUT -d producto=té`, a PUT); their labels start with the term `curl`, as every message chip names a term. `ORDER_BODY` holds the bodies the orders answer with (`Pedido 7: 2 cafés.`, `Pedido 8: 1 té.`, the orders 9 and 10 that two equal posts create and the order 7 once a put replaced it).
- `URLS_BY_PARTS` are the eight address chips of 03-01, given by parts (scheme `http`, name `localhost`, port 3000 or 80, path `/pedidos` or `/pedido`, query string `id=7` or `id=8`, written without `?`): the whole product of the values offered, because the strip of the terminal writes the url by picking one value per part and the chip it stands for is the one with those parts (`src/game/view/cards/web/url-strip.ts`). A missing combination would leave the line incomplete. From 03-02 the chips give their whole `url` (`ORDER_URL`).
- `bodyTask` is the task of a message from the terminal of `laptop-lucia` (a plain `curl` unless it says another `messageId`) that must come back with exactly this body (`expect.body`), in the last send of those chips or in the nth since the world last changed (`nth`).
- `PAGE_PIECES` is the list of pieces 03-01 and 03-02 draw: `laptop-lucia`, whose firewall is drawn with it.
- The ids of the 65 terms of the chapter, with their names and sub-chapters, are the ladder data of the tests (`LADDER_OF_CHAPTER_3`, `src/testing/web/`); `src/game/chapters/chapter-3.test.ts` checks what each incident introduces against it and that no text says a name of a later sub-chapter.
- Not here: the contract and the chips (`src/levels/contract/`), the registry (`src/levels/`), visible UI text and the reasons the box shows (`src/copy/`), the network rules (`src/engine/`).
- May import: `@/engine`, `@/copy`, `@/levels/contract/incident` and `@/levels/02-ports-and-names/shared`.
