# levels/03-the-web-inside

Chapter 3, «La web por dentro» (level 0, Bases), in seventeen sub-chapters grouped in seven blocks, each in its own folder: one file per incident (`NN-<slug>.ts`, a default export built with `defineIncident`, `chapter: 3`), and `shared.ts` with what the incidents of the chapter share. The chapter names no course module (`modules: []`), so its incidents cite no lesson and no concept note. A block folder is born with its first incident and its own `CLAUDE.md`: `asking-for-a-page/` (block 1, «Pedir una página», 03-01 to 03-04), `how-it-went/` (block 2, «Cómo salió y quién eres», 03-05 and 03-06) and `server-in-front/` (block 3, «El servidor de delante», 03-07 to 03-09; only 03-07 is built).

- `shared.ts` starts from chapter 2, not from a world of its own: 03-01 starts from the solved world of the last incident of chapter 2 (02-14 is an on-call incident and changes nothing, so it is the world of 02-13). `DEVICE`, `HOSTNAME`, `ORIGIN`, `PORT`, `sendMoveFrom` and `sendTaskFrom` are those of `../02-ports-and-names/shared` (one id and one name per piece, never a copy that can drift), and `IP`, `NAME` and `SERVER` are those of chapter 2 plus `servidor-app` (`IP.app`, `IP.destinationApp`, `SERVER.app`, `NAME.app`); the chapter adds what its incidents need. Every name is under `.example` and every public address is in the blocks RFC 5737 reserves for documentation, as in chapter 2.
- The actions a block does to the world are named constants, each with a JSDoc that says what changes and to whom (the report of the incident names the device, as `.claude/rules/explained-behavior-changes.md` asks). `APP_TAKES_ORDERS` is 03-02 (the same program also takes POST and PUT: `POST /pedidos` creates the next order with the body it carries, a POST to `/pedidos?id=<n>` also creates the next order, because the query is not part of the route, and `PUT /pedidos?id=<n>` replaces the content of that order). `APP_ANSWERS_HTTP` is 03-01: `setWebService` on the program of port 3000 of `laptop-lucia`, which since 02-01 only accepted connections. Its site, `orders`, has no name, so it is the default site and answers to any name; it serves `GET /pedidos?id=<n>` (the order book `Pedido {id}: {content}.` with the orders 7, `2 cafés`, and 8, `1 té`) and answers anything else with «No hay nada en esa URL.» (the same body in every app of the chapter). The texts of the orders and of the 404 are data of the app; the engine writes no sentence.
- `APPS_AS_PROCESSES` and `REPORTS_CLOSED` are 03-03, on `laptop-lucia`. The first is a `setServices` that carries `processes` (the only way to give a device processes): her orders app (port 3000, with the pages 03-02 left it) listens from the process `app-pedidos`, a reports app (port 3001, a web app with no pages, so it would answer only the 404 body) from `app-reportes`, both running, and her database (5432) and SSH (22) keep their services, because `setServices` replaces the list. The second is a `setProcess`: Lucía closed the terminal where the reports app ran, so `app-reportes` stopped and its service no longer listens. `LUCIA_URL` are the two whole urls 03-03 asks for (the order 7 on port 3000 and the root of port 3001, both by her IP `192.168.1.11`), `LUCIA_RULES` the Type chips `TCP · 3000`, `TCP · 3001` and `TCP · 1-1023` and the Source chips of your laptop, of the block of the first floor and of any IP for her firewall (`send.rules`), `OPEN_APP_TO_FLOOR` the move that writes the rule the incident asks for (TCP 3000 from the block of the first floor) and `LAN_PIECES` the pieces it draws (your laptop and `laptop-lucia`).
- `SHOP_SERVER` is 03-04, on `servidor-nuevo`: `setWebService` on its program of port 80, which since 02-12 only accepted connections, now a web server with one site, the shop, named `www.lucia.example` and also the default site; its port 443 stays as it was. `SITE_URL` are the three whole urls 03-04 asks for, with no port (the shop by its name, the blog by its name, which is an alias of the shop, and `servidor-nuevo` by its IP alone), `SITES_TABLE` the table of sites the player edits (`name`: `blog.lucia.example` or `blogs.lucia.example`, one letter too many; `serves`: «El blog», one of the two `SITE_BODY`, because a site that served the shop under the right name could never be taken away), `siteMove` the move that adds a site with two of its chips (`SITE_CHIP` holds their ids) and `SITE_PIECES` the pieces it draws. A site serves its home page (`/`) with its body and the 404 body of the orders app for any other path.
- `APP_CHECKS_THE_ID` is 03-05, on `laptop-lucia`: `setWebService` on the same program of port 3000, with the pages 03-02 left it, and an order book that has a `badIdBody` («El id no es un número.»), so the app checks the `id` of a GET or a PUT and answers 400 to one that is not a number; an order that does not exist, a request with no `id` and a path the app does not serve still answer 404, and `ordersWeb` and `ordersApp` take the book. `STATUS_URL` are the four whole urls 03-05 asks for, by her IP (the order 7, which is `LUCIA_URL.orders`, a path the app does not serve, `id=siete` and `id=99`), `statusTask` the task that expects the status code (`expect.statusCode`) of the response to the `curl -i` of your laptop to one of them, and `TASK` gains its three ids. `NOT_FOUND_BODY` is «No hay nada en esa URL.» for every app of the chapter, the reports app and the sites of 03-04 included.
- `APP_HAS_USERS` is 03-06, on `laptop-lucia`: `setWebService` on the same program of port 3000, with the pages and the check of the id that 03-05 left it, a page `/admin` that asks for the `admin` role (`ADMIN_BODY`, «Panel de administración.», to a user who has it), a page `/login` that opens a session for the user whose name and password a form gives and answers `LOGIN_BODY` («Sesión iniciada.») with `Set-Cookie: sesion=s<n>`, and the accounts of the site: `USER.lucia` (`lucia`, password `clave-lucia`, role `admin`) and `USER.marta` (`marta`, `clave-marta`, role `staff`), the realm `admin`, and the bodies of the 401 («Hace falta un usuario y una contraseña válidos.») and of the 403 («Ese usuario no tiene permiso para entrar aquí.»). `USER` is where a sign-in reads the user and the password. `ADMIN_URL` is the whole url 03-06 asks for (`/admin` by her IP), `curlMarta`, `curlLucia` and `curlCookie` are the message chips `curl -i -u marta:clave-marta`, `curl -i -u lucia:clave-lucia` and `curl -i -b sesion=s1`, `browserLucia` and `browserMarta` the browser messages that sign in as each user (a `signIn` with the path of the login and the user and password of `USER`; their labels are the names in the tray «Usuario»), `statusTask` takes the message chip it sends with (the `curl -i` unless it says another) and `TASK` gains four ids.
- `SERVER_IN_FRONT` is 03-07 (see `server-in-front/`): four actions, the new server `servidor-app` with the orders app of 03-06 (`USERS_PAGES`, the pages of 03-06, are shared with `APP_HAS_USERS`) and its wiring to `router-destino`, the end of the process of the orders app of `laptop-lucia`, and the proxy of `servidor-nuevo` (the shop keeps `/`, the paths `/pedidos`, `/admin` and `/api` go to `servidor-app:3000`, the blog stays). `PROXY_URL` is the whole url 03-07 asks for, by name, `PROXY_PIECES` the pieces it draws and `TASK` gains one id.
- `MESSAGE` holds the `curl` chips: the plain one (a GET), `curlPost` (`-d producto=café`, a POST) and `curlPut` (`-X PUT -d producto=té`, a PUT) and `curlTimeout` (`--connect-timeout 3`), `curlInclude` (`-i`), `curlMarta` and `curlLucia` (`-i -u user:password`), `curlCookie` (`-i -b sesion=s1`) and the chip of the `browser`; their labels start with the term `curl` (or are the term `browser`), as every message chip names a term. `ORDER_BODY` holds the bodies the orders answer with (`Pedido 7: 2 cafés.`, `Pedido 8: 1 té.`, the orders 9 and 10 that two equal posts create and the order 7 once a put replaced it).
- `URLS_BY_PARTS` are the eight address chips of 03-01, given by parts (scheme `http`, name `localhost`, port 3000 or 80, path `/pedidos` or `/pedido`, query string `id=7` or `id=8`, written without `?`): the whole product of the values offered, because the strip of the terminal writes the url by picking one value per part and the chip it stands for is the one with those parts (`src/game/view/cards/web/url-strip.ts`). A missing combination would leave the line incomplete. From 03-02 the chips give their whole `url` (`ORDER_URL`).
- `bodyTask` is the task of a message from the terminal of `laptop-lucia` (a plain `curl` unless it says another `messageId` or another `origin`) that must come back with exactly this body (`expect.body`), in the last send of those chips or in the nth since the world last changed (`nth`).
- `PAGE_PIECES` is the list of pieces 03-01 and 03-02 draw: `laptop-lucia`, whose firewall is drawn with it. `LAN_PIECES` is the list of 03-03 and 03-05: `tu-laptop` and `laptop-lucia`, each with its firewall.
- The ids of the 65 terms of the chapter, with their names and sub-chapters, are the ladder data of the tests (`LADDER_OF_CHAPTER_3`, `src/testing/web/`); `src/game/chapters/chapter-3.test.ts` checks what each incident introduces against it and that no text says a name of a later sub-chapter.
- Not here: the contract and the chips (`src/levels/contract/`), the registry (`src/levels/`), visible UI text and the reasons the box shows (`src/copy/`), the network rules (`src/engine/`).
