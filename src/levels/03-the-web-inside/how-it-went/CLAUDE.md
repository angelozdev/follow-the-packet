# levels/03-the-web-inside/how-it-went

Block 2 of chapter 3, «Cómo salió y quién eres»: the incidents that teach how to read the status code of a response and who generated it. 03-05 and 03-06 are built. `chapter-3-dead-ends.test.ts` checks that no sequence of up to three moves leaves a built incident with a task that can never hold.

- `05-how-did-it-go.ts` (title «¿Cómo salió?»): a `send` incident from `tu-laptop` to the orders app of `laptop-lucia`, with one message, `curl -i` (`MESSAGE.curlInclude`), and four whole urls by her IP (`STATUS_URL`: `/pedidos?id=7`, `/pedido?id=7`, `/pedidos?id=siete` and `/pedidos?id=99`), so the terminal draws the plain tray «Destino». It introduces `status-code`, `status-200`, `client-error`, `status-404` and `status-400`. Its only change is `APP_CHECKS_THE_ID` (`../shared.ts`): the program of port 3000 of `laptop-lucia` now checks the `id` of an order; the report names `laptop-lucia` and says so, the takeaway says why the misspelled id gives 400 and not the 404 it gave before, and `changedNames` of the two worlds is exactly `[laptop-lucia]`. It starts from the solved world of 03-04, so the firewall of `laptop-lucia` keeps the rule `TCP · 3000` from the block of the first floor and there is no rules card. It draws `LAN_PIECES`: `tu-laptop` and `laptop-lucia`, each with its firewall.
  - Three tasks (`statusTask`, `expect.statusCode`): the order 7 comes back with 200, the path the app does not serve with 404 and the id `siete` with 400. The fourth url is the contrast: `id=99` names no order, so it is a 404 like the wrong path, and no task asks for it. Before the change the id `siete` gave 404 (the apps of 03-01 to 03-04 check no id).
  - The box shows, from this incident on, «Código de estado» (the code behind its own term and its class) and «Quién lo generó» (the device whose app answered: `laptop-lucia` for the four urls), and the reason `web-answered` names the code behind its term. They come from the trace of the send; the app is the only device that can generate a code in this block.
  - The right explanation is the third option (the sheet: 05 = 3rd): «¿Qué dice la primera cifra de un 404?» The wrong ones are «Que el servidor falló…» (it names no 5xx code: those arrive in 08, so its feedback says only that another class says it, which comes later) and «Que siempre es culpa del cliente…» (a 4xx seems an error of the client and not always is one). The three options are close in length and the right one is neither the longest nor the shortest, as `chapter-3.test.ts` asks.
  - The takeaway says what the sheet's «Rigor» asks: a 200 says the server met the request, not that the business went well; a 4xx seems an error of the client and not always is one; 404 and 400 each with its cause; and that «Quién lo generó» names the app. It names no 5xx code and no later term, and `chapter-3.test.ts` checks that no text says a 4xx is always the fault of the client or that a 200 means the business went well (the text of the wrong option that holds the first claim is the one text left out).
- `06-who-you-are-and-what-you-can.ts` (title «Quién eres y qué puedes»): a `send` incident from `tu-laptop` to the `/admin` page of the orders app of `laptop-lucia`, with seven messages and one whole url by her IP (`ADMIN_URL`): four for the terminal, `curl -i` with no credential, `curl -i -u marta:…`, `curl -i -u lucia:…` and `curl -i -b sesion=s1`, and three for the browser, the one that opens an address and the two that sign in (`browserLucia` and `browserMarta`, whose labels «Lucía» and «Marta» are the chips of the tray «Usuario»; the terminal does not offer a browser message). It introduces `authentication`, `authorization`, `authorization-header`, `status-401`, `status-403` and `cookie`. Its only change is `APP_HAS_USERS` (`../shared.ts`): the program of port 3000 of `laptop-lucia`, with the pages and the check of the id that 03-05 left it, now serves `/admin` (a page that asks for the `admin` role) and `/login` (where a form with a user and a password opens a session) and knows two users, Lucía (`admin`) and Marta (`staff`); the report names `laptop-lucia` and says so, and `changedNames` of the two worlds is exactly `[laptop-lucia]`. It starts from the solved world of 03-05, so before the users `/admin` is a path the app does not serve and gets the 404. It draws `LAN_PIECES`.
  - Four tasks. Three are `statusTask` (`expect.statusCode` with the message chip it says): no credential gets 401, the credential of Marta gets 403 and the one of Lucía gets 200. Repeating the request of Marta gives the same 403 (the engine decides from the request alone). The fourth is `bodyTask` (`expect.body`): the browser of `tu-laptop` opens `/admin` and gets the page of the administration, which holds only after signing in as Lucía (as Marta it gets the 403 and its body). The player signs in from the tab «Navegador» (the tray «Usuario» and «Iniciar sesión»), which sends the `POST` to `/login` with the form of the user; the box shows the `Set-Cookie: sesion=s1` of the response and then the `Cookie: sesion=s1` of the next request. `curl` keeps nothing: without `-b` its request carries no cookie and gets the 401. `curl -i -b sesion=s1` sends the cookie of the first session the app opened since the world last changed: 200 if Lucía signed in first, 403 if Marta did, 401 if nobody did, so no text promises 200 (`chapter-3.test.ts`).
  - The box shows the `Authorization: Basic …` of the request (between `Host` and `User-Agent`, as `curl` 8.21.0 writes it), the `WWW-Authenticate` header of the 401, the code behind its own term and «Quién lo generó» (`laptop-lucia`); the reasons `web-needs-login` (who asks: the app does not know who the request comes from) and `web-forbidden` (what is allowed: the app knows the user and the authorization of the page does not let that user in) come from the trace.
  - The right explanation is the first option (the sheet: 06 = 1st): «Marta recibió un 403. ¿Le sirve repetir la petición con la misma credencial?» The wrong ones are «Sí: un 403 es temporal…» and «Sí: un 403 dice que la contraseña de Marta está mal.» (that is a 401), with feedback that says why.
  - The takeaway says what the sheet's «Rigor» asks: authentication is knowing who asks and authorization is deciding what that user can do; `Authorization` and `Unauthorized` speak of authentication; a 404 can hide a 403 (RFC 9110 §15.5.4 says a server may answer 404 to hide that a forbidden page exists); in AWS the number alone is not enough and one must see who generated it, naming no service; and the cookie is what ties the requests of a browser to one sign-in and `curl` only sends one it is given with `-b`; and a credential over HTTP can be read by anyone who sees the path, without naming base64, encryption, TLS or HTTPS (those arrive in 10). No text says the word «stateless» (or «sin estado») or that a 403 is temporary or means a wrong password (`chapter-3.test.ts`). The cookie of the game is a name and a value: `Secure`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite`, expiry and domain are for chapter 20. The browser of the game does not open the box for the user and the password that a real one opens before a 401 with `WWW-Authenticate`: the sign-in is a tray and a button, and the box of a 401 for the browser says so.
