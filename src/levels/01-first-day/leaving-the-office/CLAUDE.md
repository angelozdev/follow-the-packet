# levels/01-first-day/leaving-the-office

Block 3 of chapter 1, «Salir de la oficina»: the incidents that show what lives inside a local network, what takes a packet to another one, what a device needs to know to send it there, and what happens to a packet once it leaves the office. All five are `send` incidents with messages sent from your laptop (`ORIGIN.you`).

- `06-your-network-and-the-router.ts`: `TWO_FLOORS` (in `../shared.ts`) adds the second floor (`laptop-andres`, `192.168.2.15`), the office router `router-oficina` (`192.168.1.1` on your floor and `192.168.2.1` on the other) and the default gateway of every device. The report says what that does in plain words, naming both floors from `FLOOR` («Cada dispositivo del Piso 1 y del Piso 2 le manda a router-oficina lo que va a otra red local»); `explained-changes.test.ts` accepts the name of floor 1 in place of the five devices whose gateway is set there. The player pings the printer (straight, in your local network) and `laptop-andres` (through the router, so the box shows «Pasó por»); a third address, `laptop-lucia`, is there to compare. The box of `router-oficina` shows its IP on each floor. It introduces `lan` and `router`. The rule «aquí la red es 192.168.1.x» is given in the report, with no mask. No text says `salto`, `TTL` or `puerta de enlace predeterminada`: those arrive later.
- `07-who-gets-whats-outside.ts`: a `setGateway` takes the gateway of your laptop away («reinstalaron tu laptop»). The player pings `laptop-andres` and sees it does not leave your laptop, then sets the gateway in the «Ajustes de red» card of your laptop, a tray of chips (`Ninguna`, the IP `router-oficina` has on your floor, `192.168.1.1`, which its box shows, the printer `192.168.1.21` and the unowned `192.168.1.50`) that is not in its terminal. The printer as a gateway receives the packet and does not forward it (it is not a router), and an IP nobody has makes your laptop warn. It introduces `default-gateway`, a saved setting of the device. The wrong option that blames the router does not say it was off: it says it was not working, and the feedback refutes it with what the map shows (the packet never left your laptop).
- `08-the-network-of-networks.ts`: `INTERNET` adds the server of the internet (`198.51.100.50`), the router of the provider (`203.0.113.1`) and the one in front of the server (`203.0.113.2`), and the way from the office router to them. The player pings the server (it comes back) and `10.0.0.10`, the printer of another office (it does not): the first router outside the office drops a private IP with no warning. Only the office of 01-06 and the server are drawn (`INTERNET_PIECES`); the routers of the provider are in the world and not on the map, so nothing in 01-08, the box of the lost ping included, names them (the private IP is explained as one that «muchas redes» use): the ping «murió afuera de la oficina» and its stamp stands on the road between `router-oficina` and the globe. No text says how the packet leaves the office, and none says NAT or that a laptop has a public IP. It introduces `internet`, `private-ip` and `public-ip` (the server's IP is the public one the report shows, and its box calls it «IP pública» once the term is known). The task about `10.0.0.10` names nobody (`by`), because the router that drops it is not drawn. The report says that someone changed the configuration of `router-oficina` (it now sends towards internet everything that is not the office's), the one change the player can see, and names no router of the provider.
- `09-counting-hops.ts`: `LOOP` makes the router of the provider send everything it does not know back to `router-oficina`, which sends it on again: the loop is given and the player configures no routes. The report opens with the cause (`LOOP_CAUSE`: «Alguien cambió la configuración de router-proveedor: ahora todo lo que recibe se lo devuelve a router-oficina»), because a loop with no visible cause looked like magic; the right explanation gives the whole chain (the office router passes the packet on because that is the way out, the router of the provider hands it back, each router decides only by the destination IP and does not remember the packet went through it, and the fault is the configuration, never a router that «se confundió»), and the takeaway adds that a packet goes round when two routers are configured so that each one sends it to the other. No text says «ruta» or «tabla de rutas»: chapter 2 teaches them. The messages are pings with a TTL of 1, 2, 3, 5 and 64 (`PING_WITH_TTL`; the terminal writes `ping -t 3 198.51.100.50`). With TTL 1 `router-oficina` warns, with 2 `router-proveedor`, with 3 `router-oficina` again, and with 64 the packet goes round until a router drops it at 0. It draws your laptop, the two routers of the loop, the globe and the server (`LOOP_PIECES`); the router in front of the server is in the world and no packet crosses it. It introduces `hop`, `ttl` and `time-exceeded`. The report says «Cada router le resta 1» (the registry definition of `ttl` keeps the RFC's «al menos 1»), and the box writes the TTL line as «TTL al salir: 3», since it is the number the packet left with.
- `10-seeing-the-path.ts`: `BROKEN_STRETCH` takes the loop away and leaves the router of the provider without a way to the server and without a warning. The ping to the server ends in silence and a `traceroute -I 198.51.100.50` lists `192.168.1.1`, `203.0.113.1` and then stars up to the game's 8 hops. It draws your laptop (the only device of the office), the three routers and the server (`PATH_PIECES`): a traceroute prints the IP of every router that warns, so each of those is drawn, and `router-destino`, which no probe crosses, is drawn because the ficha lists it (the broken stretch is the one between it and `router-proveedor`). The stamp of the lost ping stands on `router-proveedor`, the router that drops it. The right explanation says the broken stretch is very probably after the last router that answered, because from there nothing answers up to the last hop and the ping does not come back; it adds that a router that does not warn is not always the break. Once the player has answered, it also tells the cause the traceroute only suggests: someone changed the configuration of `router-proveedor`, which no longer knows where to send what goes to the server and does not warn (`BROKEN_STRETCH`). No text reads a `*` as the place where the packet died: a router may forward the packet and not send Time Exceeded (`man traceroute` on macOS, checked 2026-10-10), and `chapter-1.test.ts` scans the texts of 01-10 and 01-14 and the traceroute copy for it. The traceroute is a packet of its own on the map («traceroute de tu-laptop a 198.51.100.50», not a ping), and its box defines `traceroute` and shows no TTL. It introduces `traceroute`. The task about the ping names nobody (`by`), but its box does: it lists «Saltos» (`router-oficina`, `router-proveedor`) and says that `router-proveedor` drops the packet and does not warn, so the first ping already gives away what the traceroute shows (a decision for the student: see the report of the task).
