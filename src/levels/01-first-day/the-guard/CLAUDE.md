# levels/01-first-day/the-guard

Block 4 of chapter 1, «El guardia»: the incidents that show what a firewall does to the packets of a device and how the player tells it what to let through. Three incidents, 01-11 to 01-13.

- `11-the-guard.ts`: a `send` incident with a ping message sent from your laptop (`ORIGIN.you`) to `laptop-marta` and to `laptop-lucia`. `REPAIRED_STRETCH` (in `../shared.ts`) gives the provider router its way to the server back, as in 01-08, so the world continues from 01-10 solved; no packet of 01-11 crosses a router, so its report says it in plain words («el proveedor arregló el tramo roto de «Ver el camino»: router-proveedor volvió a su configuración de antes»). The firewall `HIDDEN_FIREWALL` gave `laptop-marta` in 01-05 is the same one: the ping to `laptop-marta` has the same trace as in 01-05 and only its words change, because the player now knows the term (the stamp is the blocked one, in front of the firewall, and the reason says the firewall dropped the packet without warning). It introduces `firewall`, `traffic` and `drop`.
- `12-your-first-rule.ts`: a `filter` incident on the firewall of `laptop-marta`, which lets nothing in until the player writes a rule. Two flows run on it: the ping of support (`laptop-lucia`, required) and the ping of the visitor (`laptop-visita`, forbidden), as one task. The Type chip is `ICMP · Echo Request` and the Source chips are `Cualquier IP de origen` (term `source-ip`) and `laptop-lucia (IP 192.168.1.11)`; it offers no Destination chip, so the card has no tabs and the player writes inbound rules only. The firewall has only rules that allow: no text offers a rule that denies, and the visitor stays out because what no rule allows is denied by default (`default-deny`), which the question to Lucía asks. It introduces `rule`, `allow`, `deny`, `default-deny` and `least-privilege` (04-02 no longer does). The probe battery finds the opening that «Cualquier IP de origen» leaves and none in the rule for `laptop-lucia`, which earns the middle star. It draws `RULE_PIECES`: the two laptops that send and `laptop-marta` with its firewall. No text uses the words entrada and salida, which 01-13 introduces.
- `13-in-and-out.ts`: a `filter` incident on the firewall of your laptop (`YOUR_FIREWALL`, with no rule in either direction: someone set the laptop up so, and no text says that a firewall closes a side by default). It offers Source and Destination chips, so the card shows the tabs «entrada» and «salida», and the player must write an outbound rule for `laptop-lucia`; an inbound rule leaves the ping stopped at the same firewall, and the reason says that the outbound side was set closed. The rule of 01-12 goes on working: `FLOW.background` is its ping as an `ambient` flow, so the probe battery does not count it as an opening beyond what the incident needs (without it 01-13 would lose the middle star), and `laptop-marta` is drawn for it (`GUARD_PIECES`). The reply of the ping comes back through the firewall with no inbound rule and no text says why. It introduces `inbound` and `outbound`. Its texts never write «regla de entrada» or «regla de salida», which are the names of the Security Group terms of chapter 4.
- It draws only what each incident lists: the printer and the routers stay in the world and are not drawn, because no packet of these incidents uses them. The globe of the internet is not drawn either: with a list it needs a listed piece outside the office, and none of these is.
- 01-11 defines the firewall with no word about rules and no direction: its box says only what a firewall is, and the reason says the firewall dropped the packet without warning. The texts state the mechanism and cite no reason for it: none says why a firewall drops instead of answering with an error, why the reply of a ping passes through one, or that the laptop is switched off (the last appears only as the wrong option the feedback refutes).
- The right explanation is the second option in 01-11, the first in 01-12 and the third in 01-13: never the one of the incident before.
