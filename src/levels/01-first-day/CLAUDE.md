# levels/01-first-day

Chapter 1, «Tu primer día» (level 0, Bases), your first day at the office with Lucía, in fourteen sub-chapters grouped in five blocks, each in its own folder (`where-you-are/`, `the-ping/`, `leaving-the-office/`, `the-guard/` and `first-watch/`): one file per incident (`NN-<slug>.ts`, a default export built with `defineIncident`), and `shared.ts` with the chapter's lesson, the base world (floor 1 of the office, a local network `192.168.1.0/24` of its own inside the office range `192.168.0.0/16`: your laptop, Lucía's, Marta's, the printer and the visitor's laptop with no cable, each naming its floor), the second floor, the office router and the gateways that 01-06 adds (`TWO_FLOORS`), what 01-08 to 01-10 add or change in the world (`INTERNET`, `LOOP` with `LOOP_CAUSE`, the sentence that says what changed, `BROKEN_STRETCH` and `REPAIRED_STRETCH`) and what 01-13 adds (`YOUR_FIREWALL`), the firewalls that let nothing in and everything out that laptop-marta gets in 01-05 and laptop-lucia in case 6 of 01-14 (`HIDDEN_FIREWALL` and `LUCIA_FIREWALL`, from one builder, with the rule `letEverythingOut`), what 01-14 does to the outbound side of your laptop (`OPEN_YOUR_OUTBOUND` before its cases and `CLOSE_YOUR_OUTBOUND` in case 7), the lists of pieces each incident draws (`OFFICE_PIECES`, `INTERNET_PIECES`, `LOOP_PIECES`, `PATH_PIECES`, `GUARD_PIECES`, `RULE_PIECES` and, for the cases of 01-14, `WATCH_GATEWAY_PIECES`, `WATCH_PRINTER_PIECES`, `WATCH_SECOND_FLOOR_PIECES` and `WATCH_FIREWALL_PIECES`), the flows of the filter incidents (`FLOW`) and the chips the incidents share, among them the rule chips of 01-12 and 01-13 (`WHAT`, `FROM` and `TO`).

- 01-01 brings the base world; every later incident brings only its changes over the reference solution of the one before. The chapter is built: the fourteen incidents introduce all the chapter 1 terms, so the bridge `04-01` once had for them (`CHAPTER_1_BRIDGE` in `src/levels/04-iaas/shared.ts`) is empty and a test (`ladder.test.ts`) keeps it so. 01-14 is the only `diagnose` incident: it introduces no term and starts from the world 01-13 leaves plus one change of its own, told in its report (`OPEN_YOUR_OUTBOUND`: the firewall of your laptop lets everything out); each of its seven cases adds its own changes to that world (`first-watch/cases.ts`).
- A device's own name is its hostname (`tu-laptop`, `laptop-lucia`, `impresora`), like the `Name` of an EC2 instance: a proper name with no article, so a generated sentence reads «el firewall de laptop-lucia». The texts written here name a device as its piece shows it, marked with `nameTag` (`laptop-visita`, `laptop-marta`, `tu-laptop`, and «la impresora»), never as «la laptop de la visita».
- Texts tag their terms (`{{packet|paquete}}`) and use only terms introduced by this incident or an earlier one; no text names an AWS term, which arrive in chapter 4.
- Text that follows a change to the world names what changed (`.claude/rules/explained-behavior-changes.md`): the report, the right explanation or the takeaway of the incident, or the report or right cause of a case, says which device or router changed and how, in plain words and with the name from `DEVICE` or `ROUTER`.
- Not here: the incident contract and chips (`src/levels/contract/`), the registry (`src/levels/`), visible UI text (`src/copy/`), network rules (`src/engine/`).
- May import: `@/engine`, `@/copy`, `@/levels/contract/incident` and `./shared` (from a block folder, `@/levels/01-first-day/shared`).
- The base world declares its floor from the start because no action sets the floor of a device or widens the office range: an incident that adds a floor (01-06) can only add a LAN, a device on it and a router. Floor 1 keeps the 1 ms of a hop that a single-LAN office has, so the round trip times of 01-04 do not move. The map draws it as a slab with its label «Piso 1» from 01-01.
- The way out of the office is a black box until chapter 2: `INTERNET` gives the office router an interface towards the router of the provider (`updateRouter` replaces its interfaces, because it cannot face a router that does not exist yet in 01-06) and a default route, and nothing in the world or the texts says how a packet's source changes. The addresses of the provider and the server are documentation addresses (RFC 5737) used as if they were public; the screen never says so. The private IP of the other office (`10.0.0.10`) is outside every LAN of the office. A reply is never routed, so the routes back to the office exist for the warnings: a router warns from its interface towards the sender.
