# levels/01-first-day/first-watch

Block 5 of chapter 1, «Tu primera guardia»: one `diagnose` incident, 01-14, with seven cases. Nothing in it is taught: it introduces no term, and the player diagnoses with the ladder of the fourteen incidents before it.

- `14-final-watch.ts`: the incident. It starts from the world 01-13 leaves (`changes: []`), and it has no `shown` list of its own: each case carries one, because each case is a screen and draws only what it uses (`cases.ts`, with the lists in `../shared.ts`). Cases 1 and 7 draw your laptop, the office router and the server (`WATCH_GATEWAY_PIECES`), case 2 your laptop and the printer (`WATCH_PRINTER_PIECES`), case 3 `laptop-andres`, the router and the printer (`WATCH_SECOND_FLOOR_PIECES`), cases 4 and 5 your laptop, the two routers the packet crosses before it dies or loops and the server (`LOOP_PIECES`: case 4 names the router of the provider, and the traceroute of case 5 prints the IP of both routers), and case 6 your laptop, `laptop-lucia` (its firewall is drawn with it), the printer, the router and the server (`WATCH_FIREWALL_PIECES`). The globe is drawn when the list holds the server. A device no case uses (`laptop-marta`, `laptop-visita`) and the router in front of the server stay in the world and are not drawn. The player changes nothing in the world: a diagnose ends as it started.
- `cases.ts`: the seven cases, built with `watchCase` and `cause` so no two share a shape. Each case is a set of `changes` on the world of 01-13 (never on the case before: `caseWorld`), a report that Lucía says (the card writes `Lucía: «…»`), its own send chips, two hints and one question of cause with three options. The right option sits in a place of its own (`cause` takes the place, 0 to 2): 2nd, 3rd, 1st, 3rd, 1st, 2nd and 3rd across the seven cases, never twice in a row and not where 01-13 had it (3rd).
- Every case other than 3 sends from your laptop, whose firewall is the one 01-13 left: it lets out only the Echo Request to `laptop-lucia`. A case that pings other destinations opens that side again (`OPEN_YOUR_OUTBOUND`, an outbound rule that lets everything out), except case 7, whose cause is that firewall: it takes the rule of 01-13 away (`CLOSE_YOUR_OUTBOUND`), so the outbound side has no rule and the ping to the gateway stops at your own laptop. Case 3 is played from `laptop-andres`, on the second floor, which has no firewall.
- The seven cases, with the evidence the engine produces and each wrong option's refutation: (1) `OPEN_YOUR_OUTBOUND` and no gateway on your laptop; the four chips are the sequence (loopback, your IP, the router, the server): the first three come back and the fourth ends in `no-route` before it leaves. The only guided case: its report asks for the four tests in order. (2) `192.168.1.22`, the IP Lucía wrote down for the printer: your own laptop warns (`host-unreachable` from `192.168.1.10`), and `192.168.1.21` comes back. (3) no gateway on `laptop-andres`: loopback, its IP and `192.168.2.1` come back and the printer on floor 1 ends in `no-route`; the same cause as case 1, with no sequence in the report, from the other floor. (4) the server comes back and `10.0.0.10` (a private IP of another office) is dropped in silence by `router-proveedor`. (5) `LOOP`: every ping to the server ends in `time-exceeded`, with TTL 1, 2 and 3 warning from `router-oficina`, `router-proveedor` and `router-oficina`, and a traceroute whose eight lines alternate `192.168.1.1` and `203.0.113.1`. (6) a firewall on `laptop-lucia` with nothing allowed in: only her ping is silent, with a blocked stamp on her firewall; the printer, the router and the server come back. `laptop-marta` is not a test, because her firewall drops your ping too. (7) the outbound side of your laptop with no rule: loopback and your own IP come back (they never leave the laptop) and the router and the server end blocked on your firewall.
- Rigor of the texts. A report says only what the player sees (a ping that does not come back, a warning): no verb of the web, no `abrir`. No report and no right option names a switched-off device as the cause (a wrong option may, and its feedback refutes it with what the map shows). Each wrong option is a real mistake whose feedback cites the evidence and never claims what the world does not show. A feedback never opens with «No.» or «Eso es.»: the card already prefixes «No es eso.» or «Correcto.». The three options of a case are close in length and form (the longest is under 1.4 times the shortest, and the right one is the longest in at most half of the cases), so the right one does not give itself away; the right option states the cause alone and its feedback adds the reason. Case 7 says only that the loopback and your own IP come back, and gives no reason for it (whether a host firewall filters loopback depends on its configuration). Two traps the ficha's wording had and the code does not repeat: in case 5 a mistyped server IP would also end in `time-exceeded` (the provider router sends everything back), so the refutation is that the IP is the one in the server's box and not that a typo would give another warning; in case 6 the ping to `laptop-lucia` never crosses a router (same floor). Quantifiers are scoped to the case's chips («ningún ping a otro dispositivo», since loopback and your own IP come back). The gateway is always written with its full official name, `puerta de enlace predeterminada` (the first playtest retired «puerta»). No text says a closed outbound side is the default of a firewall.
- The stars (from the mechanic): closed is the seven causes named, the middle star is no hint in any case, and explained is every cause right at the first try. `chapter-1.test.ts` plays the seven cases.
