# levels/02-ports-and-names/which-way

Block 3 of chapter 2, «Qué camino»: the incidents that teach how a device and a router choose where a packet goes next, from its routing table (02-06) and from the routes the player writes in the table of a router (02-07). Task 27 of the plan built both and closed the block.

- `06-your-routing-table.ts` (title «Tu tabla de rutas»): a `send` incident, from your laptop, with `ping` as the only message and the printer and `laptop-andres` as the addresses. Nothing changes in the world (`changes: []`: 02-06 starts from the solution of 02-05). It draws `FLOORS_PIECES` (your laptop, the printer, `laptop-andres` and `router-oficina`) and introduces `route`, `routing-table`, `connected-route` and `default-route`. It offers no table to edit: the card of your laptop gets a read-only tab «Tabla de rutas» as soon as `routing-table` is known (`opensRoutes` in `src/game/view/cards/table-card.ts`: a device that sends and any router), so the card is tabbed («Terminal» first, since the incident names no `opensFirst`) and the report says to open the tab. The router has the same read-only tab, which the sheet does not mention; it is how task 19 built the card of every router, and the next incident edits it. **Known gap, seen on 2026-10-12 and not fixed here:** the read-only table of `router-oficina` in 02-06 lists `203.0.113.1/32 · directo`, the interface of `router-proveedor` (the link is a /32, see below), and `0.0.0.0/0 · fuera de la oficina`, and 02-06 does not draw `router-proveedor`, so the player reads an IP of a router that the map does not show. The text review of block 3 (2026-10-12) judged it a leak by the project's own standard (`routerRef` and `visibleVia` never name a router that is not drawn, by name or by IP), even though the row is a real row of the table. Fixing it means that a router's table leaves out the row of a link to a router the map does not draw, which is a change of `table-card.ts` and of its tests (task 19), so it is not part of this task; the sheet asks only for the table of `tu-laptop`.
  - Two rows, as the engine derives them (`routingTableOf`): `192.168.1.0/24 · directo` and `0.0.0.0/0 · 192.168.1.1 router-oficina`, under the columns «Destino» and «Por dónde sale» (`next-hop` is 02-07's, decided 23 of the plan). The row the last packet used is marked «en uso». The box says which route each holder used: «Ruta que usó: tu-laptop: 192.168.1.0/24 · ruta de red conectada · directo» for the printer and, for `laptop-andres`, the default route of your laptop («sale por 192.168.1.1») and the connected route of the router to the second floor.
  - The first rigor line of the sheet: `0.0.0.0/0` in a route is «lo que ninguna otra ruta cubre», and in a rule of a firewall (02-05) it is «cualquier origen». The report says the first, the takeaway says both, and `chapter-2.test.ts` guards the sentences (`the text of 0.0.0.0/0 in a route says it is what no other route covers`). No text says that the distance or the order of the rows chooses the route: the two wrong options of the question say it, because they are the errors the sheet lists, and their feedback corrects them.
  - The question is «¿Por qué el ping a laptop-andres usó la ruta por defecto?» and the right option is the third (the sheet: 06 = 3rd): no other row covers `192.168.2.15`, so the default route gets it. The three options are 111, 91 and 94 characters of plain text, so the right one is not the longest and the length gives nothing away (`chapter-2.test.ts` checks it).
- `07-a-way-to-the-other-office.ts` (title «Un camino a la otra oficina»): a `send` incident whose table of routes of `router-oficina` the player edits (`OFFICE_ROUTES_TABLE`; its card is the only card of the router, so it opens with no tabs, and the card of your laptop, a terminal and a read-only table, is tabbed). Its changes are `OTHER_OFFICE` (the LAN `10.0.0.0/16`, named «Sucursal» on the map; `impresora-sucursal` and `laptop-sucursal`, both with `router-sucursal` as their gateway; and `router-sucursal`, with its interface `10.0.0.1` towards its LAN, its interface `172.16.0.2` towards `router-oficina` and one route, `192.168.0.0/16` back to `router-oficina`) and `ROUTES_LOST` (`router-oficina` loses every route and gains its interface `172.16.0.1` towards `router-sucursal`; an update replaces the list of interfaces, so it restates the two floors and the way out to the provider, `OFFICE_INTERFACES` of chapter 1). The report names `router-oficina` («Alguien reinició router-oficina y perdió sus rutas»), says that only its connected routes are left (the four «directo» rows the card shows) and that the other office is new, with `router-sucursal`, which the map draws; `router-destino` is in the world, is not drawn and is named nowhere (`chapter-2.test.ts`: `the report names router-oficina and no router of the provider that the map does not draw`). It draws `ROUTES_PIECES`: eight pieces and the firewall of your laptop.
  - Why `router-sucursal` has a route back and no default: with a default route back to `router-oficina`, a ping to the server with `0.0.0.0/0` handed to `router-sucursal` would loop between the two and end in Time Exceeded. With only the way back, it has no route for a public address and warns Destination Net Unreachable from `10.0.0.1`, which is what the sheet says («0.0.0.0/0 → router-sucursal deja al servidor sin camino»). The replies of a ping are not routed (the reply stage has no route step), so the way home of the branch only matters for a packet that is not a reply.
  - What the player sees, measured by `chapter-2.test.ts` in the box and the stamps: with no routes, the three pings end in a warning from `router-oficina` (`192.168.1.1`), «router-oficina no sabe a qué router mandar un paquete para …: lo descarta y avisa a quien lo envió que no pudo entregarlo»; with only `0.0.0.0/0 → router-proveedor`, the server answers and the pings to `10.0.0.10` and `10.0.0.20` die in silence at `router-proveedor` (`forward-private-dropped`, the rule of 01-08, which this task does not touch: a router that faces no LAN and has no connection to a private IP drops it and does not warn); with `10.0.0.0/16 → router-sucursal` too, the three come back, and the box of the pings to the other office lists the route of `router-oficina` as the most specific one («es la más específica: también cubría 0.0.0.0/0»). `10.0.0.10/32 → router-sucursal` serves the printer and leaves `10.0.0.20` without a way, and `0.0.0.0/0 → router-sucursal` serves the other office and leaves the server with a warning from `router-sucursal`. No router that stayed silent in 01-08 warns now: `router-proveedor` is the one of 01-14, untouched, and the only routers that warn are `router-oficina`, with no route that covers the destination, and `router-sucursal`, with the default route of the player handed to it.
  - The rows of the table of a router, as `routingTableOf` derives them: one connected row per interface, and the link to another router is that router's own address as a /32 (an interface has no mask; `PEER_LINK_PREFIX`), so at the start the table of `router-oficina` has `192.168.1.0/24`, `192.168.2.0/24`, `203.0.113.1/32` and `172.16.0.2/32`, all «directo», before the routes the player writes. The /32 rows are the point-to-point links the model has, and the game declares the simplification in `src/engine/network/addressing/CLAUDE.md`; the incident does not mention them. A table keeps one route per destination: writing `0.0.0.0/0` twice gives the problem «Ese destino ya tiene una ruta…» under the table and changes nothing.
  - The chips: Destination `0.0.0.0/0`, `10.0.0.0/16` and `10.0.0.10/32`; Next hop `203.0.113.1 router-proveedor` and `172.16.0.2 router-sucursal` (the IP of the interface of the next router that faces `router-oficina`, with the name of the router, which the map draws). The right pair is `0.0.0.0/0` to the provider and `10.0.0.0/16` to the branch (`routeMove` in `../shared.ts` writes a move). Three tasks: the ping to the server, to the printer of the other office (`by` it) and to its laptop (`by` it). They state the final world (decided 27 of the plan): writing a route clears the stamps of before, so the pings are sent again after the last route, and the report says so.
  - The question is «¿Por qué el ping a 10.0.0.10 va a router-sucursal y no a router-proveedor, si las dos rutas lo cubren?» and the right option is the second (the sheet: 07 = 2nd): `10.0.0.0/16` is more specific than `0.0.0.0/0`, it has the longest prefix. The wrong ones are the sheet's: the last route written wins, and `0.0.0.0/0` is a backup that is used when another route fails; their feedback says that the order does not decide and that `0.0.0.0/0` covers every IP and loses to a more specific route. The three options are 96, 79 and 78 characters, the right one the second longest. The takeaway says that a router with no route for the destination, not even the default one, may warn (RFC 792: «may»), that 0.0.0.0/0 covers every IP and loses to `10.0.0.0/16`, that the next hop is the IP of the side of the next router that faces the one that hands the packet over (`172.16.0.2` of `router-sucursal`), and that `router-proveedor` drops what goes to the private IPs without warning because the internet does not deliver them. It does not say that a router is not obliged to warn: RFC 1812 §4.3.3.1 says it MUST, so the silence of the provider is the game's rule (decided 11), not something the RFC allows for a router with no route at all. The feedback of the third option says that no route fails and that 0.0.0.0/0 is used when no more specific route covers the IP.
  - The map of 02-07 has a layout rule of its own, in `src/scene/layout/` (the 2D map; the 3D layout it used to have in `src/scene/layout.ts` is gone): `router-sucursal` faces a floor the map draws (the LAN of the other office) and comes after `router-oficina`, so it is the gateway of another office (`Drawn.gateways`) and stands in a second group under the office, at the right of the bus of its floors and on the same `x` as the first router, joined to it by the private link, while the main line toward the internet does not move for it (`src/scene/layout/arrange/CLAUDE.md`, «The other office»). `src/ui/incident/map-sweep.test.ts` checks that the map of 02-07 fits the area at 900 px and at 1060 px and that nothing overlaps; the 900 px and 789 px of the 3D drawing no longer apply.
- **Without verifying.** Nothing in these two incidents is a claim about a real router that was not read: the longest prefix is RFC 4632 §5.1 («Forwarding in the Internet is done on a longest-match basis»), the warning is RFC 792 (a router «may» send it) and the text of the warning in the box is the game's. RFC 1812 §4.3.3.1 (read again on 2026-10-12 with `curl` on `rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1812.txt`): «If a router cannot forward a packet because it has no routes at all (including no default route) to the destination specified in the packet, then the router MUST generate a Destination Unreachable, Code 0 (Network Unreachable) ICMP message.» The text says «puede avisar» (true under both) and never that a router may stay silent.
