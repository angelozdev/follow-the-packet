# levels/02-ports-and-names/ports-and-rules

Block 2 of chapter 2, «Rechazo, bloque y regla»: the incidents that teach what a port answers when it does not accept a connection (02-03), which packets belong to a local network's block (02-04) and how a firewall rule names protocol, ports and origin (02-05). Task 25 of the plan built 02-03; task 26 built 02-04 and 02-05 and closed the block.

- `03-refused-or-silent.ts` (title «Rechazo o silencio», the title of the approved design and of the spec; the brief of the task wrote «¿Rechazo o silencio?», and the roadmap and the other incidents carry no question marks): a `send` incident with two terminals, your laptop (`ORIGIN.you`) and `laptop-lucia` (`ORIGIN.lucia`), and `nc` as the only tool: `MESSAGE.ncApp` («nc · puerto 3000») and `MESSAGE.ncMysql` («nc · puerto 3306»). The addresses are `laptop-lucia`, `laptop-marta` and `127.0.0.1` (`ADDRESS.loopback`). Its only change is `APP_ON_LOOPBACK`: Lucía restarted her app and it listens only on `127.0.0.1`, while her database keeps listening on every IP. The report names `laptop-lucia` and says what changed, as `explained-behavior-changes` asks, and nothing else of the office changes. It draws `REFUSAL_PIECES` (your laptop, `laptop-lucia` and `laptop-marta`, with the firewalls those two of them have). It introduces `closed-port`, `rst`, `connection-refused`, `filtered-port` and `listen-address`.
- Three causes of «no conecta», one for each test, and what the player sees in each (all of it is the engine and the views of earlier tasks; this incident only chooses the tests):
  - The 3306 of `laptop-lucia`: nobody listens, so she answers with an RST (`host-refused`). The map stamps it «Llegó.» (✓), the box lists `SYN` and then `RST` in «Saludo de tres pasos», and the output card prints `Ncat: Connection refused.`.
  - The 3000 of `laptop-lucia` from your laptop: the app listens, but only on `127.0.0.1`, and the packet goes to her IP of the network, so she answers with an RST as well (`host-wrong-address`). Same stamp, same two steps, same `Ncat: Connection refused.`; only the box differs: «Dónde y por qué se detuvo» says that the service of the port has `127.0.0.1` as its listen address and that the packet goes to `192.168.1.11`.
  - The 3000 of `laptop-marta`: her firewall drops it (`firewall-in-drop`), nothing answers. The map stamps it «Bloqueado.» (✕, the firewall is known since 01-11), the box lists only the `SYN`, and the output card prints `Ncat: TIMEOUT.`. A refusal and a silence therefore differ on the map (the stamp) and in the box (the second step and the line of where it stopped), and `chapter-2.test.ts` checks both (`a refusal and a silence leave different stamps`).
  - What the output prints, and the source of each line, is in `src/copy/tools/CLAUDE.md` (`ncat_connect.c` of Nmap 7.93, read on 2026-10-11).
- Four tasks, in the order of the sheet: `TASK.closedPort` (3306 refused by `laptop-lucia`), `TASK.filteredPort` (3000 to `laptop-marta` is a `timeout`, `by` the firewall `FIREWALL.marta`), `TASK.ownMachine` (the `nc` of `laptop-lucia` to `127.0.0.1`, port 3000, comes back; it is the only task whose origin is not your laptop, built with `sendTaskFrom` and `sendMoveFrom` of `../shared.ts`) and `TASK.fromTheNetwork` (the `nc` of your laptop to `192.168.1.11:3000` comes back). The tasks state the final world (decided 27 of the plan): choosing «Todas las IPs» clears the sends, as every change of the world does (decided 5), so the first three tasks hold in the world before and in the world after the fix and the player makes the four sends again after it. The report tells the player to try the 3000 first, because the question is about that refusal and its stamp is gone after the fix, and says that changing the listen address starts the tests again; the reference solution puts the `setSetting` first and then the four sends, and `chapter-2.test.ts` checks that sends made before the fix do not count after it (`a pick of the listen address clears the stamps of before, so the four sends are made after it`).
- The setting is `APP_LISTEN_ADDRESS` (`../shared.ts`): the listen address of the app of `laptop-lucia`, TCP 3000, offered as a tray named by the term (`dirección de escucha`) with two chips, «Solo este dispositivo (127.0.0.1)» (`loopback`, the chip that matches the world at the start) and «Todas las IPs (0.0.0.0)» (`all`). It sits on `laptop-lucia`, which is also an origin, so her panel has the tabs «Terminal» and «Ajustes», and «Servicios» is a section of «Ajustes»; the incident sets no `opensFirst`, so the terminal opens first and the report says to open the tab «Ajustes», section «Servicios». The label of a chip says its address in words, which is why `the settings of a send incident` in `src/levels/ladder.test.ts` now asks a listen address chip to name its address (`127.0.0.1` or `0.0.0.0`) instead of asking, as for a gateway, for a label that is the value: the value of this setting is `loopback` or `all`, which the player does not read.
- The question is «¿Por qué laptop-lucia rechazó tu nc al puerto 3000, si ahí tiene un servicio?» and the right option is the third (the sheet: 03 = 3rd): the app listens only on `127.0.0.1` and refuses what goes to another IP of the laptop. The wrong options are the ones of the sheet: a firewall that dropped the packet (confuses a refusal with a silence: here `laptop-lucia` answered with an RST) and a closed port (a closed port is one where nobody listens; here someone does, on another IP). The right option names the new term (`dirección de escucha`) and is scoped to the packet of your laptop («tu paquete iba a otra IP»), as the wrong option about the closed port names `puerto cerrado`; the three options are about the same length (88, 89 and 82 characters of plain text, the right one the shortest), which `chapter-2.test.ts` checks. The e2e helper picks the right option by «y tu paquete iba a otra IP».
- No text says that a closed port and a service on another address are the same: the RST and `Connection refused` come from both, and the texts say so with the word «también» or «rechaza igual» where they say it at all, never that the causes are one. The takeaway keeps it exact: a closed port answers with an RST, an app that listens only on `127.0.0.1` also gets an RST when another device, «como tu-laptop», sends a packet to another IP, a filtered port does not answer, and a `Connection refused` with the app running means looking at the IP it listens on. `chapter-2.test.ts` guards it by sentence (`no text says that a closed port and a service on another address are the same`). No text gives a reason a reply comes back (decided 16 of the plan; `no text says why a reply comes back`).
- **Without verifying.** The behavior of a service on `127.0.0.1` that gets a packet to the IP of the network that comes from its own laptop is reasoning from `ip(7)` and not something tried on a machine (decided 8 of the plan; the spec's «Sin verificar»). It is reachable here: the terminal of `laptop-lucia` offers her own IP as an address, and the game answers with the same RST. The texts of this incident never claim it: the right option and the takeaway talk about the packet of another device («tu paquete», «otro dispositivo, como tu-laptop»), never about a packet of `laptop-lucia` to her own IP of the network. The definition of `rst` says «en una IP y un puerto donde nadie escucha» so that it covers this refusal too. If the student tries it in an EC2 and it differs, the rule of the engine and this note change.
- No text names a term a later incident of the chapter introduces (the report says «dirección de escucha» because 02-03 introduces it, and nothing of port ranges, routes, SSH or DNS).
- `04-is-it-in-my-block.ts` (title «¿Está en mi bloque?», as the sheet writes it): an `identify` incident (D-6 = A: nothing travels, so `flows` is empty and `changes` is `[]`, the second exception to «algo viaja»). One card, the one of your laptop, which the piece opens as two tabs inside its tab «Preguntas» (`pieceCard`): «Responder» (`BLOCK_CARD.block` and `BLOCK_CARD.mask`, three chips each: `192.168.1.0/24`, `/28` and `/32`, and `255.255.255.0`, `255.255.0.0` and `255.255.255.255`) and «Clasificar» (the card that classifies, `identify.classify`: the block with its mask, the 32 bits of the IP of your laptop with the first 24 filled, `impresora` and `laptop-andres` split at the prefix, and the bins «Dentro de 192.168.1.0/24» and «Fuera»). Four tasks, one per answer: the block, the mask, the printer inside and laptop-andres outside. It draws `FLOORS_PIECES`, and the outline of the block follows what the player placed: your laptop is inside from the start, each item put in the first bin is inside, and the map writes `192.168.1.0/24` at the corner where the outline ends (`classifiedBlock`).
  - **The sheet left one thing open: the card that classifies always shows the block, the mask and the filled bits, so the two questions of the other tab can be answered by reading it.** Decided here, taking the approved prototype at face value (it gives the block): `cidr` is introduced by this very incident, so no player can answer from memory, and the incident teaches the notation by reading it. The report frames «Clasificar» as where the block (in CIDR notation), its subnet mask and the bits of the IP are, and «Responder» as choosing the block and the mask; the report does not explain `cidr` and `subnet-mask` in plain sentences (it tags them; the card, the feedback of the chips and the takeaway say what they are) and keeps its two plain explanations for `bit` and `prefix`; the two tasks say «quede elegido» and carry no digits; the wrong chips teach what the right one does not (a /28 reaches only `192.168.1.15`, so the printer, `192.168.1.21`, does not fit; a /32 is one IP; a /16 and a /32 mask are other prefixes). Nothing hides the answer behind a step of the card, because that needs a field of the contract that no task has asked for.
  - The lines under each chip appear when the player presses «Listo» in «Responder» (the card of chapter 1 does the same); the line under a placed item appears as soon as it is placed. Rigor: a /24 is 256 addresses and the texts say «direcciones» and never «equipos»; the IP is split in text only because /24 ends at a dot.
  - The question is «¿Por qué 192.168.2.15 no cabe en el bloque 192.168.1.0/24?» and the right option is the second (the sheet: 04 = 2nd): the 24 first bits of that IP are not the block's. The wrong ones are the sheet's: a /24 «deja solo 24 IPs» (the prefix is not a count; the other 8 bits give 256 addresses) and «2 es mayor que 1» (the bits have to be the same, not larger or smaller: 192.168.0.15 does not fit either). The three options are 66, 60 and 68 characters, and the two wrong ones use `prefijo` or the numbers of the IP and state the wrong rule («deja solo 24 IPs», «debe ser menor»), so the right one (which e2e picks by its first words) is neither the longest nor the only one with a new term.
- `05-the-whole-rule.ts` (title «La regla completa»): a `filter` incident on the firewall of `laptop-lucia`, which `SSH_AND_FIREWALL` puts there with SSH on the 22 and no inbound rule. Type chips (`SSH_WHAT`): `TCP · 22`, `TCP · 80`, `TCP · 1-1023`, `TCP · 0-65535` and `ICMP · Echo Request`; Source chips (`SSH_FROM`): your laptop (a /32), Cualquier IP de origen, the block of the first floor and laptop-lucia. The task holds when your laptop gets in on the 22 and the visitor does not; the middle star is for opening only that (`TCP · 22` from your laptop). `TCP · 22` from Cualquier IP de origen, and from the block of the first floor, let the visitor in (192.168.1.30 is inside it) and raise the intruder alert; `TCP · 1-1023` and `TCP · 0-65535` open the 80 and the 443 too (and the 3306 and the 5432, for the second), which the probe battery finds. Every range chip holds a port the battery tries (decided 19 of the plan, and the spec's declared simplification: the battery does not see a range outside 22, 80, 443, 3306 and 5432, so a chip such as `TCP · 20-25` would pass for «just right» without being so) and none is UDP or the 53, which arrives with DNS.
  - **The sheet's three pieces and two flows did not leave the middle star reachable.** Measured on 2026-10-12: with the exact rule (`TCP · 22` from your laptop) the probe battery still found one extra opening, the ping of laptop-lucia to laptop-marta, which the rule 01-12 made the player write on the firewall of laptop-marta lets in and which the world of 01-14 keeps (the chapter starts from the solution of the last incident of chapter 1). Decided here, as 01-13 did: the incident has a third flow, ambient (`SSH_FLOW.background`, the ping of 01-13), so the rule counts as expected, and it draws laptop-marta (`SSH_PIECES` holds four devices, not the three of the sheet) so the flow has somewhere to go; the report says that her rule «sigue en su sitio». Drawing a flow whose destination is not drawn would put a stamp of a ping that arrives at nothing beside laptop-lucia. The alternative, taking the rule of laptop-marta away with a change of the world, would need the report to name a second device that the sheet does not change.
  - `world.test.ts` asks every rule of every firewall of the world to be nameable with the chips of the incident, so the Source chips offer laptop-lucia (the Source of the rule of laptop-marta; its Type is `ICMP · Echo Request`). It is a fourth Source chip that opens nothing the incident cares about.
  - The rigor lines of the sheet, and what changed: `default-port` and `well-known-ports` are told as the plan decided (D-18). The 22 is the default port of SSH, a custom that opens nothing; the text that names `1-1023` says that it «cae dentro de los puertos conocidos» (the well-known ports are 0 to 1023, so 1 to 1023 falls inside them) and says nothing about port 0; no text calls the 3306 or the 5432 a well-known port; no text names the 53. **The sheet's warning that `0.0.0.0/0` in a route means something else is not written:** «ruta» is the term 02-06 introduces, and the ladder (`a text of an incident names no term that a later incident of chapter 2 introduces`) rejects it here; 02-06 can say it.
  - The question is «Si pones la regla TCP · 22 desde Cualquier IP de origen, ¿por qué entra la visita?» (a condition, as 01-13 asks, because a player who wrote the exact rule never saw that one) and the right option is the first (the sheet: 05 = 1st): `0.0.0.0/0` means any origin and the visitor is one. The wrong ones are the sheet's: the 22 is a well-known port and anyone can use it (a custom opens nothing, the rule does) and a TCP rule looks only at the port range, not at the origin (every rule has a Source). The right option is not the longest (76, 80 and 81 characters), and each wrong one uses a term the incident introduces (`puertos conocidos`, `rango de puertos`). The report says which part of the rule goes in Type (protocol and port range) and which in Source, and why `laptop-marta` is on the map: her rule that lets in the ping of `laptop-lucia` goes on as it was.
