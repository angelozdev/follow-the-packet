# levels/02-ports-and-names/ports-and-rules

Block 2 of chapter 2, «Rechazo, bloque y regla»: the incidents that teach what a port answers when it does not accept a connection (02-03), which packets belong to a local network's block (02-04) and how a firewall rule names protocol, ports and origin (02-05). Task 25 of the plan builds 02-03; 02-04 and 02-05 arrive with task 26.

- `03-refused-or-silent.ts` (title «Rechazo o silencio», the title of the approved design and of the spec; the brief of the task wrote «¿Rechazo o silencio?», and the roadmap and the other incidents carry no question marks): a `send` incident with two terminals, your laptop (`ORIGIN.you`) and `laptop-lucia` (`ORIGIN.lucia`), and `nc` as the only tool: `MESSAGE.ncApp` («nc · puerto 3000») and `MESSAGE.ncMysql` («nc · puerto 3306»). The addresses are `laptop-lucia`, `laptop-marta` and `127.0.0.1` (`ADDRESS.loopback`). Its only change is `APP_ON_LOOPBACK`: Lucía restarted her app and it listens only on `127.0.0.1`, while her database keeps listening on every IP. The report names `laptop-lucia` and says what changed, as `explained-behavior-changes` asks, and nothing else of the office changes. It draws `REFUSAL_PIECES` (your laptop, `laptop-lucia` and `laptop-marta`, with the firewalls those two of them have). It introduces `closed-port`, `rst`, `connection-refused`, `filtered-port` and `listen-address`.
- Three causes of «no conecta», one for each test, and what the player sees in each (all of it is the engine and the views of earlier tasks; this incident only chooses the tests):
  - The 3306 of `laptop-lucia`: nobody listens, so she answers with an RST (`host-refused`). The map stamps it «Llegó.» (✓), the box lists `SYN` and then `RST` in «Saludo de tres pasos», and the output card prints `Ncat: Connection refused.`.
  - The 3000 of `laptop-lucia` from your laptop: the app listens, but only on `127.0.0.1`, and the packet goes to her IP of the network, so she answers with an RST as well (`host-wrong-address`). Same stamp, same two steps, same `Ncat: Connection refused.`; only the box differs: «Dónde y por qué se detuvo» says that the service of the port has `127.0.0.1` as its listen address and that the packet goes to `192.168.1.11`.
  - The 3000 of `laptop-marta`: her firewall drops it (`firewall-in-drop`), nothing answers. The map stamps it «Bloqueado.» (✕, the firewall is known since 01-11), the box lists only the `SYN`, and the output card prints `Ncat: TIMEOUT.`. A refusal and a silence therefore differ on the map (the stamp) and in the box (the second step and the line of where it stopped), and `chapter-2.test.ts` checks both (`a refusal and a silence leave different stamps`).
  - What the output prints, and the source of each line, is in `src/copy/tools/CLAUDE.md` (`ncat_connect.c` of Nmap 7.93, read on 2026-10-11).
- Four tasks, in the order of the sheet: `TASK.closedPort` (3306 refused by `laptop-lucia`), `TASK.filteredPort` (3000 to `laptop-marta` is a `timeout`, `by` the firewall `FIREWALL.marta`), `TASK.ownMachine` (the `nc` of `laptop-lucia` to `127.0.0.1`, port 3000, comes back; it is the only task whose origin is not your laptop, built with `sendTaskFrom` and `sendMoveFrom` of `../shared.ts`) and `TASK.fromTheNetwork` (the `nc` of your laptop to `192.168.1.11:3000` comes back). The tasks state the final world (decided 27 of the plan): choosing «Todas las IPs» clears the sends, as every change of the world does (decided 5), so the first three tasks hold in the world before and in the world after the fix and the player makes the four sends again after it. The report tells the player to try the 3000 first, because the question is about that refusal and its stamp is gone after the fix; the reference solution puts the `setSetting` first and then the four sends, and `chapter-2.test.ts` checks that sends made before the fix do not count after it (`a pick of the listen address clears the stamps of before, so the four sends are made after it`).
- The setting is `APP_LISTEN_ADDRESS` (`../shared.ts`): the listen address of the app of `laptop-lucia`, TCP 3000, offered as a tray named by the term (`dirección de escucha`) with two chips, «Solo este dispositivo (127.0.0.1)» (`loopback`, the chip that matches the world at the start) and «Todas las IPs (0.0.0.0)» (`all`). It sits on `laptop-lucia`, which is also an origin, so her label opens one card with two tabs, «Terminal» and «Servicios»; the incident sets no `opensFirst`, so the terminal opens first and the report says to open the tab «Servicios». The label of a chip says its address in words, which is why `the settings of a send incident` in `src/levels/ladder.test.ts` now asks a listen address chip to name its address (`127.0.0.1` or `0.0.0.0`) instead of asking, as for a gateway, for a label that is the value: the value of this setting is `loopback` or `all`, which the player does not read.
- The question is «¿Por qué laptop-lucia rechazó tu nc al puerto 3000, si ahí tiene un servicio?» and the right option is the third (the sheet: 03 = 3rd): the app listens only on `127.0.0.1` and refuses what goes to another IP of the laptop. The wrong options are the ones of the sheet: a firewall that dropped the packet (confuses a refusal with a silence: here `laptop-lucia` answered with an RST) and a closed port (a closed port is one where nobody listens; here someone does, on another IP). The three options are about the same length (93, 89 and 89 characters of plain text, the right one among the two shorter ones), which `chapter-2.test.ts` checks.
- No text says that a closed port and a service on another address are the same: the RST and `Connection refused` come from both, and the texts say so with the word «también» or «rechaza igual» where they say it at all, never that the causes are one. The takeaway keeps it exact: a closed port answers with an RST, a service that listens only on `127.0.0.1` refuses what goes to another IP as well, and a filtered port does not answer. `chapter-2.test.ts` guards it by sentence (`no text says that a closed port and a service on another address are the same`). No text gives a reason a reply comes back (decided 16 of the plan; `no text says why a reply comes back`).
- **Without verifying.** The behavior of a service on `127.0.0.1` that gets a packet to the IP of the network that comes from its own laptop is reasoning from `ip(7)` and not something tried on a machine (decided 8 of the plan; the spec's «Sin verificar»). It is reachable here: the terminal of `laptop-lucia` offers her own IP as an address, and the game answers with the same RST. The texts of this incident never claim it: they talk about the packet of your laptop. If the student tries it in an EC2 and it differs, the rule of the engine and this note change.
