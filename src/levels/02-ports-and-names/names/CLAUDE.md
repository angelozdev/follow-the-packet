# levels/02-ports-and-names/names

Block 5 of chapter 2, «Nombres»: the incidents that teach how a device finds the IP of a name. Task 29 of the plan built 02-10 and Task 30 built 02-11; 02-12 and 02-13 arrive with their tasks (31 and 32) and add their files and their notes here.

- `10-asking-for-a-name.ts` (title «Preguntar por un nombre»): a `send` incident from your laptop with `dig` and `ping` as the messages and the name `www.lucia.example` (`ADDRESS.www`) as the only address. Because the address is the only one, the terminal draws no tray for it: it lists the two messages and «Enviar» (the e2e play and the browser test pick the message and send, with no address to click). It introduces `domain-name`, `dns`, `dns-query`, `resolver`, `udp`, `dig` and `dhcp`. Its changes are `NAME_SERVERS` (`shared.ts`): `servidor-dns` (the resolver, `198.51.100.8`, UDP and TCP 53, which asks `servidor-autoritativo` for the zone `lucia.example`) and `servidor-autoritativo` (`198.51.100.53`, UDP and TCP 53, the zone with one address, `www.lucia.example` A `198.51.100.50`, the server of the internet, with the time to live of the game) appear in the internet; `router-destino` gets one interface for each, `198.51.100.2` and `198.51.100.3` (an update replaces the list, so the action restates `DESTINATION_INTERFACES`, exported by chapter 1, before the two new links); and `tu-laptop` gets the resolver as its DNS server. The servers are pieces an action only adds, so the report does not have to name them; `tu-laptop` already existed, so the report names it and tells the cause: the name and the DNS are explained in plain words first and named after («Ahora tu-laptop puede usar un nombre de dominio…», «Ese servidor es su resolver. Es servidor-dns, en 198.51.100.8.»), then «Apareció en «Ajustes de red» de tu-laptop porque se lo prestó DHCP.» (the sheet's sentence, in the order the text review asked for), and it says, before the player sees it, that a `ping` by name asks first and that each question takes a new source port. `changedNames` of the two worlds is exactly `[tu-laptop]` (`chapter-2.test.ts`). It draws `NAMES_PIECES`: `tu-laptop` with its firewall, `router-oficina`, the globe, `servidor-dns` and `servidor-internet`. The authoritative server is in the world and in the resolver's `upstreams`, and is not drawn and not named by any text until 02-11, where its term arrives (`chapter-2.test.ts` checks both).
  - The card of `tu-laptop` has three tabs, «Terminal» first, «Ajustes de red» and «Tabla de rutas». «Ajustes de red» is the read-only card: the incident offers no setting to pick, and the card shows the DNS server of the device (`Device.dnsServer`) with the line «Lo prestó DHCP.» once `dhcp` is known (`dnsLineOf` in `src/game/view/cards/settings-card.ts`). `send.opensFirst` cannot name a read-only card (see `src/levels/contract/CLAUDE.md`), so the report says which tab to open, as 02-08 does. `servidor-dns` is a locked piece: its cache is a tab of 02-12, and the records are those of `servidor-autoritativo`, a card of 02-11.
  - What the player sees, measured by `chapter-2.test.ts` (and, for the three lines of the «DNS» field of a `ping` by name, by `src/game/view/inspection.test.ts` › `the dns field of a ping or an nc by name says the device asked its resolver first, or that its hosts file answered`). **The numbers** follow the order of the sends. With the `dig` first, the `dig` is the datagram itself: `UDP 51514 → 53`, translated to `203.0.113.10:40001`, and the `ping` that follows asks its own question from 51515 (row 51515 → 40002). With the `ping` first, its question takes 51514 → 40001 and the `dig` leaves from 51515 → 40002. The `ping` has no port and leaves no row; its box says the IP changed and that ICMP has no ports. The table of translations of `router-oficina` lists `TCP · fija` first (the forward of 02-09 stays in the world) and then one `UDP · consulta DNS` row for each question or `dig`. **The box of a `dig`** is the box of a UDP datagram to port 53 as the view builds it: a «DNS» field («Contestó servidor-dns.» and `www.lucia.example A 198.51.100.50`), the four headers with ports, «Traducción» and «Consulta y respuesta» with two lines, the query and the answer, and no «Saludo de tres pasos». It does not carry the labels the sheet drew («Consulta DNS», «Respuesta», «El resolver buscó la respuesta por ti»): the view was built before the incident, in Tasks 16 and 28 quater, and this task did not change it. **The box of the `ping`** keeps the three lines of its «DNS» field («Antes de mandar el paquete, tu-laptop averigua la IP de www.lucia.example.», the question and its answer, and «La consulta salió del puerto 51514 y se ve en el mapa, antes de este paquete.»). The `dig` that follows a `ping` leaves from 51515 and its own box does not say why; the cause is in the box of the `ping`, in the report («En este juego, cada pregunta sale de un puerto de origen nuevo»), in the takeaway («En este juego, cada consulta, la de un ping y la de un dig, sale de un puerto de origen nuevo: por eso la segunda no repite el de la primera», then why the row exists, because the resolver is outside the office and `router-oficina` translates it, and why the ping leaves none, because ICMP has no ports and in that table each row carries one) and in the rule of 02-02 («Para cada conexión nueva, tu-laptop elige uno»).
  - **The cache.** The `ping` after a `dig` still sends its question, and the resolver answers it from what the `dig` made it keep (`question.trace.dns.fromCache`); no text and no box of 02-10 says «caché», because its term is 02-12's (`chapter-2.test.ts` reads every text and every box after a `dig` and a `ping`).
  - Tasks: `TASK.digWww` (the `dig` comes back and its answer holds `198.51.100.50`, `answerTaskFrom` in `shared.ts`) and `TASK.pingWww` (the `ping` comes back from `servidor-internet`).   - Rigor (the sheet's, guarded by `chapter-2.test.ts`): no text says that the computer asks «una sola vez» or that the query is the only one (a real `ping` or `nc` sends two questions, A and AAAA, from the same socket, and the game draws the one for the A record: `src/engine/dns/CLAUDE.md`); IPv6, AAAA, the authoritative server, records, the cache, the time to live, NXDOMAIN, SERVFAIL, the hosts file and Port Unreachable are named nowhere; the names of `lucia.example` are fictitious and the takeaway says so (`.example` is not delegated in the root zone, D-13, verified on 2026-10-11); DHCP is a line of the card and nothing more.

- `11-the-records.ts` (title «Los registros»): a `send` incident from your laptop with an `nc` to port 80 (`MESSAGE.ncWeb`) and a `dig` as the messages and two names as the addresses, `www.lucia.example` and `blog.lucia.example` (`ADDRESS.www`, `ADDRESS.blog`), so the terminal now draws a tray of addresses. It introduces `authoritative-server`, `dns-record`, `a-record` and `cname-record`, and its one change is `WRONG_RECORDS` (`shared.ts`): `setDns` on `servidor-autoritativo`, which restates its role and its zone `lucia.example` with `www` and `blog` both as A records to `198.51.100.51`, an address nobody has (`IP.nobodyOutside`). The report names `servidor-autoritativo` (`changedNames` is exactly that, `chapter-2.test.ts`). It draws `RECORDS_PIECES`: `tu-laptop` with its firewall, `router-oficina`, the globe, `servidor-dns`, `servidor-autoritativo` and `servidor-internet`; the sheet names four pieces (your laptop, the resolver, the authoritative server and the server of the internet) and `RECORDS_PIECES` adds the router, because it translates the question and the `nc`: with it undrawn the sweep of `map-sweep.test.ts` (the router that translates stands on the path of a translated packet) fails, and the row 51514 → 40001 the sheet promises has no table to live in. 02-12 draws the same list plus `servidor-nuevo` (`CACHE_PIECES`), so it inherits the router.

  - **The numbers and the order of the edits.** The `nc` by name asks first, as the `ping` of 02-10 does: on a fresh world its question leaves from 51514 and leaves the row 51514 → 40001, and the `nc` from 51515 → 40002 (`chapter-2.test.ts`; the «DNS» field of the box of the `nc` says why it is not 51514: «La consulta salió del puerto 51514 … Este paquete salió del puerto 51515.   - **What the player sees before fixing.** The question comes back with the wrong address (`198.51.100.51`, in the «DNS» field of the box of the `nc`); the `nc` then dies at `router-destino`, which is in the world and not drawn, and the box says «Un router de afuera de la oficina tiene conectada la red de 198.51.100.51, pero ningún dispositivo tiene esa IP: descarta el paquete y avisa a quien lo envió que no pudo entregarlo.» (`Destination Host Unreachable`, `by` `router-destino`). No text names `router-destino`, as the sheet asks. The resolver answers with the first A record it finds for a name, so adding the right A for `www` without taking away the wrong one leaves the `nc` failing: the «DNS» field of its box lists only the first answer («la respuesta es 198.51.100.51»), the `dig` lists both, and the report («En este juego, si un nombre tiene dos registros A, el nc usa el primero de la respuesta.») and the takeaway both say why («Con dos registros A para un mismo nombre, en este juego el nc usa el primero de la respuesta: por eso había que quitar el registro equivocado y no solo agregar el correcto.»). What a real client does with several A records was not verified, which is why the sentence says «en este juego»; the player has to take the wrong one away. An alias made before `www` is fixed answers `blog` through the alias with the wrong address and still fails the second task.
  - **The CNAME rule.** `blog.lucia.example` with a CNAME to `www.lucia.example` while it has its A is refused with `cname-with-other-records` («Ese nombre ya tiene otros registros, y un nombre con un registro CNAME no puede tener otros: o es un alias, o tiene datos propios.»); a CNAME at `lucia.example` is refused with `cname-at-apex` («La zona ya tiene sus propios registros en ese nombre, y un nombre con un registro CNAME no puede tener otros registros.»). The codes and the texts are the engine's and `TABLE_TEXT.problems` (Tasks 12 and 19); the incident adds only the data. No text says «apex», Alias of Route 53, the cache, the time to live, NXDOMAIN or SERVFAIL (`chapter-2.test.ts` reads every text and every box in nine states, with a positive control for the pattern). The one place where the last two can appear is the literal output of `dig` (the declared exception of D-5): the step the incident asks for, taking away the A record of `blog`, lets the player `dig` a name with no record, which prints `status: NXDOMAIN`, and a name made an alias of itself (the engine accepts it) prints `status: SERVFAIL`. The boxes and the card say it in plain words («blog.lucia.example no se convirtió en una IP.»), and `chapter-2.test.ts` pins both: the status of the tool and the absence of both words in every text of the game.
