# levels/02-ports-and-names/one-address

Block 4 of chapter 2, «Una IP, muchos equipos»: the incidents that teach how many private IPs share one public IP (02-08) and what a packet that comes from outside needs to be delivered (02-09). Task 28 of the plan built both and closed the block.

- `08-one-address-for-the-whole-office.ts` (title «Una IP para toda la oficina»): a `send` incident from `tu-laptop` and from `laptop-lucia`, with `ping` and `nc · puerto 80` as messages and the server as the only address. Its change is `WEB_PORTS` (`servidor-internet` gets TCP 80 and 443, only as numbers: what runs behind them is not this chapter's). The NAT of `router-oficina` is **not** a change: it has translated what leaves the office since the office reached the internet (decided 3 of the plan; `src/engine/nat/CLAUDE.md`), and the map did not show it before. The report says so («Lo hace desde que la oficina salió a internet: no es un cambio nuevo, solo que el mapa no lo mostraba») and names `servidor-internet` for the only thing that did change, its two services; `changedNames` of the two worlds is exactly `[servidor-internet]` and the routers of the world are untouched (`chapter-2.test.ts`). It draws `TRANSLATION_PIECES`: the two laptops, `router-oficina`, the globe and `servidor-internet`, with the firewalls of the two laptops that have one (`laptop-lucia` got hers in 02-05).
  - What the player sees: both `nc` leave from the same source port, 51514 (each device starts its own sequence there, decided 6), and come out as 40001 and 40002 in the order they were sent (the public ports are the game's, decided 4: «otros NAT eligen otros números», the takeaway says so). The box lists «Encabezado de ida (en tu red local)» and «Encabezado de ida (en internet)» and «Traducción» («Es una fila nueva de la tabla de traducciones»); the note «Fuera de la oficina el encabezado cambia: lo verás en el capítulo 2» is gone from the box once `nat` is known. A second `nc` of the same laptop leaves from 51515 and makes a row of its own. A `ping` leaves translated too (its IP only: ICMP has no ports) and leaves no row, so after it «Ruta que usó» and «Traducción» change and the table does not.
  - The table of translations is the tab «Tabla de traducciones» of the card of `router-oficina` (read only, derived from `Session.history`; `translationsCard`). The card has two tabs, the table of routes (derived, since `routing-table` is known) and this one, and it opens on the routes: `opensFirst` can only name a card the incident declares in `send` (`PIECE_CARDS` has no `translations`), so the report says which tab to open. The contract was not widened for this. **Known gap, the same as 02-06, not fixed here:** the read-only routes tab of `router-oficina` lists `203.0.113.1/32 · directo` and `172.16.0.2/32 · directo`, the links to `router-proveedor` and `router-sucursal`, which 02-08 does not draw (the next hops are written as «fuera de la oficina» and «dentro de la oficina», so no name leaks; `chapter-2.test.ts` checks that no text, box or table names an undrawn router). Fixing it means changing `table-card.ts` (task 19).
  - The question is «Las dos laptops salieron con la misma IP pública. ¿Cómo sabe router-oficina a cuál devolver cada respuesta?» and the right option is the first (the sheet: 08 = 1st): by the port, each connection has its row with its public port. The wrong ones are the sheet's, the server has a different IP for each laptop (it is the same server) and the router answers both laptops at once (it does not copy the reply); their feedback corrects them. No text says why a reply returns beyond its row (decided 16): the right option and its feedback stop at the row, and no text says «stateful». In the takeaway, «En un NAT real, las filas caducan; aquí no» says what the simplification leaves out, and the ping is told as a fact, never as a reason: it leaves translated, ICMP has no ports so it leaves no row in the table of the game, and a real NAT also translates the identifier of the Echo Request, which the game does not show; the ping of 01-08 «también salía traducido». The line of the box and the empty text of the table say the same with «en el juego» and «en esta tabla», so none claims a NAT keeps nothing for a ping.
- `09-from-outside.ts` (title «Desde afuera»): a `send` incident from `casa-lucia` (a client of the internet at `192.0.2.77`, an actor that sends from its own piece, with a terminal and no other card) to the public IP of `router-oficina`, `203.0.113.10`, with `nc · puerto 40009` and `nc · puerto 3000`. Its changes are `FORWARDED_APP`: `casa-lucia` appears (a piece the action only adds, so the text need not name it), `laptop-andres` puts its app on 3000 (listening on every IP) and `router-oficina` gets one fixed forward, TCP 3000 of its public IP to port 3000 of `laptop-andres` (D-14: the row is given by the world, the player does not write it). The report names `router-oficina` and `laptop-andres`. It draws `OUTSIDE_PIECES`: `casa-lucia`, the globe, `router-oficina` and `laptop-andres`.
  - The `nc` to 40009 has no row and no forward, so `router-oficina` does not deliver it to any device (`nat-no-translation`, outcome `timeout`, `by` the router, no warning, `nc` prints `Ncat: TIMEOUT.`); the `nc` to 3000 is delivered by the forward, nothing stops it (`laptop-andres` has no firewall) and it is answered (`nat-forwarded`, `translation.fixed`). A forward makes no row, so the card of translations is empty in 02-09 («Todavía no hay filas…») and the forward is told by the report and by the line «Un reenvío de puertos lo entregó a ese dispositivo.» in the box.
  - **No text says that NAT blocks or filters.** The text says «no lo entrega» and the takeaway says «NAT traduce, no filtra: no da seguridad por sí solo. Lo que decide qué entra es un firewall» (RFC 4864 §2.2, in the spec). The one text that does say it is the first wrong option of the question («Porque NAT es un firewall y bloquea todo lo que viene de afuera…»), which the sheet asks for, with a feedback that denies it; `chapter-2.test.ts` scans every other text and the boxes for a sentence that says NAT blocks, filters or protects, ignoring the sentences that deny it. The RFC 5382 REQ-4 caveat (a real NAT may answer with an ICMP Port Unreachable after at least 6 seconds, and `nc -w 3` gives up before) lives in the spec («Simplificaciones declaradas»), in `src/engine/nat/CLAUDE.md` and here, and in no text of the game (decided D-14 A). A forward is the fixed row of RFC 3022 §2 («static address maps»).
  - The question is «¿Por qué el paquete al puerto 40009 no llegó a ningún dispositivo?» and the right option is the third (the sheet: 09 = 3rd): there was no row for that port, and `router-oficina` did not know which device to deliver it to. The wrong ones are the sheet's: NAT is a firewall and blocks what comes from outside (it translates, it does not filter), and no device has 40009 open (the packet reached no device, so no device could have refused it; the feedback names the RST and Connection refused of 02-03 as what a closed port would have given). The right option is not the longest (99 characters against 100 and 93). The text says «dispositivo», tagged, everywhere the sheet says «equipo»: the task texts, `reasons.ts` and the definition of the forward use the term.
