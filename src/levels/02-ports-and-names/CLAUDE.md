# levels/02-ports-and-names

Chapter 2, «Puertos y nombres» (level 0, Bases), in fourteen sub-chapters grouped in six blocks, each in its own folder (`who-listens/`, `ports-and-rules/`, `which-way/`, `one-address/`, `names/` and `on-call/`): one file per incident (`NN-<slug>.ts`, a default export built with `defineIncident`, `chapter: 2`), and `shared.ts` with the registry of the chapter's world and what each block does to it. The block folders arrive with their tasks (a folder is born with its first incident and its own `CLAUDE.md`): `who-listens/` exists with 02-01 and 02-02, `ports-and-rules/` with 02-03 to 02-05, and the others arrive with theirs.

- `shared.ts` starts from chapter 1, not from a world of its own: 02-01 starts from the reference solution of 01-14 (`worldBeforeChanges`), so the chapter has no `BASE_WORLD`. `DEVICE`, `ROUTER`, `NAME`, `IP`, `LESSON` and `CONCEPT` extend or re-export those of `../01-first-day/shared` (one id and one name per piece, never a copy that can drift), and the chapter's new pieces are added beside them: the other office (`impresora-sucursal`, `laptop-sucursal`, `router-sucursal`), the servers of the internet (`SERVER`: `internet` is the server of chapter 1; `resolver`, `authoritative`, `news` and `fresh` are the servers of names and the one the site moves to), and `casa-lucia` (`CLIENT`, a client of the internet that sends from outside the office). `SERVER` is a record of servers, unlike the flat one of chapter 1.
- Names: an incident text takes every device, router, server and client from `DEVICE`, `ROUTER`, `SERVER`, `CLIENT` and `NAME` (`nameTag`), never as a literal. `PORT` holds the ports of the world (and 40009, the port the 02-09 probe asks for, which no forward has); `DOMAIN` and `HOSTNAME` hold the names of the game. Every name is under `.example` (RFC 2606 §2, decided D-13 = B on 2026-10-11) and every public address is in the three blocks RFC 5737 reserves for documentation (192.0.2.0/24, 198.51.100.0/24 and 203.0.113.0/24), used as if they were public; the screen never says so. `chapter-2.test.ts` guards both.
- The actions a block does to the world are named constants in `shared.ts`, as in chapter 1, each with a JSDoc that says what changes and to whom (the report of the incident names the device, as `.claude/rules/explained-behavior-changes.md` asks). Block 1 and block 2 are written: `LUCIA_SERVICES` (02-01: the app on port 3000 and the database on 5432, both on every IP), `APP_ON_LOOPBACK` (02-03: the app listens only on 127.0.0.1) and `SSH_AND_FIREWALL` (02-05: SSH on 22 and a firewall of laptop-lucia that lets nothing in and everything out; `setServices` replaces the list, so it holds the three services). 02-04 changes nothing in the world (`changes: []`). The actions of the other blocks (the other office and the routes of 02-07, the forward of 02-09, the servers of names and their zones of 02-10 to 02-13, and the cases of 02-14) are written by the task that builds each block, with the data of its sheet in `docs/plans/2026-10-11-follow-the-packet-chapter-2.md` and `docs/specs/2026-10-11-follow-the-packet-chapter-2-design.md` («El mapa que crece»). A router that faces a piece the same action adds is wired by that action (decided 2); the wiring of a new server of the internet to `router-destino` uses the addresses `IP.destinationResolver` to `IP.destinationFreshServer` (198.51.100.2 to 198.51.100.5).
- The lists of pieces each incident draws (`shown`) are already here, one per sheet: `SERVICES_PIECES` (02-01 and 02-02), `REFUSAL_PIECES` (02-03), `FLOORS_PIECES` (02-04 and 02-06), `SSH_PIECES` (02-05: the sheet draws three pieces and the list holds four, see the guide of `ports-and-rules/`), `ROUTES_PIECES` (02-07), `TRANSLATION_PIECES` (02-08), `OUTSIDE_PIECES` (02-09), `NAMES_PIECES` (02-10), `RECORDS_PIECES` (02-11), `CACHE_PIECES` (02-12) and `FAILURES_PIECES` (02-13). The sheets of 02-01 to 02-05 name the pieces they draw, while decided 28 of the plan says those five carry no list; the sheets and Task 23 win, because they say exactly which pieces to draw. 02-14 lists its pieces per case. A list holds ids of the world (devices, routers and actors) and not the globe, which the map draws when the list holds something outside the office; a router, server or actor that the map does not draw is not named in the text (`drawsRouter`, `drawsActor`). The tests of each block check that its list names pieces of its world; the lists of 02-01 to 02-06 are checked now, against the world of 01-14.
- `ADDRESS` and `MESSAGE` are those of chapter 1 (`ADDRESS` is a copy of its chips, `MESSAGE` adds to its three the `nc` chips of the ports of the world: `ncApp` for 3000, `ncDatabase` for 5432 and `ncMysql` for 3306, whose port is part of the chip, decided 26 of the plan). `ORIGIN` is the one of chapter 1 with `lucia` added (the terminal of `laptop-lucia`, which 02-03 tests from); `FIREWALL`, `pingTask`, `sendTask` and `sendMove` are those of chapter 1, re-exported so an incident of the chapter imports one module, and `sendTaskFrom` and `sendMoveFrom` are `sendTask` and `sendMove` for any terminal and not only your laptop (the constructors of chapter 1 keep `ORIGIN.you`). `APP_LISTEN_ADDRESS` (with `APP_LISTEN`, the ids of its setting and its chips) is the tray of 02-03 that sets where the app of `laptop-lucia` listens: a setting of a device that listens, which an incident may offer for a device that does not send. A later block adds its chips (a loopback address, a name, a `dig`) beside these.
- 02-04 and 02-05 add their constants beside the others: `BLOCK` (the block of the first floor, `192.168.1.0/24`, and its mask, `255.255.255.0`), `BLOCK_CARD` (the ids of the trays and chips of the card of your laptop and of the two bins of the card that classifies; the first bin is the one of what fits) and, for 02-05, `SSH_WHAT` and `SSH_FROM` (the Type and Source chips: `tcpChip` writes `TCP · 22` for one port and `TCP · 1-1023` for a range, and every one of them holds a port the probe battery tries, so a range that opens more than the 22 is found; none is UDP) and `SSH_FLOW` (the SSH of your laptop, required; the SSH of the visitor, forbidden; and the ping of laptop-lucia to laptop-marta, ambient, which is the one of 01-13). `addressChip` is the one of chapter 1, exported for the Source chip of your laptop.
- `TASK` holds the ids of the tasks of the incidents built so far; each block adds its own.
- Not here: the contract and the chips (`src/levels/contract/`), the registry (`src/levels/`), visible UI text and the reasons the box shows (`src/copy/`), the network rules (`src/engine/`).
- May import: `@/engine`, `@/copy`, `@/levels/contract/incident`, `@/levels/01-first-day/shared` (the world chapter 2 starts from) and `./shared` (from a block folder, `@/levels/02-ports-and-names/shared`).
