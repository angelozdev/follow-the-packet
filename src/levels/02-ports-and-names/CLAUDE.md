# levels/02-ports-and-names

Chapter 2, «Puertos y nombres» (level 0, Bases), in fourteen sub-chapters grouped in six blocks, each in its own folder (`who-listens/`, `ports-and-rules/`, `which-way/`, `one-address/`, `names/` and `on-call/`): one file per incident (`NN-<slug>.ts`, a default export built with `defineIncident`, `chapter: 2`), and `shared.ts` with the registry of the chapter's world and what each block does to it. The block folders arrived with their tasks (a folder is born with its first incident and its own `CLAUDE.md`): `who-listens/` exists with 02-01 and 02-02, `ports-and-rules/` with 02-03 to 02-05, `which-way/` with 02-06 and 02-07, `one-address/` with 02-08 and 02-09, `names/` with 02-10 to 02-13 and `on-call/` with 02-14, so the fourteen incidents are registered.

- `shared.ts` starts from chapter 1, not from a world of its own: 02-01 starts from the reference solution of 01-14 (`worldBeforeChanges`), so the chapter has no `BASE_WORLD`. `DEVICE`, `ROUTER`, `NAME`, `IP`, `LESSON` and `CONCEPT` extend or re-export those of `../01-first-day/shared` (one id and one name per piece, never a copy that can drift), and the chapter's new pieces are added beside them: the other office (`impresora-sucursal`, `laptop-sucursal`, `router-sucursal`), the servers of the internet (`SERVER`: `internet` is the server of chapter 1; `resolver`, `authoritative`, `news` and `fresh` are the servers of names and the one the site moves to), and `casa-lucia` (`CLIENT`, a client of the internet that sends from outside the office). `SERVER` is a record of servers, unlike the flat one of chapter 1.
- Names: an incident text takes every device, router, server and client from `DEVICE`, `ROUTER`, `SERVER`, `CLIENT` and `NAME` (`nameTag`), never as a literal. `PORT` holds the ports of the world (and 40009, the port the 02-09 probe asks for, which no forward has); `DOMAIN` and `HOSTNAME` hold the names of the game. Every name is under `.example` (RFC 2606 §2, decided D-13 = B on 2026-10-11) and every public address is in the three blocks RFC 5737 reserves for documentation (192.0.2.0/24, 198.51.100.0/24 and 203.0.113.0/24), used as if they were public; the screen never says so. `chapter-2.test.ts` guards both.
- `QUOTED_TITLE` holds the titles of the incidents whose text another incident quotes («Abrir una conexión» in the hint of 02-10 and «Preguntar por un nombre» in the takeaway of 02-11). The incident takes its own `title` from it and the quoting text reads the same value, so the quote cannot go stale; an incident file may not import another incident file, so the shared title lives in `shared.ts`.
- The actions a block does to the world are named constants in `shared.ts`, as in chapter 1, each with a JSDoc that says what changes and to whom (the report of the incident names the device, as `.claude/rules/explained-behavior-changes.md` asks). The six blocks are written (block 5 with 02-10 to 02-13 and block 6 with 02-14): `LUCIA_SERVICES` (02-01: the app on port 3000 and the database on 5432, both on every IP), `APP_ON_LOOPBACK` (02-03: the app listens only on 127.0.0.1) and `SSH_AND_FIREWALL` (02-05: SSH on 22 and a firewall of laptop-lucia that lets nothing in and everything out; `setServices` replaces the list, so it holds the three services). 02-04 and 02-06 change nothing in the world (`changes: []`), and 02-07 does two things: `OTHER_OFFICE` adds the other office (its LAN, its two devices and `router-sucursal`) and `ROUTES_LOST` takes every route from `router-oficina` and wires its private link. 02-08 does one, `WEB_PORTS` (`servidor-internet` gets TCP 80 and 443; the NAT of `router-oficina` is derived from the topology and is no action), and 02-09 does three in `FORWARDED_APP`: it adds the client `casa-lucia`, puts the app of `laptop-andres` on 3000 and writes the one fixed forward of `router-oficina` (TCP 3000 to port 3000 of `laptop-andres`). 02-10 (block 5) does four in `NAME_SERVERS`: it adds the resolver `servidor-dns` and `servidor-autoritativo` with the zone `lucia.example` (one address, `www`), restates the interfaces of `router-destino` and adds the two links to the new servers (an update replaces the list, so it starts from `DESTINATION_INTERFACES`, exported by chapter 1 with `SERVER_MS`, which the servers of names use for their latency), and sets the DNS server of `tu-laptop` to the resolver. 02-11 does one, `WRONG_RECORDS`: `setDns` on `servidor-autoritativo`, which restates its role and its zone and points `www` and `blog` to `198.51.100.51` (`IP.nobodyOutside`), an address nobody has (`setDns` replaces the whole `dns` of a server, as `updateRouter` replaces the interfaces). 02-12 does four in `MOVED_SITE`: it adds `servidor-nuevo` (`198.51.100.60`, TCP 80 and 443) and wires it to `router-destino` (`198.51.100.5`; the update restates `NAME_SERVER_INTERFACES`, the interfaces of chapter 1 and those of the two servers of names, which `NAME_SERVERS` uses too, so `router-destino` differs between the two worlds only by the wiring of the new piece and `changedNames` does not list it), sets the zone of `servidor-autoritativo` with `www` pointing to the new IP and `blog` still an alias of `www` (as 02-11 left it), and sets the `dns` of `servidor-dns` with its cache holding the old answer, `www.lucia.example` A `198.51.100.50`, that expires at second `OLD_ANSWER_SECONDS` (240) of the game clock (`RESOLVER_DNS` is the role and the `upstreams` that each of its `setDns` restates). 02-13 does five in `NAME_FAILURES`: it adds `servidor-autoritativo-noticias` (`198.51.100.54`, UDP and TCP 53, the zone `noticias.example` with no record, `failing: true`) and wires it to `router-destino` (`198.51.100.4`; `NEWS_INTERFACES` restates `MOVED_SITE_INTERFACES`, the list 02-12 left, before the new link, so `router-destino` differs from the world of 02-12 only by the wiring of the new piece), sets the `dns` of `servidor-dns` with the zone of the site and the new one in `upstreams` and no cache (`resolverDns`, built from `SITE_UPSTREAM` and `NEWS_UPSTREAM`, is what `RESOLVER_DNS` is made of too: the answer 02-12 left in the cache does not carry into 02-13), gives `tu-laptop` a hosts file with one line, `tienda.lucia.example` to `198.51.100.50` (`SHOP_HOSTS`), and sets the DNS server of `laptop-andres` to the printer. 02-14 (block 6) changes nothing of its own (`changes: []`) and each of its seven cases is a change on the world 02-13 leaves, never on the case before: `SITE_ON_LOOPBACK` (case 1: the service of `servidor-nuevo` on TCP 80 listens only on 127.0.0.1; `servidor-nuevo` and not `servidor-internet` because `www.lucia.example` points to it since 02-12), `STALE_SHOP` (case 3: the hosts file of `tu-laptop` has `tienda.lucia.example` at `198.51.100.70`, `IP.staleHost`, an IP nobody has, and `servidor-autoritativo` gets an A record of that name to `198.51.100.50`, with `addRecord`, so the rest of its zone is untouched), `DEFAULT_ROUTE_LOST` (case 4: `removeRoute` of the default route of `router-oficina`, which keeps the route to the other office) and `LOOP` (case 5, the action of chapter 1, re-exported: `router-proveedor` sends everything back to `router-oficina`), with the fix of case 5 (`PROVIDER_ROUTES_FIX`, the ids of its table and chips; `PROVIDER_ROUTES_TABLE`, the table of routes of `router-proveedor` the player edits; `LOOP_FIX`, that table, the task that the `ping` to `servidor-internet` comes back and the reference solution). Cases 2, 6 and 7 change nothing: the typo is only in the chip, the firewall of `laptop-lucia` already has TCP 22 as its only inbound rule (02-05) and the printer is already the DNS server of `laptop-andres` (02-13). The data of every case is in `on-call/cases.ts` and its guide. A router that faces a piece the same action adds is wired by that action (decided 2); the wiring of a new server of the internet to `router-destino` uses the addresses `IP.destinationResolver` to `IP.destinationFreshServer` (198.51.100.2 to 198.51.100.5, the news server's being .4).
- The lists of pieces each incident draws (`shown`) are already here, one per sheet: `SERVICES_PIECES` (02-01 and 02-02), `REFUSAL_PIECES` (02-03), `FLOORS_PIECES` (02-04 and 02-06), `SSH_PIECES` (02-05: the sheet draws three pieces and the list holds four, see the guide of `ports-and-rules/`), `ROUTES_PIECES` (02-07), `TRANSLATION_PIECES` (02-08), `OUTSIDE_PIECES` (02-09), `NAMES_PIECES` (02-10), `RECORDS_PIECES` (02-11: the sheet names four pieces and the list adds `router-oficina`, which translates the question and the `nc`, so the map has a place where the port changes and the table of translations an owner to open; `CACHE_PIECES` is built from it, so 02-12 draws the router too), `CACHE_PIECES` (02-12) and `FAILURES_PIECES` (02-13: the sheet's seven pieces plus `router-oficina`, which the sweep of the map asks for as in 02-11, so the router stands on the path of the questions that cross it). The sheets of 02-01 to 02-05 name the pieces they draw, while decided 28 of the plan says those five carry no list; the sheets and Task 23 win, because they say exactly which pieces to draw. 02-14 lists its pieces per case: `NAME_TEST_PIECES` (case 2: your laptop, `router-oficina` and the resolver), `LOOPBACK_SITE_PIECES` (case 1: those and `servidor-nuevo`), `STALE_SHOP_PIECES` (case 3: those, `servidor-autoritativo`, which the report names, and `servidor-internet`, whose IP the `dig` gives), `LOST_WAY_PIECES` (case 4: your laptop, `router-oficina`, `router-sucursal`, the printer of the other office and `servidor-internet`), `LOOP_FIX_PIECES` (case 5: the pieces of the loop of chapter 1 and `router-destino`, which the chips of the fix name), `SERVICES_PIECES` (case 6, the same list as 02-01 and 02-02: your laptop and `laptop-lucia`, whose firewall is drawn with it) and `PRINTER_DNS_PIECES` (case 7: `laptop-andres`, the router between the floors and the printer). A list holds ids of the world (devices, routers and actors) and not the globe, which the map draws when the list holds something outside the office; a router, server or actor that the map does not draw is not named in the text (`drawsRouter`, `drawsActor`). The tests of each block check that its list names pieces of its world; the lists of 02-01 to 02-06 are checked against the world of 01-14 and `ROUTES_PIECES`, against the world 02-07 starts from.
- `ADDRESS` and `MESSAGE` are those of chapter 1 (`ADDRESS` is a copy of its chips plus `otherLaptop`, `officePublic`, the public IP of `router-oficina` that `casa-lucia` writes to in 02-09, and `www`, the name `www.lucia.example`, the first address that is not an IP (02-10), and `blog`, the name `blog.lucia.example` (02-11); `MESSAGE` adds to its three the `nc` chips of the ports of the world: `ncApp` for 3000, `ncDatabase` for 5432, `ncMysql` for 3306, `ncWeb` for 80 and `ncUnmapped` for 40009, whose port is part of the chip, decided 26 of the plan, and `dig`, the chip `{{dig}}` of 02-10, a UDP datagram to port 53 that reads no hosts file, and `ncSsh` for 22, the test of case 6 of 02-14 that comes back). `ADDRESS.mistyped` is the name `wwww.lucia.example` (case 2 of 02-14). `ORIGIN` is the one of chapter 1 with `lucia` added (the terminal of `laptop-lucia`, which 02-03 tests from) and `home` (the terminal of `casa-lucia`, an actor origin, which 02-09 sends from); `FIREWALL`, `pingTask`, `sendTask` and `sendMove` are those of chapter 1, re-exported so an incident of the chapter imports one module, and `sendTaskFrom` and `sendMoveFrom` are `sendTask` and `sendMove` for any terminal and not only your laptop (the constructors of chapter 1 keep `ORIGIN.you`). `APP_LISTEN_ADDRESS` (with `APP_LISTEN`, the ids of its setting and its chips) is the tray of 02-03 that sets where the app of `laptop-lucia` listens: a setting of a device that listens, which an incident may offer for a device that does not send.
