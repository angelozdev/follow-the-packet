# levels/contract

What an incident is, and what its chips can do: the zod schema, types and constructor of an incident (`incident.ts`: `incidentSchema`, `defineIncident`, `ruleIdFor`, `isGuided`, `settingKind`, `cardsOfPiece` and `firstCardOf`, the flows, tasks, chips, settings, tables, clock, cases and player moves) and the chips (`chips.ts`: the rule two chips name and the chips a rule has, the packet three send chips make (`packetFromChips`, with the TTL of the message, the port of an `nc` or the datagram of a `dig`) or, for a traceroute, the probe it repeats (`probeFromChips`), every combination of send chips (these three take whatever offers send chips, a send incident or a case of a diagnose: `SendOwner`), the check that no combination leaves an office with no routers (a packet to a loopback address stays in the device, so it is no leak), and the checks over ids that name something in a world: the ids a task expects, the devices whose card an identify incident offers, the devices its card that classifies holds, the owners of the tables, the devices of the settings, and the ids of the list of shown pieces; and `routersCrossedByTraceroutes`, the routers every probe of the traceroutes a send owner offers crosses, which the registry compares with the list of shown pieces) and the changes a world goes through (`changes.ts`: `changedNames`, the devices, routers and servers of the internet whose configuration differs between two worlds (every field of a device or of a server), whatever the kind of action that made the difference; a piece that only exists in the second world is not a change, and neither is a new interface of a router that faces such a piece (its wiring), while any other change to the interfaces, routes or warnings of that router is; `changedFloors`, the LANs of two or more devices on which every device changed, whose name may stand for those devices; `mentions` and `unnamedChanges`, which ask whether the texts of an incident name them).

- The contract is the only place that says what an incident file may hold; the incident files and the registry read it, they never extend it from outside.
- A filter incident may offer outbound rules: `chips.to` is a second tray of peer chips (the Destination of a rule, same shape as the Source chips of `chips.from`, ids unique and at least one). The move `placeRule` is one for both directions: `{ firewallId, whatId, fromId?, toId?, direction? }`; without `direction` it is inbound and needs `fromId` (and no `toId`), with `outbound` it needs `toId` (and no `fromId`), and the solution may only name chips the incident offers in that tray. `removeRule` takes the same optional `direction`. `ruleIdFor(whatId, peerId, direction)` names an inbound rule `<what>-from-<peer>` and an outbound one `<what>-to-<peer>`, so the same two chips make two different rules; `ruleFromChips` and `chipsOfRule` take the direction too (inbound by default).
- A send task's `expect.outcome` is one of `OUTCOMES` of the engine, `reached` or `any`; its `by` is an id, and the contract cannot say what it names, because the world may come from the incidents before it: the registry test does (`unknownExpectedIds`).
- A message chip is `{ id, label, command?, protocol?, port?, icmpType?, ttl? }`. The `command` fixes the protocol: `packet`, `ping` and `traceroute` are ICMP, `nc` is TCP and `dig` is UDP; a `protocol` that is written must agree. An ICMP message needs its `icmpType` and has no `port`; an `nc` needs its `port` (1 to 65535) and has no `icmpType`; a `dig` goes to port 53 (its `port`, if written, is 53) and has no `icmpType`; only a `ping` has a `ttl`. A message with no `command` is ICMP. `packetFromChips` builds the TCP packet of an `nc` with the port of its chip, and the UDP datagram of a `dig` with `lookup: 'dig'`, port 53 and the address chip as its destination. An address chip is any text: an IP, `localhost` or a name; the contract does not tell them apart, so `officeLeaks` counts an `nc` or a `dig` only when its address is an IP outside the office (a `dig` of a name leaves toward the resolver of the device, which the contract does not read).
- A send incident may offer settings (`send.settings`): a setting saved on a device, which the player changes on that device's card and not in its terminal. A setting is `{ id, deviceId, kind?, service?, name, chips }` and its `kind` is `gateway` (the kind of a setting that names none, as every setting of chapter 1), `dns-server` or `listen-address`. The chips are `{ id, label, value }`: for a gateway and a DNS server `value` is an IPv4 address or `null` (none), and for a listen address it is `all` or `loopback`, never an IP and never `null`; a value that does not fit its kind is rejected. A listen address names the service it sets, `service: { protocol, port }`, because `setListenAddress` needs both, and the other kinds name none. It has at least two chips, with ids and values that do not repeat (the chip that matches the world is the one whose value is the device's setting), and a gateway or a DNS server belongs to a device that is an origin of the incident (an origin chip whose endpoint is a device; an actor has neither), while a listen address belongs to any device, because the device that listens is usually not the one that sends. The `name` of the tray is a text with a term tag, so the ladder reads it. The move `setSetting` (`{ deviceId, settingId, chipId }`) is part of a send incident's solution, next to `sendPacket`, and names a setting and a chip the incident offers. The contract cannot say whether the device is in the world (the world may come from the incidents before it): the registry does (`unknownSettingDeviceIds`), and `applyMove` leaves a world without that device as it is.
- A send incident may offer tables the player edits (`send.tables`), and a case of a diagnose a fix with the same tables (`fix.tables`). A table is `{ id, kind, ownerId, trays }`. A table of `routes` has two trays, `destination` (chips whose `value` is `default` or a CIDR block) and `nextHop` (the id of a router); a table of `records` has three, `name`, `type` (`A` or `CNAME`, the only records a player edits) and `value`. A tray is a list of chips `{ id, label, value }` with at least one chip and ids that do not repeat; the headers of the columns are the view's, not the contract's (they depend on which terms are known). The TTL of a record is not a tray, because the player does not choose it. The contract cannot say who owns the table: the registry does (`unknownTableOwnerIds`, in the world of each case for a fix).
- A send incident may have a clock (`send.clock`: at least one step `{ id, label, seconds }`, with ids that do not repeat and a whole, positive number of seconds) whose steps the card «Reloj del juego» offers as «Esperar».
- A send task may expect, besides the outcome, what the DNS answered: `expect.answer` is `{ ip?, alias? }` (the IP of the answer, and whether it came through an alias; it names at least one) and `expect.status` is `NXDOMAIN` or `SERVFAIL`. They are for the session to read; the contract only checks their shape.
- A send incident may say which card a piece opens first (`send.opensFirst`: the id of a piece of the world and one of `terminal`, `settings`, `services`, `routes` or `records`). The piece must have that card: the terminal when it is an origin, `settings` when a gateway or a DNS server of it is offered, `services` when a listen address of it is, and `routes` or `records` when it owns a table of that kind (`cardsOfPiece`). With none, `firstCardOf` gives the first card the piece has in that order. The read-only cards (the translation table, the cache) are not in the list: no incident asks to open them first.
- An identify incident (`identify.cards`) has one card per device; a card's trays have unique ids and unique chip ids, at least two chips each, and a `segmented` tray has exactly two. A tray's `initialPick` names one of its chips, and an `initialPickRule` is only accepted next to an `initialPick`. Every task and every move of the solution names a device, a tray and a chip some card offers, and an identify incident has no flows. The cards' device ids are checked against the world by the registry (`unknownCardDeviceIds`), as `shown` is.
- An identify incident may also have a card that classifies other devices (`identify.classify`, beside the cards): `{ deviceId, cidr, trayId, bins, items }`. `deviceId` is the device whose card it is (the one that opens it), `cidr` the block the items are compared with (a CIDR block, from which the view takes the prefix, the mask and the bits it marks), `bins` exactly two `{ id, name }` with ids that do not repeat, and `items` the devices to sort, at least one and none repeated, none of them the owner. The answer for an item is saved as every answer is, under its own device and under the common `trayId`, and the chip it names is the id of a bin: a task may expect it (`expect: { deviceId: <item>, trayId, chipId: <bin> }`) and a move `answerCard` of the solution may give it. A `trayId` that the card of one of the items already uses is rejected, because the two answers would share a place. The registry checks that the owner and the items are in the world (`unknownClassifiedDeviceIds`), and the list of shown pieces must draw them.
- A diagnose incident (`diagnose.cases`) has no tasks, no solution and none of the incident-level `hints`, `question` and `explanation` (`isGuided` is false for it: it is the type guard of the other three mechanics, which have them). Each case holds an `id` (unique), optional `changes` on the world the incident starts from (never on the case before), a `report`, its own `send` chips (origins, addresses and messages, with the same checks as a send incident's: ids unique per tray, none empty), two `hints` and a `cause`: the `question` and exactly three options, one correct. There is at least one case; the contract does not fix their number. Its flows are ambient only, as a send incident's. A case may carry a `fix` (`{ tables, tasks }`): the tables the player edits to repair it, in the same shape as `send.tables`, and the send tasks that must hold to close it, which name only the chips of the case. The moves of the player on a fix, and the reference solution that repairs it, belong to the session. The registry's checks over ids and leaks (`officeLeaks`, `missingShownIds`) read the chips of every case.
- `shown` is an optional list of the ids of the world (devices, routers and actors, a server of the internet included) that the incident draws; without it the incident draws the whole world, as chapter 4 does. It has at least one id and none repeated. A firewall is not listed: it is drawn with its device. Whether the ids exist in the world, and whether the list holds what the incident uses, are the registry's checks (`unknownShownIds`, and `missingShownIds`: the sender of every flow or origin chip, the owner of every address it sends to, and whoever a task expects, a firewall through its device), because the world may come from the incidents before it. An incident that offers a traceroute must list every router its probes cross (`routersCrossedByTraceroutes`, checked by the registry in the world it starts from and the one its solution leaves, and, for a diagnose, in the world of each case): the traceroute prints the IP of each router that warns, so the map must draw them all. A router that no probe crosses may be listed too (01-10 lists the router in front of the server, which none reaches, and never draws it). A send incident may set `revealRoutersByTraceroute`: its routers, which the list still holds, are drawn only once a traceroute the player sent prints their IP, and the incident must offer a traceroute chip, or they would never appear (`revealsRoutersByTraceroute` reads it; 01-10 is the only one). A case of a diagnose may carry a `shown` list of its own (same shape and same checks, `shownOf`): in a diagnose each case is a screen, so it draws only what it uses, and the incident's list, if it has one, serves only the cases that have none. The registry checks a case's list in the world of that case (`unknownShownIds` and `missingShownIds` take the case): its origin, the owner of each of its addresses and, if it offers a traceroute, every router its probes cross.
- Not here: incident content (`src/levels/NN-<chapter>/`), the registry, the world chain and the term ladder (`src/levels/`), game-session state (`src/game/`).
- May import: `@/engine`, `@/copy`, and other files of this folder (`./x`).
