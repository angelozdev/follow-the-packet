# levels/contract

What an incident is, and what its chips can do: the zod schema, types and constructor of an incident (`incident.ts`: `incidentSchema`, `defineIncident`, `ruleIdFor`, `isGuided`, the flows, tasks, chips, settings, cases and player moves) and the chips (`chips.ts`: the rule two chips name and the chips a rule has, the packet three send chips make (`packetFromChips`, with the TTL of the message) or, for a traceroute, the probe it repeats (`probeFromChips`), every combination of send chips (these three take whatever offers send chips, a send incident or a case of a diagnose: `SendOwner`), the check that no combination leaves an office with no routers (a packet to a loopback address stays in the device, so it is no leak), and the checks over ids that name something in a world: the ids a task expects, the devices whose card an identify incident offers, and the ids of the list of shown pieces; and `routersCrossedByTraceroutes`, the routers every probe of the traceroutes a send owner offers crosses, which the registry compares with the list of shown pieces) and the changes a world goes through (`changes.ts`: `changedNames`, the devices and routers whose configuration differs between two worlds, whatever the kind of action that made the difference; a piece that only exists in the second world is not a change; `changedFloors`, the LANs of two or more devices on which every device changed, whose name may stand for those devices; `mentions` and `unnamedChanges`, which ask whether the texts of an incident name them).

- The contract is the only place that says what an incident file may hold; the incident files and the registry read it, they never extend it from outside.
- A filter incident may offer outbound rules: `chips.to` is a second tray of peer chips (the Destination of a rule, same shape as the Source chips of `chips.from`, ids unique and at least one). The move `placeRule` is one for both directions: `{ firewallId, whatId, fromId?, toId?, direction? }`; without `direction` it is inbound and needs `fromId` (and no `toId`), with `outbound` it needs `toId` (and no `fromId`), and the solution may only name chips the incident offers in that tray. `removeRule` takes the same optional `direction`. `ruleIdFor(whatId, peerId, direction)` names an inbound rule `<what>-from-<peer>` and an outbound one `<what>-to-<peer>`, so the same two chips make two different rules; `ruleFromChips` and `chipsOfRule` take the direction too (inbound by default).
- A send task's `expect.outcome` is one of `OUTCOMES` of the engine, `reached` or `any`; its `by` is an id, and the contract cannot say what it names, because the world may come from the incidents before it: the registry test does (`unknownExpectedIds`).
- A send incident may offer settings (`send.settings`): a setting saved on a device, which the player changes on that device's «Ajustes de red» card and not in its terminal. Today the only one is the default gateway: a list of `{ id, deviceId, name, chips }` whose chips are `{ id, label, value }`, where `value` is an IPv4 address or `null` (the device has no gateway). It has at least two chips, with ids and values that do not repeat (the chip that matches the world is the one whose value is the device's gateway), and a setting belongs to a device that is an origin of the incident (an origin chip whose endpoint is a device; an actor has no gateway). The `name` of the tray is a text with a term tag, so the ladder reads it. The move `setSetting` (`{ deviceId, settingId, chipId }`) is part of a send incident's solution, next to `sendPacket`, and names a setting and a chip the incident offers. The contract cannot say whether the device is in the world (the world may come from the incidents before it): `applyMove` leaves a world without that device as it is.
- An identify incident (`identify.cards`) has one card per device; a card's trays have unique ids and unique chip ids, at least two chips each, and a `segmented` tray has exactly two. A tray's `initialPick` names one of its chips, and an `initialPickRule` is only accepted next to an `initialPick`. Every task and every move of the solution names a device, a tray and a chip some card offers, and an identify incident has no flows. The cards' device ids are checked against the world by the registry (`unknownCardDeviceIds`), as `shown` is.
- A diagnose incident (`diagnose.cases`) has no tasks, no solution and none of the incident-level `hints`, `question` and `explanation` (`isGuided` is false for it: it is the type guard of the other three mechanics, which have them). Each case holds an `id` (unique), optional `changes` on the world the incident starts from (never on the case before), a `report`, its own `send` chips (origins, addresses and messages, with the same checks as a send incident's: ids unique per tray, none empty), two `hints` and a `cause`: the `question` and exactly three options, one correct. There is at least one case; the contract does not fix their number. Its flows are ambient only, as a send incident's. The registry's checks over ids and leaks (`officeLeaks`, `missingShownIds`) read the chips of every case.
- `shown` is an optional list of the ids of the world (devices, routers and actors, a server of the internet included) that the incident draws; without it the incident draws the whole world, as chapter 4 does. It has at least one id and none repeated. A firewall is not listed: it is drawn with its device. Whether the ids exist in the world, and whether the list holds what the incident uses, are the registry's checks (`unknownShownIds`, and `missingShownIds`: the sender of every flow or origin chip, the owner of every address it sends to, and whoever a task expects, a firewall through its device), because the world may come from the incidents before it. An incident that offers a traceroute must list every router its probes cross (`routersCrossedByTraceroutes`, checked by the registry in the world it starts from and the one its solution leaves, and, for a diagnose, in the world of each case): the traceroute prints the IP of each router that warns, so the map must draw them all. A router that no probe crosses may be listed too (01-10 draws the router in front of the server, which none reaches). A case of a diagnose may carry a `shown` list of its own (same shape and same checks, `shownOf`): in a diagnose each case is a screen, so it draws only what it uses, and the incident's list, if it has one, serves only the cases that have none. The registry checks a case's list in the world of that case (`unknownShownIds` and `missingShownIds` take the case): its origin, the owner of each of its addresses and, if it offers a traceroute, every router its probes cross.
- Not here: incident content (`src/levels/NN-<chapter>/`), the registry, the world chain and the term ladder (`src/levels/`), game-session state (`src/game/`).
