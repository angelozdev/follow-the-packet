# design/primitives/panels

The primitives that hold content: `Card` (`card.tsx`), a panel with an optional dark stripe on top; `Tabs` (`tabs.tsx`, with its hook `use-reveal-selected-tab.ts`), a list of panels of which one shows at a time; and `Table` (`table.tsx`), rows under named columns. Their tests sit next to them (`card`, `tabs` and `table`; `tabs.browser.test.tsx` reads in the real browser what CSS does to a bar of tabs: it scrolls by itself, fades the edge that hides tabs and brings a focused tab into view); their styles are in `../primitives.css`, next to the others.

- A primitive here frames or arranges content it does not own: the content arrives as `children`, as the `panel` of a tab, as data for the stripe or as the cells of a row, and its inner layout belongs to the caller. It takes no `className` and no `style`: a new look is a new variant of its file.
- `Card` has no `overflow`: it never clips its content and a grid or flex parent never squeezes it below its height. The stripe rounds its own top corners instead (see "Style B" in `DESIGN.md`).
- `Table` is data in and markup out. The props are listed in its file, and none of them is a `className`, a `style` or a rest spread, so TypeScript rejects what the standard forbids. `cells` hold nodes, not only text, because a cell may carry a `<code>` or a name tag; the name of the remove button of a row is built from the content of its row with `aria-labelledby` (the word `removeText` first, then the cells), so two rows never share a name. The words (`caption`, `emptyText`, `inUseText`, `removeText`) arrive as props because this folder imports no text. A row in use wears a pill with `inUseText`, so the mark is a word and a shape, never only a colour. Its wrapper (`.table`) scrolls: sideways when the columns do not fit and, inside a card with a height cap such as a card on the map, up and down, because it is a flex item that shrinks (do not give it a fixed height); it draws its own scrollbar, like the floating boxes. The caption is visually hidden (`sr-only`, from `src/styles.css`) because the `Card` around it already shows the title, and an empty table shows `emptyText` in `muted`; both classes are the app's, not this folder's.
- `Tabs` is one row that never makes its parent wider: when the tabs do not fit, the list scrolls sideways by itself, fades the edge that hides tabs and brings a focused or clicked tab into view (`bringIntoView` on the focus and on the click of each tab, because Safari and Firefox on macOS do not focus a button on click; it is `scrollIntoView` with `nearest`, and the `scroll-padding-inline` of the list places the tab clear of the fade; Chrome alone does not scroll a tab that is only partly hidden). It also brings the selected tab into view when the bar mounts and every time `selected` changes (`use-reveal-selected-tab.ts`, the same `scrollIntoView`), so a tab the caller chose from outside, past the edge of a bar that scrolls, is not left out of sight; a render that changes nothing scrolls nothing, and it is behavior, not a variant. It never wraps to a second row (T4-360 = A). Radix draws the body of a tab one render after the selection reaches it, which would leave a caller that scrolls the panel into view right after a choice (`useSelection`) measuring the old body; so each panel is keyed by `id` and by whether it is the selected one, and the body of the tab just chosen is drawn in the same render as the choice.
- Not here: an element the player acts on or picks with (`button.tsx`, `choice.tsx`) or a name (`name-tag.tsx`), which stay in `src/design/primitives/`; the tokens (`src/design/`), screens (`src/ui/`), map pieces (`src/scene/`) and visible text (`src/copy/`).
- May import: no layer; `react` and, for `Tabs`, `@radix-ui/react-tabs`. A sibling primitive of `src/design/primitives/` is imported with the `@/design/primitives/<file>` path (`Table` uses `@/design/primitives/button`), never with `../`. Other layers import this folder through the barrel of `src/design/` (`@/design`).
