# design/primitives/panels

The primitives that hold content: `Card` (`card.tsx`), a panel with an optional dark stripe on top; `Tabs` (`tabs.tsx`, with its hook `use-reveal-selected-tab.ts`), a list of panels of which one shows at a time; `Table` (`table.tsx`), rows under named columns; and `Dialog` (`dialog.tsx`), a window drawn as a `Card` over Radix Dialog. Their tests sit next to them (`card`, `dialog`, `tabs` and `table`; `tabs.browser.test.tsx` reads in the real browser what CSS does to a bar of tabs: it scrolls by itself, fades the edge that hides tabs and brings a focused tab into view; `tabs-variants.browser.test.tsx` reads the look of each variant by computed style and plays real mouse, wheel and touch events on a bar that overflows); their styles are in `../primitives.css`, next to the others.

- A primitive here frames or arranges content it does not own: the content arrives as `children`, as the `panel` of a tab, as data for the stripe or as the cells of a row, and its inner layout belongs to the caller. It takes no `className` and no `style`: a new look is a new variant of its file.
- `Card` has no `overflow`: it never clips its content and a grid or flex parent never squeezes it below its height. The stripe rounds its own top corners instead (see "Style B" in `DESIGN.md`). `folded` shows only the stripe: the body stays mounted with the `hidden` attribute, so it leaves the page, the Tab order and the accessibility tree and keeps its state; it is behavior, not a look, and the caller owns the arrow that toggles it.
- `Table` is data in and markup out. The props are listed in its file, and none of them is a `className`, a `style` or a rest spread, so TypeScript rejects what the standard forbids. `cells` hold nodes, not only text, because a cell may carry a `<code>` or a name tag; the name of the remove button of a row is built from the content of its row with `aria-labelledby` (the word `removeText` first, then the cells), so two rows never share a name. The words (`caption`, `emptyText`, `inUseText`, `removeText`) arrive as props because this folder imports no text. A row in use wears a pill with `inUseText`, so the mark is a word and a shape, never only a colour. Its wrapper (`.table`) scrolls: sideways when the columns do not fit and, inside a card with a height cap such as a card on the map, up and down, because it is a flex item that shrinks (do not give it a fixed height); it draws its own scrollbar, like the floating boxes. The caption is visually hidden (`sr-only`, from `src/styles.css`) because the `Card` around it already shows the title, and an empty table shows `emptyText` in `muted`; both classes are the app's, not this folder's.
- `Tabs` is one row that never makes its parent wider: when the tabs do not fit, the list scrolls sideways by itself, fades the edge that hides tabs and brings a focused or clicked tab into view (`bringIntoView` on the focus and on the click of each tab, because Safari and Firefox on macOS do not focus a button on click; it is `scrollIntoView` with `nearest`, and the `scroll-padding-inline` of the list places the tab clear of the fade; Chrome alone does not scroll a tab that is only partly hidden). It also brings the selected tab into view when the bar mounts and every time `selected` changes (`use-reveal-selected-tab.ts`, the same `scrollIntoView`), so a tab the caller chose from outside, past the edge of a bar that scrolls, is not left out of sight; a render that changes nothing scrolls nothing, and it is behavior, not a variant. It never wraps to a second row (T4-360 = A). The scroll never gets in the way of a click: Radix chooses a tab on the press of the button, so the scroll that `bringIntoView` or the reveal starts after the press cannot take the tab from under the pointer before the choice, and a wheel or a trackpad over the row scrolls it and chooses nothing; do not move the choice to the `click`, and never scroll the row on hover or before the press (`tabs-variants.browser.test.tsx` fails if the row moves before a press lands on a tab half hidden by the fade). `variant` picks the look: `pills` (the default, the tabs of a piece) or `underline` (the tabs inside a tab: plain text on one row under a thin rule, the chosen tab bold in ink with a `--stroke-heavy` underline); the underlined tab holds the width of its bold text with an `aria-hidden` span that draws the label from `data-text`, so a tab does not shift when it is chosen and a pseudo-element never leaks into its accessible name. `TabsVariant` is the type of `variant`. Radix draws the body of a tab one render after the selection reaches it, which would leave a caller that scrolls the panel into view right after a choice (`useSelection`) measuring the old body; so each panel is keyed by `id` and by whether it is the selected one, and the body of the tab just chosen is drawn in the same render as the choice.
- `Dialog` is a window: Radix Dialog drawn as a `Card` whose stripe holds `name` (the title that names the `dialog`, an `h2`) and the X (`closeText`, a `small` `Button` named `closeLabel`), with `children` as the body, which scrolls when the window is shorter than its content. The caller owns `open` and hears `onOpenChange(false)` from Escape and the X; the words arrive as props because this folder imports no text. `modal` goes to Radix: with it, an overlay (`dialog__overlay`), the focus trapped and the rest of the page inert; without it none of that, and a press or the focus outside never asks to close (`onInteractOutside` is held): the window stays while the player uses the rest of the page, which a non-modal window exists for, and only Escape, the X and the caller's own button close it. Radix defers the dismissal by a press outside to the `click` that follows, after the handlers of the button that was pressed, so a button that toggles the window closes it in one click even without the hold (measured in Chromium with 1.3.1); the hold is what keeps a press on any other control from closing it. The focus enters the window when it opens; where it goes when the window closes is the caller's (`onCloseAutoFocus`), since there is no Radix trigger. It renders where the caller places it, not in a portal in the body, so the caller gives it its size and place (a `Card` has no height of its own); in the catalog it is a plain block. `Dialog` takes no `className` and no `style`.
- Not here: an element the player acts on or picks with (`button.tsx`, `choice.tsx`) or a name (`name-tag.tsx`), which stay in `src/design/primitives/`; the tokens (`src/design/`), screens (`src/ui/`), map pieces (`src/scene/`) and visible text (`src/copy/`).
- May import: no layer; `react`, for `Tabs` `@radix-ui/react-tabs` and, for `Dialog`, `@radix-ui/react-dialog`. A sibling primitive of `src/design/primitives/` is imported with the `@/design/primitives/<file>` path (`Table` uses `@/design/primitives/button`), never with `../`. Other layers import this folder through the barrel of `src/design/` (`@/design`).
