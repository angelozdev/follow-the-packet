# engine/network

The network model as zod schemas and types: an office or a VPC (`kind`), its range, its devices (laptop, printer, EC2 instance), its firewalls (host firewall or Security Group), its edge (router or Internet gateway) and the actors on the internet. An office can also have floors (LANs) and routers. Also actions that change a network. CIDR math (the private ranges of RFC 1918, the loopback range of RFC 1122), the topology questions and the questions a router asks of the model live in `addressing/`. EC2 DNS names and app targets live in `naming/`. What listens on a device or a server and on which address lives in `services.ts`.

- `networkSchema` checks each kind: a VPC has a region, subnets, a Security Group on every device and an Internet gateway as its edge, and declares no LANs and no routers; an office has no region, no subnets and no public IPs, and its edge is a router. Both kinds use a private range (RFC 1918).
- An office has one LAN, the one of its `cidr`, unless it lists `lans` (floors, each with its range and the `latencyMs` of a hop). A device says its LAN with `lanId`, which it can leave out only while the office has one LAN, and sits inside that LAN's range. `lansOf` and `lanOf` (`addressing/topology.ts`) answer both questions, and `isConnected` says whether a device has its cable. A LAN's range is a detail of the model and the game never shows it.
- A device can also carry a default `gateway` (an IPv4 address) and `connected: false`, which is a device with no cable: it neither sends nor receives.
- `routers` lists the routers of the office and of the road to the internet. A router has `interfaces` (`ip` and `faces`, the id of a LAN, another router or a server), `routes` (`to` is a range or `default`, `next` the id of a router), the `latencyMs` of a hop and `sendsUnreachable`, which says whether it warns when it has no route or stays silent. Routes are given by the world; the player never edits them in chapter 1.
- No IP repeats among devices, router interfaces and servers. Every interface faces a LAN, a router or a server that exists, every route goes through a router that exists, and a LAN, a router and any other id never share a name.
- The questions a router asks about the model (`interfaceTowards`, `ownsIp`, `facesALan`, `bestRoute` and `onwardFrom`) are answered in `addressing/routing.ts`.
- An actor of kind `server` is a server of the internet: it answers Echo Request, accepts a TCP connection on the ports of its `services` (none unless it lists them), has no firewall and carries its `latencyMs`. It is not an actor of the map (`ClientActor` is every other actor, and `isClientActor` tells them apart).
- A `Service` is `{ protocol: 'tcp' | 'udp', port, listen: 'all' | 'loopback' }`: a program that listens. `port` goes from 1 to 65535 (IANA marks 0 as reserved). A device lists its services in `host.services` (always present; `[]` for a device with no program) and a server of the internet in its optional `services`. `host.services` replaced `host.listeningTcpPorts`, which was TCP only and had no address. `lookupService(services, protocol, port, destinationIp)` (`services.ts`) answers `accepts` (a service of that protocol and port listens on the destination IP), `wrong-address` (one listens on that protocol and port, but not on that IP) or `closed` (none does). `listensOn(service, ip)` is the rule: `all` takes a packet whatever its destination IP, because the packet reached the device by one of its own IPs (a `127.x.x.x` included), and `loopback` listens on `127.0.0.1` only (`LOOPBACK_IP`), so it takes a packet to that exact address and refuses one to any other IP of the device, another `127.x.x.x` address included (decided 2026-10-11). A packet to a `127.x.x.x` address reaches a device only when that device sends it to itself, because the route stage delivers any address of the loopback range (RFC 1122 §3.2.1.3) to the sender.
- **Sin verificar (2026-10-11).** That the listening address decides by the destination IP of the packet (decided item 8 of `docs/plans/2026-10-11-follow-the-packet-chapter-2.md`) is reasoned from `ip(7)` ("bound to all local interfaces" for `INADDR_ANY`), not tested on a real machine. So a service on the loopback address answers a packet to the IP of the network of its own device with a reset, also when the device sends it itself. The same holds for a packet to another `127.x.x.x` address, which a service bound to `127.0.0.1` refuses too. The student can test both on an EC2 (a service bound to `127.0.0.1` and an `nc` from another machine, from the same machine to its network IP and from the same machine to `127.0.0.2`); if the real behavior differs, change `listensOn`, this note and the decided item, and write the result here. Until then no text of the game states it as a fact beyond what the trace shows.
- A `PacketSpec` can carry a `ttl`, an integer from 1 to 255; `DEFAULT_TTL` (64) is the one it leaves with otherwise. It can also carry a `sourcePort`, an integer from 1 to 65535 (IANA marks port 0 as reserved; the `port` a packet is for still allows 0, as before); `DEFAULT_SOURCE_PORT` (51514) is the one a TCP or UDP packet leaves with otherwise. ICMP has no ports.
- An action changes a network: it adds or removes a rule (`addRule` and `removeRule` take a `direction`, `inbound` or `outbound`; without it the action works on the inbound list, and `removeRule` only looks in the list of its direction; `DEFAULT_DIRECTION` is the direction of an action that gives none), sets an app target, the host's echo setting or a gateway, plugs a device in, adds an actor on the internet, a firewall (which it puts on its device), a LAN, a device or a router, or replaces the services of a device or of a server of the internet (`setServices`, with its `ownerId`) or changes the listening address of one of them (`setListenAddress`, with `ownerId`, `protocol`, `port` and the new `listen`: it changes the one service with that protocol and port and keeps the rest), or replaces the routes, the interfaces or the unreachable behavior of a router (a router that must face one added after it, such as the uplink of the office router to the first router of the provider, gets its interfaces replaced then). An action that names something the network does not have returns the same network: an owner that is no device and no server of the internet (a client actor included), or a `setListenAddress` for a protocol and port the owner does not serve.

- Not here: CIDR math, the topology questions and the routing questions (`addressing/`), anything that moves a packet (`src/engine/simulation/`).
- May import: `zod` and other files of `src/engine/`.
