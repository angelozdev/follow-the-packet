# engine/simulation/stages

One file per pipeline stage, in this order: `address`, `dns`, `route`, `firewall-out`, `forward`, `firewall-in`, `host`, `reply`. A stage reads and extends the simulation context and says whether the packet continues.

- `route` decides, before any firewall, where a packet from an office device goes (RFC 1122, §3.3.1.1): to the device itself (loopback, its own IP), straight into its own LAN, or to its default gateway when the destination is in another LAN of the office. A device with no cable, or with no gateway for a destination outside its LAN, ends as `no-route` with no warning. In a VPC, and for an actor, `route` also settles the destination, as before.
- `forward` delivers what `route` handed over (`ctx.delivery`), after the sender's outbound firewall. It finds a connected owner of the IP in the sender's LAN. Nobody owns it: the sender's own device warns (`unreachable`, a `host-unreachable` notice from the sender), unless an outbound firewall silenced the packet first. The router of the sender's LAN, the gateway or the owner of the IP, takes the packet over in `paths/routers.ts` (TTL, hops, warnings, the road to the server). The gateway is any other device: it drops the packet in silence (`timeout`). A packet `route` already settled passes through, and `forward` writes a step only when it stops the packet or when a router passes it on (`forward-hop`). It also records the sender's LAN as the first leg of the trip (`addLeg`), for the round trip time, once someone in that LAN takes the packet.
- `route` also hands a destination outside the office to the default gateway when the office has routers (`route-leaves-office`); with no routers it keeps the old behavior.
- `host` and `reply` serve a device and a responder (a router answering for its own interface, or a server of the internet) the same way, through `receiverOf`. `host` finds a TCP service by port and by destination IP (`lookupService`): a service, a closed port and a service on another address are three different outcomes (accepted, `host-refused` and `host-wrong-address`; the last two are an RST). `firewall-in` lets a packet pass when it reached no device.
- `host` and `reply` also say what the ends of a TCP or UDP exchange said (`sayRefusal` and `sayAnswer`, in `context.ts`), in `ctx.exchange`, which `simulate` seeds with the opening (SYN or query). That is data for `Trace.exchange`, not a step: no stage writes a step for it.

- A new network mechanic (for example NACL) adds a stage here and registers it in the pipeline of `src/engine/simulation/simulate.ts`.
- May import: other files of `src/engine/`.
