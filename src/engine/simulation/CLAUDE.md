# engine/simulation

`simulate(network, packet)` and its trace: the stage pipeline, rule matching and where a packet stops.

- Each step carries a reason code, never a sentence; `src/copy/` turns codes into Spanish.
- Outcomes: `replied`, `timeout`, `refused`, `arrived-no-reply`, `unresolved`, `invalid-address`, `no-route`, `unreachable`, `ttl-exceeded` and `lost`. In a VPC a packet is lost when no instance has its IP; anywhere it is lost when it goes from the internet to a private IP (RFC 1918). A device that sends a packet to its own IP, or to any `127.x.x.x` loopback address (RFC 1122, §3.2.1.3), answers itself, past no firewall.
- The `address` stage runs first. Four numbers that are not an IPv4 address (one above 255) end as `invalid-address` before any lookup; `192.168.1`, with the wrong number of groups, is a name and goes on to `dns`. The name `localhost` becomes `127.0.0.1` with no lookup (RFC 6761, §6.3). A valid address or a name writes no step there, so a trace that does not use them is the same as before.
- In an office, a device that cannot send ends as `no-route` with no warning: it has no cable (`route-not-connected`) or no default gateway for an IP outside its LAN (`route-no-gateway`). An IP that nobody connected has in the sender's LAN, or a gateway that nobody has, ends as `unreachable`: the sender's own device warns, and `Trace.notice` says `host-unreachable` from the sender (`forward-no-owner`). A gateway that is not a router drops the packet in silence, so it ends as `timeout` (`route-gateway-not-a-router`). The sender's outbound firewall comes first: it silences a packet to an IP nobody has, and then no warning comes back.
- Routers (`routers.ts`): an office with `routers` carries a packet outside its LAN through them. Every router the packet reaches first asks whether the destination is one of its own interfaces: if so it answers (`forward-router-replies`), whatever the TTL, and the packet goes on to `host` and `reply`. Otherwise it takes 1 from the TTL (`packet.ttl`, `DEFAULT_TTL` = 64 by default; RFC 1812, §5.3.1), adds a `Trace.hops` entry and, at 0, discards the packet and warns with `time-exceeded` (`forward-ttl-exceeded`, outcome `ttl-exceeded`). Then `onwardFrom` (`src/engine/network/routing.ts`) picks the next stop: a device of a LAN it faces (`forward-hop`, then the firewall of the device), a server behind it (it answers like a device with no firewall and no programs), another router, or a failure. A failure is `forward-private-dropped` (a private IP at a router outside the office: silent, `timeout`), `forward-no-route` (`net-unreachable`) or `forward-no-host` (`host-unreachable`, the router has the network connected and nobody has the IP). The last two warn only if the router has `sendsUnreachable`; otherwise they are silent, and the outcome is `timeout`. The walk is bounded by the TTL, so a loop between two routers ends when it runs out.
- `Trace.hops` lists every router that took the packet, in order, with the TTL left after it. The router that discards at TTL 0 is in the list (`ttlLeft` 0); the router that answers for itself is not (it took no TTL). A hop's `ip`, and the source of every warning, is the router's interface towards the sender (RFC 1812, §4.3.2.4): the one on the sender's LAN, or else the one the way back to the sender leaves through. So in a loop the same router always warns from the same IP.
- A router or a server that answers is `ctx.responder` and the trace's `destinationId` is its id; `receiverOf` gives `host` and `reply` the same shape for a device and for a responder. Only a device writes tcpdump lines. A responder has no firewall and no program, so a TCP or UDP packet to it ends as `refused` or `arrived-no-reply`. The reply of a ping never goes through a firewall.
- Leaving the office writes `route-leaves-office` and no step of NAT: the trip out of the office is a black box, the source stays the private IP.
- `Trace.header` is the packet's header in an office: `request` (`source` = the sender's private IP, always, even when the packet leaves the office, because the way out is a black box; `destination` = the resolved IP, so `localhost` shows `127.0.0.1`; `ttl` = `packet.ttl` or `DEFAULT_TTL`) and, only when the packet was answered (`replied`), `reply`: the same two addresses swapped (RFC 792). A packet that stopped at its sender (`invalid-address`, an unresolved name, `no-route`) has no header.
- `Trace.rttMs` is the round trip time of an office, and exists only when something came back to the sender: `replied`, `refused`, `unreachable` from a router and `ttl-exceeded`; silence has none. It is twice the sum of the legs the packet travelled, recorded by `addLeg` where each is crossed: the sender's LAN (`forward`, once someone in it takes the packet), every router the packet reaches (the one that answers or warns included, since the warning travels from it), the destination's LAN when a router hands the packet to a device, and the server. Nothing past whoever answered or warned counts. A trip that never left the device (`127.x.x.x`, `localhost`, its own IP) has no legs and takes `HOST_ONLY_RTT_MS` (0.05 ms), smaller than any trip that crosses a wire. A `host-unreachable` the sender generates itself has no `rttMs`: on Linux it comes only after the laptop waited for someone to answer for that IP, and ping prints no `time=` on that line. All the latencies are illustrative, not measured.
- `Trace.notice`, `Trace.hops`, `Trace.header` and `Trace.rttMs` are optional: they are absent, not `null` or empty, in every trace without a warning, a router, a sent packet or an answer, and so are the traces of a VPC (the golden test serializes the whole trace). A stage that has nothing to say writes no step.
- A step's `at` names where it happened: a device id, the network (`office` or `vpc`), its edge (`router` or `internet-gateway`) or `internet`. A step of `forward` stops at the sender or at the device that dropped the packet.
- `firewall-check.ts` filters a packet through a device's firewalls for both firewall stages and names their kind in the step. A device without firewalls is not filtered and gets no step.
- Not here: the stages themselves (`stages/`), the network model (`src/engine/network/`).
- May import: other files of `src/engine/`.
