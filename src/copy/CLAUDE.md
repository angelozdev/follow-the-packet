# copy

Every Spanish text the player sees, as named constants and formatters, one file per area: the term registry (`terms.ts`), tagged text (`term-text.ts`), engine reason codes (`reasons.ts`), packet labels, the texts of the screens in `screens/` (the roadmap, notice and entity catalog in `ui.ts`, the incident screen in `incident-ui.ts`) and the lines that the commands `nc` and `dig` print (`tools/`, which has its own guide with the source of each line).

- An AWS service, resource or setting is named as the English console shows it (`Security Group`, `Internet gateway`, `Anywhere-IPv4`); the other terms keep a Spanish name (`instancia EC2`, `regla de entrada`) or are general concepts (`ping`, `CIDR`, `mínimo privilegio`). Every term has a rigorous definition of at most 120 characters; only the IP address (`IP`) and the Security Group (`SG`) have a short form.
- A term enters the game when an incident lists it in `introduces`, and until then it stays inert: the fourteen incidents of chapter 1 introduce every term of the chapter, so `04-01` bridges none (`CHAPTER_1_BRIDGE` is empty), and `src/levels/ladder.test.ts` checks each definition against the order of the incidents. A definition names no term that comes later in the course, so write it for the player who has only the terms before it. The term of an ICMP message keeps the name the RFC gives it (`Time Exceeded`, `Destination Host Unreachable`); an acronym is the name and its definition expands it (`RTT`, `TTL`).
- The terms of chapter 2 (50 new ids, `service` to `hosts-file`, and the rewritten `cidr`, `dns` and `nat`) are registered and inert until their incident lists them in `introduces`. None has a short form, and only `tcp` and `udp` name an entity (the four shapes of a TCP and a UDP packet, see below); `src/levels/ladder.test.ts` checks their definitions against the approved order of the 13 subchapters. `default-port` and `well-known-ports` are distinct terms of subchapter 05: the default port is a custom (22, 80, 443, 3306, 5432), the well-known ports are IANA's 0–1023 range.
- A packet's label also says its protocol and ports when the trace has them (an office; a VPC trace has none and keeps `TCP 22`): «TCP 51514 → 80», each part once its term is known. The protocol is `{{tcp}}` or `{{udp}}` once that term is known and `{{packet}}` before, with «al puerto 80» when `port` is known; the source port comes with `source-port`. The label once the NAT has changed the source («TCP 40001 → 80», from `Trace.translation`) is the second leg of `packetPortLabels`, below; the helper that wrote it alone (`packetLabelOutside`) had no caller in production and took the source port from `translation.outside`, which is wrong for a packet that comes in from outside, so it was removed with its tests. `serviceLabel` keeps the plain `TCP 22` for a packet whose trace has no ports. The map draws a short label beside a packet with ports, just its protocol and ports, leg by leg: `packetPortLabels(packet, trace, known)` gives one `PortLabels` (`request` and `reply`, the reply with the ports swapped) for the leg where the packet left and a second one for the leg after the router that translated it, only when the NAT changed a port and the label says so (the source port for a packet of an office device, the destination port for one that comes in from outside), and null for a packet with no ports. `PacketPorts` (the legs and the router where they change) is what `src/game/` hands to the scene. Until `source-port` is known the reply keeps the port of the service, as the request does, so no label shows a port whose term the player does not know.
- `warningOf(notice, packet, known?)` takes `known` as an exception to the rule that no `known` means every term is known: without it, and until `net-unreachable` (02-07) is known, a router with no route is named by `host-unreachable`, as it always was (the game had one term for both); with the term, it is Destination Net Unreachable. `by` is `sender` (the sender's own device), `host` (the equipment the datagram was addressed to, which returns Port Unreachable) or `router`.
- `describeResult(trace, known, destination?)` is `describeOutcome` for the results the outcome alone cannot tell apart: a refusal says whether nobody listens on the port («ningún programa lo estaba esperando», `OUTCOME_TEXT.refused`) or the service listens on another address (a hidden cause of `listen-address`), and a Port Unreachable says that the datagram arrived at the equipment, that no program received it and that the equipment returned the warning (a hidden cause of `port-unreachable`). While the term is unknown both say «Llegó, pero lo rechazaron.». Neither names an operating system or a reason. Every other result is `describeOutcome`'s.
- A packet's label names the device the trace reached, except when the packet never left its sender (to `127.0.0.1`, `localhost` or its own IP): then it names the address as it was written, so the three have labels of their own (`ping de [[tu-laptop]] a localhost`).
- A packet is named by the term of its message only once the player knows it (`serviceLabel` and `packetLabel` take `known`): an Echo Request is «ping» when `ping` is known and «paquete» before, and an Echo Reply is «Echo Reply» when that is known and «paquete» before. With no `known`, every term is known.
- Every entity of the map, in every variant (`EntityRef`: its kind and the variant its model gives it), names its term in `ENTITY_TERMS` (`terms.ts`), or `null` while the ladder has none; `entityTerm` reads it. The office variants have the terms of chapter 1 (`network`, `device`, `firewall` for a host firewall, `router` and `internet`); an actor has none. The other way round, a term that names an entity declares the entity whose icon goes with it in `TERM_ENTITY`: a term that names several variants shows one of them, and `packet` shows an Echo Request; a term with no entity, like `least-privilege`, has no icon. `tcp` and `udp` are the only terms of chapter 2 that name an entity: each names the two packet shapes of its protocol (`tcp-request` and `tcp-reply`, `udp-request` and `udp-reply`; a filled shape asks and a ring answers) and shows the one that asks as its icon. `port-unreachable` and `net-unreachable` are not in `TERM_ENTITY`: `warningOf` names the warning by them, but the warning is one look for every kind (`warning`, named by no term), and `terms.test.ts` lists only `host-unreachable`, `time-exceeded` and `packet` as the terms that show it. `packetShape(packet)` is the shape a packet is drawn with (the one of its protocol, and an Echo Reply as a reply), `replyShape(shape)` is the ring of the same protocol, and `packetMessage` stays the ICMP message that names a term. `ENTITY_REFS` lists every entity in every variant for the catalog page (every packet that a sender sends, in any protocol, also as an intruder's, and the outline of a block, the `block` variant of a network, which names no term), and `entityName` names one as that page shows it.
- A text that uses a term tags it: `{{security-group}}` shows the term's name and `{{inbound-rule|reglas de entrada}}` shows other words. An unknown tag throws. Pieces and chips show only the name or the short form (`unofficialNames`).
- A resource's or actor's own name (`servidor2`, `Atacante`) is not a term: it is marked with `nameTag(name)`, which writes `[[servidor2]]`, and the views draw it as a name tag (monospace, in a shaded box) apart from the entity (`Security Group`, `Internet gateway`), which stays plain. Never write `[[` by hand outside `term-text.ts`. A stray `[[` or `]]` throws, like a stray `{{`. Whatever builds a text from the world marks the names it reads from it (`deviceName`, `endpointName`, `MAP_TEXT.firewall`, the titles and pieces of `game/view`); the texts written by hand in the incidents of chapter 1 mark the names of its devices with `nameTag` too (`laptop-marta`, `la impresora`), as the pieces show them, while those of chapter 4 write them plain. A `TermText` that ends in an attribute (`aria-label`, `title`) goes through `toPlainText`.
- A text that names a router asks `placeOf(routerId)` (the fourth argument of `describeReason`, all named by default): a router the map draws is named, and one it does not is "un router de afuera de la oficina" or "de la oficina", never by name or IP. `roundTripText(ms)` writes the engine's time in milliseconds with the decimal comma.
- A text that names a cause the player may not know yet takes `known`: `describeReason(reason, net, known)` and `describeOutcome(outcome, known)`. While the term that names a hidden cause is unknown, the text says only what the player saw (a packet that arrived says «Llegó a» and the name of the device it reached when `describeOutcome` is given it, and only «Llegó.» otherwise) and never guesses a cause: «No volvió ninguna respuesta.» for a silence (a firewall that drops, `icmp_echo_ignore_all`), and «No llegó a su destino.» for a warning that did come back (Time Exceeded, Destination Host Unreachable), since the map draws that warning travelling back. A firewall that drops with `firewall` known and `rule` unknown says that it dropped the packet and does not talk about rules. The drop of a host firewall on the way out, once `outbound` is known, says that its outbound side was set closed («su salida está cerrada, alguien la configuró así») when the device's host firewalls have no outbound rule at all, because that is what the world says, and never a rule about firewalls in general; with some outbound rule that does not match, it says that no rule lets the packet out, as a Security Group does. The sentences that hide a cause tag a term only when it is known. So does every sentence that says a router discards a packet: it writes «descarta» (or «descartó») plain until the term `drop` (01-11) is known (`word(known, term, text)` in `term-text.ts` writes a word as the tagged term or plain), whatever the cause (TTL 0, no route, no host, a private IP). The reason of a private IP that a router dropped (`forward-private-dropped`) tells what happened to this packet («descartó el paquete y no avisó»), not a rule of routers: whether a router warns varies. The result of a silence reads «timeout: pasó el tiempo de espera y no volvió nada.» (it does not say the packet did not arrive: the sender cannot know), with the term tagged only once `timeout` is known. The reply of a ping through a host firewall states the fact («Llega la respuesta.») and gives no reason why it passes. For the same reason the empty inbound list of a host firewall states only the fact («Este firewall no tiene reglas.», «Ninguna.» in its box) and never says that nothing comes in, since the reply of a ping does; a Security Group keeps the wording of AWS's own docs («no deja entrar nada»).
- A router of the office whose table has no route for the destination (`router-office` with no routes, the start of 02-07) says the sentence of `forward-no-route` that any router with no route says: it does not know which router to send the packet to, drops it and, when it warns, tells the sender it could not deliver it. It names neither a route nor the type of the warning; while `host-unreachable` is unknown it is the generic «No llegó a su destino.» like any warning. `reasons.test.ts` pins the sentence for `router-office`.
- A refusal because the service listens on another address (`host-wrong-address`) is a hidden cause: while `listen-address` is unknown it says only that the packet arrived and was rejected («Llega, pero [[x]] lo rechaza.»), and once it is known it names the service, its listening address (127.0.0.1) and the IP the packet went to, tagging `service`, `port` and `packet` only when known. It never says that no program listens, because one does. `host-refused` keeps the plain words it had in chapter 1 («ningún programa escucha en el puerto N»), and tags `service` («programa»), `listen` («escucha») and `port` («puerto») once each is known, so its plain text is the same and only the marks differ.
- A UDP datagram that a host answers with Port Unreachable (`host-udp-closed`) is a hidden cause too: while `port-unreachable` is unknown it says only that the datagram arrived and was rejected («Llega, pero [[x]] lo rechaza.», the same words as the refusal because of the listening address, which a UDP datagram to a loopback service at another IP also gets), because the player saw it arrive and a warning they cannot read yet come back, and it says neither UDP, port nor program. Once it is known it reads «Llega por UDP al puerto N, pero ningún programa escucha ahí: [[x]] avisa con Port Unreachable.», tagging `udp` and `port` only when known. It gives no reason for a reply to come back and quotes no operating system. The warning's term is `port-unreachable` (`warningOf`), and a warning that a host the packet was for returns is `by: 'host'`, not a router's. `describeOutcome` does not see the kind of notice, so the box asks `describeResult(trace, known, destination)` instead (see below).
- A packet from outside that the NAT of a router does not deliver (`nat-no-translation`) is a hidden cause: while `translation-table` is unknown it says «No volvió ninguna respuesta.», and once it is known it says that the router has no row in its table for that port (and protocol) of its public IP, does not know which device to give the packet to and «no lo entrega a nadie». It never says that NAT blocks, filters or protects, never says a NAT would not answer (a real one may, after at least 6 seconds: `src/engine/nat/CLAUDE.md`) and gives no reason for any reply to come back. A packet that a forward or a row delivers (`nat-forwarded`) is hidden too: while `port-forwarding` (a forward) or `translation-table` (a row) is unknown it says «Llega a [[x]].», and once known it names the forward or the row, the public port and the device with the port it listens on. `tcp`, `udp`, `port`, `device` and `packet` are tagged only when known. `reasons.test.ts` pins the sentences and checks that no text of an unsolicited packet says «bloquea» or «filtra».
- What a resolver answered (`dns-answered`, `dns-cached`, `dns-nxdomain`, `dns-servfail`, `dns-no-address`) and what happened when it did not (`dns-resolver-silent`, `dns-no-resolver`) is written with the terms the player knows: the question is a «consulta DNS» and the resolver is named with its term only once known (`[[tu-laptop]] hizo una {{dns-query|consulta DNS}} por www.lucia.example a [[servidor-dns]], su {{resolver}}: la respuesta es …`), and before, «preguntó por … a …». NXDOMAIN and SERVFAIL are hidden causes: while `nxdomain` or `servfail` is unknown the text says only «<nombre> no se convirtió en una IP.». A name that exists and has no address says «no tiene un {{a-record}}» or, before, «no tiene ninguna IP asociada». An answer from the cache of the resolver (`dns-cached`) is a hidden cause too: while `dns-cache` is unknown it reads exactly as `dns-answered`, because the player saw an IP come back and nothing more; once known it adds «Salió de la caché de [[servidor-dns]].», and the seconds («A la respuesta le quedan 240 segundos de TTL.») only once `dns-ttl` is known too. A name that the hosts file answered (`dns-hosts-file`) says «encontró … en su archivo hosts … No preguntó a nadie.» once `hosts-file` is known and, before, «ya sabía que … es …: no preguntó a nadie.», which names no hosts file and no DNS. A question that never reached a host (`dns-resolver-silent`) says the device «no recibió respuesta a su pregunta» and calls the resolver «servidor de nombres» until `resolver` is known; it covers a firewall that dropped it and a router or the sender that warned, and says nothing of which. A question that a host got and did not answer as a resolver (`dns-no-resolver`) names that host, a router only as the map draws it (`placeOf`): «[[impresora]] no le contestó como un resolver» (or «un servidor de nombres»), whether it rejected the datagram with Port Unreachable or answered anyway; it never says Port Unreachable, which a `dig` shows by its own warning. None gives a reason for an answer to come back or names an operating system. `dns-unresolved` (a device with no resolver, or a VPC) keeps its wording.
- A rule that no chip of the incident names is written from the rule itself by `describeRule(rule, direction, net, known)` (`rule-text.ts`): the rule that allows everything is a sentence («deja salir todo el tráfico», with «tráfico» tagged only once `traffic` is known, and «deja salir todo» before), any other reads «Type ← Source» or «Type → Destination», the peer being a device when the rule names its only address. Each term is tagged only if the player knows it.
