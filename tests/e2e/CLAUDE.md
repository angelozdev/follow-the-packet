# tests/e2e

Playwright tests against the production build (`pnpm e2e`, on its own port from 4183 to 4282: see «Port and parallel suites»). One file per flow, named `<flow>.spec.ts`; the whole-chapter test and the helper that plays each incident of a chapter live in that chapter's folder (`chapter-1/`, `chapter-2/`).

- Interact through roles and labels, the way a player would.
- Shared helpers are plain modules next to the specs: `console-errors.ts`, `saved-progress.ts` (open a page with progress already saved: `CHAPTER_1_DONE`, the fourteen incidents of chapter 1 with three stars, and `CHAPTER_2_DONE`, chapter 1 and then `02-01` to `02-14` with three stars, which is what opens 04-01 once chapter 2 is registered), `still-scene.ts` (wait until the scene stands still before measuring a box; see «Deterministic tests»), `cards.ts` (open the card of a piece, press «Listo», open a terminal and send from it), `closing.ts` (send the solution, or name the cause of a case, answer Lucía and see the stars), `map-boxes.ts` (the boxes of the map's labels, stamps and floor names, `packetBoxes` (piece labels, stamps, floor names and the labels packets with ports wear) and `pairsThatOverlap`, the pairs that overlap, what a card covers, what the map draws outside its area, and where the labels stood after each change of the page, from `recordLabels` on), and `card-boxes.ts` (the defects in the boxes of the cards on a page: content cut off or spilled, cards that overlap, a stamp or a button that touches what is next to it, a card of the map that sticks out of it above or below, or a card beside its piece that rises under the title bar; and reaching an element with the mouse wheel, over the side panel or over another scroller such as the body of the inspection box, with turns short enough that the mouse stays over a scroller the page carries away). A spec that plays a later chapter starts with the chapters before it done.
- A spec in a subfolder imports a shared helper through the alias `@e2e/`, which points at `tests/e2e/` and is configured in `tsconfig.json` (`@e2e/cards`), never with `../`; a spec at this level may still use `./`.
- `chapter-2/` holds `chapter-2-play.ts`, how each of the fourteen incidents of chapter 2 is played with the mouse (`CHAPTER_2`, which holds the incidents built so far and `LAST_BUILT_OF_CHAPTER_2`), and `chapter-2.spec.ts`, the smoke tests of each block, and will hold the whole-chapter test of chapter 2 (task 34 of the plan); it has its own guide.
- `chapter-1/` holds the whole-chapter test of chapter 1 and `chapter-1-play.ts`, how each of its fourteen incidents is played with the mouse, which the whole-chapter test and `card-layout.spec.ts` share (imported there as `@e2e/chapter-1/chapter-1-play`); the folder has its own guide.
- A geometry test runs at 1280×800 and at 1440×900, each in its own `describe` named after the window, so the test keeps its name; the map is never scaled to fit. What a card covers includes the pieces under the labels, not only the labels, and the drawing counts as outside the map when it touches the edge of the canvas, which cuts it off.

## Deterministic tests

A layout or timing test gives the same result in every run: if the defect exists it fails every time, and if it does not it passes every time. Never `retries`, never a fixed wait, never `skip` or `fixme` to hide a failure.

- **No fixed waits.** ESLint rejects `waitForTimeout`, `setTimeout` and `setInterval` in `tests/**` (`eslint.config.js`, proven by `scripts/eslint-config.test.ts`). Wait for a state (an expectation, `expect.poll`, `standStill`) or move `page.clock`.
- **The only timer of the game is the grace of the inspection box** (`GRACE_MS` = 400 ms in `src/ui/incident/use-inspection.ts`). It starts when the pointer or the focus leaves a piece or a stamp, and the box goes when it ends. Clicking a piece that opens a card moves the focus into the card, so the box that the hover brought up lingers 400 ms after the click and then goes.
- **`page.clock` controls that timer.** Call `await page.clock.install()` before the page loads; time then flows by itself. `page.clock.fastForward(ms)` jumps and fires each due timer once (the cheap way to pass the grace); `page.clock.runFor(ms)` fires everything on the way, frame by frame. `freezeTime` (in `still-scene.ts`) pauses the page, so no timer fires and no frame is drawn until `stepFrame` or `fastForward` moves the time. **Keep a heavy scene paused**: with the clock flowing, the frames of the page come from a timer and not from the browser, and a scene drawn by the CPU starved the page of real frames; two suites at once (two worktrees) left 3 of 20 runs of the beside-a-piece test hanging, and with the clock paused during the scene 0 of 52 did (2026-10-11; the cause is a guess, the counts are not). A paused page also needs a `page.clock.resume()` before the next page loads, and only the tests that need the clock install it.
- **`standStill(page)` measures only a scene that stands still**: the boxes of the cards, labels, stamps and inspection box (and the attributes that decide who takes the pointer) are the same in five frames in a row. It counts frames, not milliseconds, and it throws when the scene has not settled after 1200 frames. With `eachFrame: passTheGrace` (a `fastForward` past the grace) the scene it waits for is the one at rest, with the lingering box gone; with `eachFrame: stepFrame` it works on a paused page. It never moves the mouse.
- **What a test measures, at rest.** `card-layout.spec.ts` measures the cards that depend on the box (`cardProblems`, which looks at the help of the inspection box only when the box is gone, and the foot of the card beside a piece) with `playUntilItRests`: load the scene, wait for its pieces, `standStill`, `freezeTime`, reach the scene, and `standStill` with `eachFrame` jumping past the grace, so every timer armed along the way has fired. The other tests there do not depend on the box and keep `play`.
- **Known gap, measured on 2026-10-11 (not a regression of any task).** In the first 400 ms after a click, at 1280×360, the inspection box of the firewall (`tu-laptop`, 01-13) is up, scrolls (`data-scrolls`) and takes the pointer over «Listo»: `elementFromPoint` on «Listo» returns the box. It is the same on `1edede0` and on the paused task 19 (the branch `wip/task-19`): the box appears about 12 ms after the click and starts to scroll about 14 ms later, and the old test passed on `1edede0` only because it measured 10 to 30 ms after the click, before that. A test that measures right after the click fails every time on both trees, with `1280×360, the firewall with its tabs: its foot is out of reach` (10 runs out of 10 on each, 2026-10-11). It is not asserted because fixing it is a product decision (close the inspection when a card opens from its piece, or never let the box take the pointer over an open card). To measure right after a click: `page.clock.install()` before loading; for each scene `page.clock.resume()` (a page left paused by the previous scene never draws its pieces), load it, wait for `.map-button`, `standStill(page)`, `freezeTime(page)`, reach the scene, `standStill(page, { eachFrame: stepFrame })`, and measure. Freezing before the page has settled leaves it half drawn.
- **Not used: `reducedMotion`.** By reading `src/ui/use-still-mode.ts` and where `still` goes, it stills the traffic, the shake and the pulse; the card placement (`useCardSide`, one `useFrame`) and the grace of the inspection box do not read it, so it does not make this scene quiet. The specs that need the game without animations keep opening the page with `contextOptions: { reducedMotion: 'reduce' }`.

## Port and parallel suites

- `playwright.config.ts` takes the port from `e2ePortOf` (`scripts/e2e-port.ts`): a hash of the real path of the folder that holds the config, in 4183–4282, never 5173 or 5180, the same for the same path. The server starts with `--strictPort`: two checkouts whose paths land on the same port (one chance in a hundred) make the second suite stop with an error instead of sharing a server. Two worktrees run their suites at once; **never two suites at once in the same folder**.
- `reuseExistingServer` stays `false`, and `pnpm build` runs before every suite: a suite never runs on an old build. The build takes about half a second (measured 2026-10-11), so there is nothing to save there.
- `fullyParallel` is `true` and `workers` is not set, so Playwright uses half of the logical cores. Measured on 2026-10-11 on a 16-core machine, whole suite of 77 tests: 106 s with `fullyParallel: false` (5 workers, one per spec file); 65 s with `fullyParallel` and the 8 workers of the default; 69 s with 12 workers; 72 s with 16, where one screenshot of `entities.spec.ts` timed out waiting for the element to be stable (the canvas is drawn by the CPU, so too many workers starve each other). `pnpm e2e --repeat-each=3` passed twice (231 of 231 each) and `card-layout.spec.ts --repeat-each=10` passed (80 of 80) with `fullyParallel`.

## Running only some specs

- One spec: `pnpm e2e tests/e2e/card-layout.spec.ts`. One test: add `-g "<part of its title>"`. Repeat it to prove it does not flake: `--repeat-each=10` (Playwright runs the repetitions in parallel, so it also loads the machine). List without running: `--list`.
- **`--only-changed` is not used.** It follows the files that the tests import, and these tests open the game in the browser and import almost none of its code. Verified on 2026-10-11 on `1edede0`: a change in `src/ui/incident/use-inspection.ts` selects 0 tests and the command ends with exit code 0, a false green; a change in `src/storage/progress.ts` (which `saved-progress.ts` imports) selects 46 tests in 2 files.

## When to run what

- **Each task:** `pnpm check:fast` (types, lint, structure, jscpd and Vitest, about 10 s, no browser) and the specs that the task names or that cover what it touched.
- **Closing a block and before every merge:** `pnpm check`, which is `pnpm check:fast` and then the whole suite.
- **A failing e2e is compared with the base: the last commit whose full `pnpm check` passed.** If it fails there too, it is not of the task or of the block: write it in the backlog (`docs/plans/2026-10-08-follow-the-packet-followups.md`), say so, and do not chase it. If it passes on that base, the regression belongs to the block: some task closed with only `pnpm check:fast` and let it through, and it shows up in the next full `pnpm check`. Fix it inside the block. Do not compare against «HEAD without the changes of the task»: the regression is already in that HEAD, so it also fails there and would go to the backlog by mistake.
- **Never two suites at once in the same folder.**

## Playwright sources (version 1.64.0, consulted on 2026-10-11)

- `page.clock`: https://playwright.dev/docs/api/class-clock and https://playwright.dev/docs/clock. Installed `types.d.ts` of 1.64.0 says «Advance the clock by jumping forward in time. Only fires due timers at most once.» (`fastForward`), «Advance the clock by jumping forward in time and pause the time.» (`pauseAt`) and, for `runFor`, that it advances the clock firing all the time-related callbacks. `install` fakes `Date`, `setTimeout`, `setInterval`, `requestAnimationFrame`, `requestIdleCallback` and `performance`, and must be called before any other clock call.
- `reducedMotion` (not used): https://playwright.dev/docs/api/class-testoptions, «Emulates 'prefers-reduced-motion' media feature.» The release notes list `testOptions.reducedMotion` as new in 1.63; the version that added the context option was not verified.
- `--only-changed`: https://playwright.dev/docs/test-cli and `playwright/lib/program.js` of 1.64.0: «Only run test files that have been changed between 'HEAD' and 'ref'. Defaults to running all uncommitted changes. Only supports Git.»
- `workers`, `fullyParallel`: https://playwright.dev/docs/api/class-testconfig («Defaults to half of the number of logical CPU cores.») and https://playwright.dev/docs/test-parallel; the options `--workers` («Number of concurrent workers or percentage of logical CPU cores, use 1 to run in a single worker (default: 50%)»), `--fully-parallel` («Run all tests in parallel (default: false)») and `--repeat-each` («Run each test N times (default: 1)») are in `program.js` of 1.64.0.
- The pages were read through a summarizer: the sentences between quotes are the ones checked against the installed package. Anything else on this list was checked by running it.
