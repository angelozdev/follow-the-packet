# tests/e2e

Playwright tests against the production build (`pnpm e2e`, port 4183). One file per flow, named `<flow>.spec.ts`.

- Interact through roles and labels, the way a player would.
- Shared helpers are plain modules next to the specs: `console-errors.ts`, `saved-progress.ts` (open a page with progress already saved), `cards.ts` (open the card of a piece, press «Listo», open a terminal and send from it), `closing.ts` (send the solution, or name the cause of a case, answer Lucía and see the stars), `chapter-1-play.ts` (how each of the fourteen incidents of chapter 1 is played with the mouse, which the whole-chapter test and the single-incident tests share), `map-boxes.ts` (the boxes of the map's labels, stamps and floor names, the pairs that overlap, what a card covers, and what the map draws outside its area), and `card-boxes.ts` (the defects in the boxes of the cards on a page: content cut off or spilled, cards that overlap, a stamp or a button that touches what is next to it, a card of the map that sticks out of it above or below, or a card beside its piece that rises under the title bar; and reaching an element with the mouse wheel, over the side panel or over another scroller such as the body of the inspection box, with turns short enough that the mouse stays over a scroller the page carries away). A spec that plays a later chapter starts with the chapters before it done.
- A geometry test runs at 1280×800 and at 1440×900, each in its own `describe` named after the window, so the test keeps its name; the map is never scaled to fit. What a card covers includes the pieces under the labels, not only the labels, and the drawing counts as outside the map when it touches the edge of the canvas, which cuts it off.
- `card-layout.spec.ts` plays a scene for each kind of card (Lucía, the question before and after answering, the result, a case before and after answering, the terminal, the card of a device, the network settings and a firewall with its tabs), at 390 px and at 1440×900 for the boxes (`cardProblems`) and at 390×700 and 1280×720 for the wheel: the last element of each card must come into view and be what a click lands on, scrolled by the wheel alone, never by `scrollIntoView`. Every scene runs at every window, the firewall card included, and `cardProblems` also reports a card that covers the help of the inspection box (at 900 px and under, that help sits under the map, so no card can reach it).
- The floating boxes (the inspection box and the definition of a term) have their tests in `card-layout.spec.ts`, and the mouse of Playwright jumps where a hand crosses the map in a few hundred milliseconds, so a test that jumps onto a preview never meets the 400 ms the box waits for the pointer: these tests pin the box, and where the hand's path matters they move the mouse in steps with a real wait between them. The inspection box of a ping that ended in a warning (01-05), at 1280×800, 390×844, 1000×700 and 1280×500, is first a preview: the mouse hovers the stamp, goes onto the box within the grace and the box must stay, without «Fijada», for longer than the grace; a click on the stamp then pins it («Fijada»), the mouse goes onto the box, which must stay, the body must draw its scrollbar (read from the computed style of `::-webkit-scrollbar`, because the browser of the test hides scrollbars), and the wheel over its body must bring its last line into view with its band in the window. `a pinned box survives a human-paced trip across another piece and scrolls under the mouse` plays the lost ping to 10.0.0.10 of 01-08, one `describe` per window (1440×900, 1280×800 and 1000×700): it clicks the «?» stamp and moves the mouse in 30 steps of 30 ms to the nearest corner of the box, across the label of another piece (the straight path crosses «impresora» at 1440×900; where it crosses none, the route goes through the label nearest to the path, and the test reads what is topmost at the points of the path to assert that a label was crossed); the box still shows the ping and «Fijada», the wheel brings its last line into view, and Escape closes it. With the keyboard, Enter on the stamp pins the box and puts the focus on its heading, PageDown scrolls its body and nothing else, Shift+Tab goes through the body (Chrome makes a scroller focusable) to «Cerrar», and Escape closes the box, gives the focus back to the stamp and does not reopen the box. With touch (`hasTouch`), a tap pins and a tap outside, on the title, releases. `a click on a piece that opens a card opens it, pins the piece's box, and a click in the card releases the box` clicks the label of tu-laptop in 01-08: the terminal opens and holds the focus, the box shows «Fijada» and the label carries `data-pinned`; a click on a chip of the terminal releases the box and picks the chip; with the keyboard, Enter on the label does the same opening and pinning. A term of Lucía's card (`.term--tip`) shows its definition on hover; the mouse crosses the gap in steps onto it and it stays longer than the grace; one jump of the mouse from the definition back onto its term keeps it (the definition is portaled, so the term gets no new `pointerenter`, and a path in steps would reopen it and hide a bug); leaving the term closes it after the grace; and Escape closes it.
- The cards of the map (the terminal, with and without a traceroute listed, the card of a device, the network settings and a firewall with tabs), at 1280×360, 1000×700, 700×600 and 844×460, must stay inside the map, a card beside its piece under the title bar, with their foot within reach.
- `entities.spec.ts` compares two regions of the `/entidades` page with their captures in `snapshots/`: the map pieces and the interface elements, with the focus ring on one option. Both are taken still, at a fixed window size and pixel ratio, with web fonts blocked so the captures do not depend on the network. They tolerate no different pixel and no colour threshold, except the edges pixelmatch counts as anti-aliasing. The same spec measures what a capture cannot see, in the real browser: the primary button as a stamp (computed style, and its movement when pressed with and without reduced motion) and the letters of the options (a DOM snapshot) with their accessible names (the accessibility tree).
