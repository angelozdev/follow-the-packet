# Follow the packet

The game moved out of the course repo (https://github.com/angelozdev/aws-commit-academy) on 2026-11-03 with its history, and docs written before then name paths from that repo, where `projects/follow-the-packet/` is this repo's root.

A study game about AWS. On screen it is called "Sigue el paquete". It has 33 chapters in five levels, from zero to AWS specialist, as the [chapters doc](docs/research/2026-10-09-follow-the-packet-chapters.md) cuts them: a chapter's number is its place in the game, not a course module, and each chapter names the course modules it plays, if any. Chapter 1, "Tu primer día", is playable: fourteen subchapters in five blocks (where you are, the ping, leaving the office, the guard, the first watch), each with one new idea, at the office with Lucía. The player marks devices and picks IPs with chips, sends packets, pings and traceroutes from a terminal, sets the default gateway of a laptop in the «Ajustes» tab of its panel, writes inbound and outbound firewall rules in the «Tablas» tab, on two tabs of their own, and ends by diagnosing seven cases alone. Its human test is one playthrough of the whole chapter by the student, still to do. Chapter 2, "Puertos y nombres", is playable too: fourteen subchapters in six blocks (who listens, refused or silent with the block and the rule, which way, one address for many devices, names, on call) on ports and services, TCP and UDP, CIDR blocks, routing tables, NAT and DNS, with 53 terms, `nc` and `dig` in the terminal, tables in the panel of the pieces and a game clock, and a last incident of seven cases to diagnose. Its human test, one playthrough of the whole chapter by the student, is still to do, and 03-01 opens once 02-14 has a star. Chapter 3, "La web por dentro", is built block by block; block 1, "Pedir una página" (03-01 to 03-04), is playable: what a URL says, an HTTP request and its response, who answers (a process, a web server, a browser) and one server with many sites, with 15 terms, `curl` and a «Navegador» tab in the panel of the devices, the strip of the URL in the terminal and a ficha of sites. Block 2, "Cómo salió y quién eres" (03-05 and 03-06), is playable too: the status code of a response (200, 404, 400) and who generated it, then who you are and what you can do (401 and 403, `curl -u`, and a cookie that the browser keeps after a sign-in from its tab and `curl` does not), with 11 more terms (26 in all). Its human test is one playthrough of the whole chapter 3 by the student, still to do, and 04-01 opens once the last incident registered in chapter 3 (03-06 today) has a star. Chapter 4 is being rebuilt as live incidents based on [lab 03](https://github.com/angelozdev/aws-commit-academy/blob/main/labs/03-ping-connectivity/README.md); incidents 04-01 and 04-02 are playable, with official AWS names defined on hover and an "Enviar solución" button. The engine models any network: an office with floors, routers, a default gateway and a server of the internet, or a VPC, with devices and firewalls by kind; since chapter 2 it also models services that listen on a port, TCP and UDP, routing tables, NAT with its translation table, DNS (resolver, records and cache) and a game clock; since chapter 3 it also models processes that run or stop and web services that answer an HTTP request, with sites chosen by the Host header, pages, orders, users with roles, sessions and the cookies a browser keeps. The incident screen is five fixed zones, and the page does not scroll: a strip across the top (the title, the «Almanaque» button and, in an incident that has one, the clock of the game), Lucía at the left (she folds to a band and unfolds by herself when she says something new), the map in its own box (the player drags it, zooms it with the wheel, `+` and `-` or the buttons «Acercar» and «Alejar», moves it with the arrow keys or with the fingers, and fits it again with «Ajustar»; the map is also drawn under the card of Lucía, the panel and the bottom strip), the panel of what the player chose at the right, a piece with the tabs it has out of «Estado», «Ajustes», «Tablas», «Terminal», «Navegador» and «Preguntas» (always in that order, with their sections and fichas inside) or the box of a packet, with an X that frees the choice, and a strip across the bottom, of one fixed height. The bottom strip says the newest line of what happened, or that nothing has yet, and the «Cambiaste …» notice when a change to the network emptied the sends, and has a bar of buttons: «Terminal» and «Navegador», shortcuts to those tabs where the incident has them, and «Historial (N)». «Historial (N)» and the «Almanaque» (its tabs «Términos», the glossary of the terms the player knows, and «Lecciones», what each solved incident taught) open a window between the two strips that covers the map, Lucía and the panel and leaves the strips operable; the registry lists the lines newest first, with equal lines in a row that counts them, and «Ver fila en la tabla de rutas» on a send opens the row of the table of routes that decided it. At 900 px and under the zones stack in that order, the bottom strip sticks to the bottom of the window and the page scrolls. Saved progress lives under one versioned key in the browser (`v2`); the old key is removed on load and nothing is migrated. Every entity of the map, in every variant and state, and every element of the interface are on the `#/entidades` page, which the roadmap does not link to. How the game looks (tokens, scales and the one component for each element) is in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md).

## Run

    pnpm install
    pnpm exec playwright install chromium
    pnpm dev

The map is flat SVG, animated with SMIL, built in `src/scene/`. It needs no build flag and no graphics library; only its pan and zoom use a library, `@panzoom/panzoom` (`src/scene/camera/`).

## Check

    pnpm check:fast
    pnpm check

`pnpm check:fast` runs types, lint (layer boundaries, names, comments), the folder-structure check, the copy-paste detector and unit tests, with no browser (about 10 seconds). `pnpm test:browser` runs the browser tests (`src/**/*.browser.test.tsx`) in headless Chromium. `pnpm check` is `pnpm check:fast`, then `pnpm test:browser`, then Playwright. "Done" for a task means `pnpm check:fast` plus `pnpm test:browser` when the task touches geometry or CSS and the end-to-end specs that cover what it touched; closing a block and merging need the whole `pnpm check` (see `tests/e2e/CLAUDE.md`).

## Layout

The code lives in layers under `src/`; every folder has a `CLAUDE.md` guide that says what belongs there and what it may import. The rules are in `.claude/rules/`.
